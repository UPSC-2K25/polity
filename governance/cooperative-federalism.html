Cooperative and Competitive Federalism

Federalism is a system of government in which constitutional powers are distributed between different levels of government. In India, cooperative and competitive federalism are complementary approaches intended to improve Centre-State relations, public service delivery and balanced development.

1. Meaning of Federalism

Federalism combines shared rule at the national level with self-rule by constituent units. Each level of government exercises powers assigned to it by the Constitution.

The Indian Constitution establishes a Union with a federal distribution of powers, while also providing a relatively strong Union Government and mechanisms for national coordination.

Key Features of Indian Federalism

  • Written and supreme Constitution.
  • Constitutional distribution of legislative powers.
  • Two levels of government: Union and States.
  • Independent judiciary to interpret the Constitution and resolve disputes.
  • Bicameral Parliament, with the Rajya Sabha representing the States.
  • Special procedures for constitutional amendments affecting federal provisions.
  • Single citizenship and an integrated judicial system.
  • Emergency provisions that can strengthen Union authority in specified circumstances.

2. Constitutional Distribution of Powers

Articles 245–255 and the Seventh Schedule establish the principal framework for legislative relations between the Union and the States.

List Legislative authority Examples
Union List Parliament has exclusive power, subject to constitutional provisions. Defence, foreign affairs, currency and railways.
State List State Legislatures ordinarily have exclusive power, subject to constitutional exceptions. Police, public order, agriculture and public health.
Concurrent List Both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate, subject to Article 254 and other constitutional rules. Education, forests, marriage and criminal law.

Under Article 248, Parliament has residuary legislative power. Article 249 permits Parliament to legislate on a State List matter in the national interest when the Rajya Sabha passes the required resolution.

3. Cooperative Federalism

Cooperative federalism refers to collaboration between the Union, States and local governments in policy formulation, resource sharing and programme implementation.

Principles

  • Consultation and dialogue between governments.
  • Shared responsibility for national development.
  • Coordination in policy and programme implementation.
  • Fiscal cooperation and equitable resource distribution.
  • Respect for constitutional roles and regional diversity.
  • Joint efforts to address inter-State and national challenges.

4. Institutional Mechanisms for Cooperation

Institution / Mechanism Role
Inter-State Council Provides a forum for discussion and coordination on matters of common interest between the Union and States.
Zonal Councils Promote regional cooperation and resolve inter-State issues through consultation.
GST Council Provides a constitutional forum for recommendations on important aspects of GST.
Finance Commission Recommends distribution of tax revenues and grants-in-aid under its constitutional mandate.
NITI Aayog Governing Council Provides a platform for the Prime Minister, Chief Ministers and Lieutenant Governors to discuss development priorities.
National Development Council Historically provided a forum for Centre-State discussion on planning and development; it is not an active contemporary institution.
Regional Councils and joint working groups Facilitate cooperation on specific regional or policy issues.

5. Important Constitutional Provisions

  • Article 263: Provides for the establishment of an Inter-State Council to investigate and discuss subjects of common interest and make recommendations.
  • Article 280: Provides for the Finance Commission.
  • Article 246: Distributes legislative powers between Parliament and State Legislatures through the Seventh Schedule.
  • Article 246A: Provides special legislative power relating to GST to Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Article 269A: Provides for levy and collection of GST on inter-State supplies.
  • Article 279A: Provides for the GST Council.
  • Article 282: Allows the Union or a State to make grants for any public purpose, subject to constitutional provisions.
  • Article 293: Deals with borrowing by States.
  • Article 131: Provides the Supreme Court's original jurisdiction in specified disputes involving the Union and States or between States.

6. Fiscal Federalism

Fiscal federalism concerns the distribution of taxation powers, public expenditure responsibilities and financial resources between different levels of government.

Major Components

  • Vertical devolution: Distribution of the divisible pool of Union taxes between the Union and the States.
  • Horizontal devolution: Distribution of the States' share among individual States.
  • Grants-in-aid: Financial assistance recommended by the Finance Commission under Article 280 and other grants permitted by the Constitution.
  • GST: A shared indirect tax framework requiring coordination between the Union and States.
  • Borrowing: Constitutional rules govern borrowing by the Union and States.

Challenges in Fiscal Federalism

  • Vertical fiscal imbalance between revenue capacity and expenditure responsibilities.
  • Unequal fiscal capacity among States.
  • Dependence on transfers and grants.
  • Concerns regarding the predictability and flexibility of funds.
  • Differences over tax devolution, cesses and surcharges.
  • Need for greater consultation on fiscal policy decisions.

7. Competitive Federalism

Competitive federalism encourages States and Union Territories to improve governance, attract investment, develop infrastructure and deliver better public services by comparing their performance with one another.

Major Features

  • Performance benchmarking across States.
  • Competition to attract investment and create jobs.
  • Innovation in public service delivery.
  • Improved ease of doing business and regulatory processes.
  • Use of rankings, indicators and performance dashboards.
  • Sharing of successful practices and policy innovations.

Examples of Performance-Based Comparisons

  • State-level ease of doing business assessments.
  • SDG India Index published by NITI Aayog.
  • Health, education and innovation-related indices.
  • Performance assessments in local governance and public service delivery.

8. Cooperative and Competitive Federalism: Comparison

Basis Cooperative Federalism Competitive Federalism
Approach Collaboration and coordination. Performance comparison and competition.
Objective Achieve shared national and regional goals. Improve performance and innovation.
Mechanism Councils, consultations and joint programmes. Rankings, indicators and benchmarking.
Relationship Emphasises collective responsibility. Emphasises State-level initiative and performance.
Expected outcome Coordination and balanced development. Efficiency, innovation and improved service delivery.

The two approaches are complementary. Cooperation can provide common platforms and resources, while competition can encourage innovation and improved performance.

9. NITI Aayog and Cooperative Federalism

NITI Aayog was established on 1 January 2015 as an executive policy think tank, replacing the Planning Commission. It seeks to promote cooperative and competitive federalism.

Important Features

  • Governing Council provides a platform for participation by Chief Ministers and Lieutenant Governors.
  • Encourages States to participate in national development priorities.
  • Promotes policy innovation and sharing of best practices.
  • Uses indices and performance assessments to encourage competition.
  • Supports long-term policy thinking and evidence-based governance.

NITI Aayog is not a constitutional or statutory body and does not have the financial devolution powers of the Finance Commission.

10. Challenges to Cooperative Federalism

  • Centralisation: Perceptions of excessive central control can weaken State autonomy.
  • Fiscal dependence: States may face constraints in raising resources for their responsibilities.
  • Political differences: Differences between governments can affect consultation and cooperation.
  • Governor-related issues: Disagreements concerning gubernatorial powers and constitutional conventions can create friction.
  • Inter-State disputes: River-water, boundary and resource disputes can strain relations.
  • Unequal capacity: Differences in administrative and fiscal capacity make uniform implementation difficult.
  • Conditional transfers: Excessively rigid conditions can limit local policy flexibility.
  • Weak institutional dialogue: Infrequent meetings and inadequate follow-up can reduce the effectiveness of coordination bodies.

11. Measures to Strengthen Federal Relations

  1. Strengthen regular consultation through the Inter-State Council and other constitutional forums.
  2. Promote predictable and transparent fiscal transfers.
  3. Respect the constitutional division of legislative and executive responsibilities.
  4. Improve consultation with States before major policy changes affecting them.
  5. Strengthen State capacity through technical and administrative support.
  6. Resolve inter-State disputes through dialogue, negotiation and constitutional mechanisms.
  7. Promote evidence-based performance assessment that accounts for different starting conditions.
  8. Encourage local government participation in federal policy implementation.
  9. Share successful practices and support innovation across States.
  10. Maintain institutional trust and constitutional conventions.

12. Key Takeaways

  • Indian federalism combines constitutional division of powers with strong mechanisms for Union coordination.
  • Cooperative federalism emphasises consultation, coordination and shared responsibility.
  • Competitive federalism encourages States to improve governance through performance comparison.
  • The Inter-State Council, Finance Commission, GST Council and Zonal Councils are important institutional mechanisms.
  • NITI Aayog promotes cooperative and competitive federalism through consultation and policy coordination.
  • Fiscal autonomy, institutional trust and respect for constitutional boundaries are essential for healthy Centre-State relations.
