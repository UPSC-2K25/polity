Transparency and Accountability

Transparency and accountability are essential foundations of democratic governance. Transparency enables citizens to access information about public decisions and actions, while accountability requires public authorities to explain their conduct and accept responsibility for their performance.

1. Meaning of Transparency

Transparency refers to openness in public administration and decision-making. It requires government institutions to make relevant information about policies, procedures, expenditure, decisions and performance accessible to citizens, subject to lawful restrictions.

Transparency reduces information asymmetry between the State and citizens. It enables public scrutiny, informed participation and the detection of irregularities.

2. Meaning of Accountability

Accountability is the obligation of public officials and institutions to explain and justify their decisions, actions and use of public resources. It also involves mechanisms for review, correction and consequences where duties are not properly performed.

Accountability is not limited to punishment. It also includes learning from mistakes, improving institutional performance and ensuring that public authority is exercised in accordance with law and public interest.

3. Relationship Between Transparency and Accountability

Transparency provides the information necessary for accountability. Without access to information, citizens, legislatures and oversight institutions may find it difficult to evaluate government performance. Accountability, in turn, ensures that disclosure of information leads to scrutiny and appropriate corrective action.

Basis Transparency Accountability
Core idea Openness and access to information. Answerability and responsibility for conduct.
Primary purpose Enable scrutiny and informed participation. Ensure proper performance and correction of failures.
Key mechanism Disclosure, records and public reporting. Audits, review, oversight and grievance redressal.
Outcome Reduced secrecy and information asymmetry. Responsible exercise of public authority.

4. Constitutional Foundations

  • Article 14: Equality before law and equal protection of laws support non-arbitrary public action.
  • Article 19(1)(a): Freedom of speech and expression has been interpreted by the Supreme Court to include the right to know in appropriate contexts.
  • Article 21: Protection of life and personal liberty provides a wider constitutional basis for fair and lawful administration.
  • Article 75(3): The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
  • Article 148: Establishes the office of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India.
  • Article 149: Provides for the duties and powers of the CAG as prescribed by Parliament.
  • Articles 243A and 243S: Provide for Gram Sabhas and Wards Committees, supporting local participation and oversight.

5. Legislative and Institutional Mechanisms

Right to Information Act, 2005

The RTI Act enables citizens to seek information from public authorities. It provides for Public Information Officers, proactive disclosure, Information Commissions and an appeal mechanism, subject to statutory exemptions.

Comptroller and Auditor General

The CAG audits government receipts and expenditure and submits reports to the President or Governor for placement before the appropriate legislature. Audit findings support legislative financial oversight.

Parliamentary Committees

Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee and Committee on Public Undertakings examine public expenditure, administrative performance and the functioning of public enterprises.

Central Vigilance Commission

The CVC exercises vigilance-related functions under its statutory framework and supports integrity in central government administration.

Central Information Commission

The CIC hears appeals and complaints under the RTI Act in matters falling within its jurisdiction and monitors implementation of the information-access framework.

Lokpal and Lokayuktas

These anti-corruption institutions provide mechanisms for dealing with specified allegations of corruption against public functionaries under their respective legal frameworks.

6. Administrative and Social Accountability

  • Citizens' Charters: Publicly state service standards, timelines and grievance channels.
  • Social Audits: Enable beneficiaries and communities to verify records, expenditure and delivery of public programmes.
  • Public Hearings: Provide a forum for citizens to question authorities and raise service-related concerns.
  • Grievance Redressal: Allows citizens to report administrative failures and seek remedies.
  • Citizen Report Cards: Capture users' experiences and assessments of public services.
  • Performance Audits: Assess whether programmes achieve economy, efficiency and effectiveness.

7. Importance in Democratic Governance

  1. Strengthens public trust in democratic institutions.
  2. Helps detect corruption, misuse of funds and administrative irregularities.
  3. Improves the quality of public services and policy implementation.
  4. Enables citizens to participate in informed public debate.
  5. Promotes responsible use of public resources.
  6. Strengthens legislative and institutional oversight.
  7. Protects citizens against arbitrary and unresponsive administration.

8. Challenges

  • Secrecy and restricted access: Excessive classification and weak disclosure practices can limit public scrutiny.
  • Information delays: Vacancies, inadequate staffing and poor record management can delay responses to information requests.
  • Weak enforcement: Oversight bodies may face capacity constraints and delays in follow-up action.
  • Fear and retaliation: Citizens and whistleblowers may face pressure or threats when exposing wrongdoing.
  • Digital exclusion: Online disclosure and complaint systems may not be accessible to all citizens.
  • Privacy concerns: Transparency must be balanced with personal privacy and lawful confidentiality.
  • Token compliance: Formal disclosure without usable information or corrective action weakens the purpose of accountability.

9. Measures for Strengthening Transparency and Accountability

  1. Improve proactive disclosure and maintain accurate, searchable and regularly updated public records.
  2. Strengthen Information Commissions and other oversight bodies through adequate staffing and infrastructure.
  3. Protect whistleblowers and ensure safe channels for reporting corruption and misconduct.
  4. Expand social audits and public hearings in welfare programmes and local service delivery.
  5. Establish clear service standards and time-bound grievance redressal systems.
  6. Strengthen internal audits, financial controls and legislative scrutiny.
  7. Improve digital access while retaining assisted and offline channels for citizens who need them.
  8. Promote ethical leadership and a culture of public service responsibility.

10. Key Takeaways

  • Transparency makes public action visible and open to scrutiny.
  • Accountability ensures public authorities answer for their conduct and performance.
  • The RTI framework and public audit are important instruments of oversight.
  • Social accountability brings citizens directly into the monitoring process.
  • Effective systems must balance openness with privacy, security and lawful confidentiality.
