Local Governance in India

Local governance refers to institutions and processes through which people participate in decision-making and administration at the grassroots level. Constitutional recognition of Panchayati Raj Institutions and Urban Local Bodies seeks to deepen democracy, decentralise power and improve public service delivery.

1. Meaning and Importance of Local Governance

Local self-government is a system in which elected institutions at the village, intermediate, district or urban level exercise responsibilities over local affairs.

Importance

  • Brings government closer to citizens.
  • Promotes democratic participation and accountability.
  • Helps identify local needs and priorities.
  • Encourages inclusive development and representation of disadvantaged groups.
  • Improves the planning and delivery of local public services.
  • Creates opportunities for local leadership and social mobilisation.
  • Strengthens the foundations of democratic governance.

2. Evolution of Local Self-Government in India

3. Constitutional Framework: 73rd Amendment

The Constitution (Seventy-third Amendment) Act, 1992 inserted Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule into the Constitution. It came into force on 24 April 1993.

Three-Tier Structure

  • Gram Panchayat: Village-level institution.
  • Panchayat Samiti: Intermediate or block-level institution.
  • Zila Parishad: District-level institution.

States with a population not exceeding twenty lakh may choose not to constitute Panchayats at the intermediate level.

4. Gram Sabha

Article 243A provides for the Gram Sabha. It consists of persons registered in the electoral rolls relating to a village comprised within the area of a Gram Panchayat.

Functions and Significance

  • Provides a forum for direct participation in local democracy.
  • Discusses local development priorities and needs.
  • Participates in identifying beneficiaries under applicable programmes.
  • Reviews the functioning of the Gram Panchayat.
  • Promotes social audit and public accountability.
  • Provides a platform for raising local grievances.

The precise powers and procedures of Gram Sabhas are governed by State legislation.

5. Reservation and Representation in Panchayats

Article 243D provides for reservation in Panchayats:

  • Seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in proportion to their population in the Panchayat area.
  • Not less than one-third of the total seats to be filled by direct election are reserved for women, including seats reserved for SC and ST women.
  • Not less than one-third of the total number of offices of Chairpersons at each level are reserved for women.
  • States may provide reservation for backward classes.
  • States may provide a higher proportion of reservation for women under their laws.

Reservation has expanded the political participation of women and historically disadvantaged communities in rural decision-making.

7. Constitutional Framework: 74th Amendment

The Constitution (Seventy-fourth Amendment) Act, 1992 inserted Part IXA and the Twelfth Schedule into the Constitution. It came into force on 1 June 1993 and provides the constitutional framework for urban local bodies.

Types of Urban Local Bodies

  • Nagar Panchayat: For a transitional area moving from rural to urban character.
  • Municipal Council: For a smaller urban area.
  • Municipal Corporation: For a larger urban area.

9. Local Government Finance

Effective decentralisation requires adequate, predictable and autonomous financial resources. Local bodies generally depend on own-source revenue, assigned revenues, grants and other transfers.

Sources of Revenue

  • Own-source revenue: Property taxes, fees, user charges, licences and other local levies permitted by law.
  • Assigned revenue: A share of specified State revenues assigned to local bodies.
  • Grants-in-aid: Transfers from State and Union Governments.
  • Finance Commission grants: Recommendations of the Central Finance Commission and State Finance Commissions.
  • Borrowing: Permitted financing arrangements subject to applicable laws and conditions.

State Finance Commission

Article 243I requires the Governor of a State to constitute a Finance Commission every five years to review the financial position of Panchayats and make recommendations. Article 243Y extends the relevant Finance Commission framework to municipalities.

State Finance Commissions recommend principles for distributing State revenues, assigning taxes and providing grants to local bodies, as well as measures to improve their financial position.

10. Elections and Accountability

State Election Commission

Articles 243K and 243ZA provide for superintendence, direction and control of Panchayat and municipal elections by the State Election Commission.

Duration

  • The normal duration of a Panchayat or municipality is five years from the date appointed for its first meeting.
  • Election to constitute a new body is ordinarily required before the expiry of its term.
  • If dissolved prematurely, elections are generally required within six months, subject to the constitutional exception where the remainder of the term is less than six months.
  • A body constituted after premature dissolution serves only for the remainder of the original term.

Accountability Mechanisms

  • Gram Sabha meetings and public participation.
  • Ward Committees and citizen consultations.
  • Social audits and public disclosure.
  • Audit of local body accounts.
  • Local grievance redressal mechanisms.
  • Transparency in budgets, expenditure and procurement.

11. Special Provisions for Scheduled Areas

The Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996, commonly known as PESA, extends Part IX of the Constitution to Fifth Schedule areas with specified modifications and exceptions.

Important Features of PESA

  • Recognises the central role of Gram Sabhas in Scheduled Areas.
  • Seeks to protect customary law, social and religious practices and traditional resource management.
  • Requires consultation with the Gram Sabha or appropriate Panchayat before land acquisition for development projects and before resettlement or rehabilitation in Scheduled Areas, as provided by the Act.
  • Provides for ownership of minor forest produce to Gram Sabhas or Panchayats at the appropriate level, subject to the statutory framework.
  • Provides powers relating to village markets, money-lending and management of community resources under the Act.
  • Requires reservation of seats in Scheduled Area Panchayats in accordance with the special provisions of PESA.

PESA is intended to make local self-government responsive to the social, cultural and resource-related rights of Adivasi communities.

13. Challenges in Local Governance

  • Incomplete devolution: Functions, funds and functionaries are not always transferred adequately.
  • Financial constraints: Many local bodies have a limited own-source revenue base and depend on transfers.
  • Capacity gaps: Shortages of trained staff, technical expertise and administrative support affect performance.
  • Political interference: External influence can weaken the autonomy of elected representatives.
  • Proxy representation: In some areas, elected women representatives face interference from family or other influential persons.
  • Weak Gram Sabhas: Low attendance and limited awareness reduce meaningful citizen participation.
  • Delayed elections: Delays undermine democratic accountability and continuity.
  • Parallel institutions: Overlapping agencies and development bodies can weaken elected local institutions.
  • Urban governance fragmentation: Multiple agencies may exercise overlapping responsibilities in metropolitan areas.
  • Social inequalities: Caste, gender and economic hierarchies can restrict participation and access to benefits.
  • Weak accountability: Inadequate auditing, transparency and grievance redressal can lead to poor service delivery.

14. Measures to Strengthen Local Governance

  1. Implement meaningful devolution of the three Fs: functions, funds and functionaries.
  2. Strengthen Gram Sabhas and Ward Committees through regular meetings and informed participation.
  3. Ensure timely local body elections and strengthen State Election Commissions.
  4. Constitute State Finance Commissions regularly and act on their recommendations.
  5. Improve local revenue mobilisation through better property tax systems, user charges and financial management.
  6. Provide continuous training and professional support to elected representatives and officials.
  7. Strengthen social audits, public disclosure and independent auditing.
  8. Promote meaningful participation of women, Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other disadvantaged groups.
  9. Clarify responsibilities and improve coordination among local bodies and other agencies.
  10. Use digital platforms while ensuring accessibility for citizens with limited digital access.
  11. Strengthen district and metropolitan planning committees for integrated development planning.

15. Key Takeaways

  • The 73rd Amendment gave constitutional recognition to Panchayati Raj Institutions through Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule.
  • The 74th Amendment established the constitutional framework for municipalities through Part IXA and the Twelfth Schedule.
  • Articles 243G and 243W provide the framework for devolution of functions to rural and urban local bodies.
  • Gram Sabhas and Ward Committees provide avenues for direct citizen participation.
  • State Finance Commissions and State Election Commissions are important constitutional institutions for local governance.
  • PESA extends the Panchayati Raj framework to Fifth Schedule areas with special safeguards.
  • Effective local governance requires genuine devolution, adequate finances, capable institutions and citizen participation.
