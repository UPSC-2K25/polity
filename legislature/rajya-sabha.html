Indian Legislature · Chapter 03

Rajya Sabha

The Rajya Sabha is the Council of States and the Upper House of the Indian Parliament. It provides representation to States and certain Union Territories, acts as a revising chamber for legislation and performs special constitutional functions important to India's federal system.

1. Meaning and purpose of the Rajya Sabha

The Rajya Sabha is the second chamber of the Indian Parliament. While the Lok Sabha represents the people directly through elections, the Rajya Sabha provides a different form of representation, primarily representing the States of the Indian Union.

India adopted a bicameral Parliament at the Union level. Bicameralism means that the legislature consists of two chambers. The two Houses examine legislation from different perspectives and participate in parliamentary decision-making.

Why does India need a second House?

A second chamber serves several purposes. It provides an additional opportunity to examine proposed laws, gives States a voice at the Union level, and allows experienced individuals to contribute to parliamentary debate.

The Rajya Sabha can also provide continuity in Parliament. Since it is not dissolved, parliamentary representation does not disappear entirely when the Lok Sabha completes its term or is dissolved.

Core idea: The Lok Sabha primarily reflects the will of the people. The Rajya Sabha adds a federal and revising dimension to parliamentary government. The two Houses are complementary, but their constitutional powers are not identical.

2. Constitutional position

Article 79 provides that Parliament consists of the President and two Houses: the Council of States and the House of the People. The Rajya Sabha is the constitutional name for the Council of States.

The Rajya Sabha is not merely an advisory body. It is a constitutional House of Parliament and participates in ordinary legislation, constitutional amendments and several important parliamentary decisions.

However, the Constitution gives it a distinct role in certain matters. For example, it has special powers under Articles 249 and 312, while the Lok Sabha has greater authority in Money Bill proceedings and control over the Council of Ministers.

3. Composition of the Rajya Sabha

Article 80 deals with the composition of the Council of States. The Constitution provides for a maximum strength of 250 members.

The constitutional composition includes:

  • Not more than 238 representatives of the States and Union Territories.
  • 12 members nominated by the President.

The actual number of members at a given time may be lower than the constitutional maximum.

Representation of States

States are allocated seats in the Rajya Sabha broadly according to their population, as provided by the constitutional scheme. Consequently, all States do not have an equal number of representatives.

This is an important distinction between the Indian Rajya Sabha and certain federal second chambers in other countries, where every constituent State may have equal representation.

Representation of Union Territories

The Constitution permits representation of Union Territories in the Rajya Sabha as provided by parliamentary law. The method of representation depends on the applicable constitutional and statutory provisions.

Nominated members

The President nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha from among persons who have special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service.

The purpose is to bring knowledge, experience and expertise into parliamentary discussion, even where those individuals may not have entered Parliament through the ordinary electoral process.

Remember: The President nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha. The Constitution identifies literature, science, art and social service as the relevant fields.

4. Election of Rajya Sabha members

Unlike Lok Sabha members, most Rajya Sabha members are not directly elected by the general electorate. They are elected indirectly by the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies.

Method of election

Article 80 provides for the election of representatives of States by the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies through the system of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.

This method allows votes to be transferred according to the preferences expressed by electors. It is intended to provide representation to different political groups in proportion to their strength, subject to the electoral system.

What is proportional representation?

Under a proportional representation system, the objective is to distribute seats in a manner that broadly reflects the relative strength of different groups or parties, rather than simply awarding every seat to the candidate with the largest number of votes.

What is the single transferable vote?

In the single transferable vote system, an elector ranks candidates in order of preference. A candidate who secures the required quota is elected. Surplus votes and votes received by candidates who cannot be elected may be transferred according to the prescribed counting rules.

For Rajya Sabha elections, the electors are the elected members of the relevant State Legislative Assembly, not the entire population of the State.

Election in Union Territories

Representatives of Union Territories are chosen according to the method Parliament provides by law. The electoral arrangements are therefore not necessarily identical for every Union Territory.

UPSC distinction: Rajya Sabha representatives of States are elected by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies. Nominated members are appointed by the President. These are two different routes of membership.

5. Duration: Why is the Rajya Sabha a permanent House?

Article 83(1) provides that the Rajya Sabha is not subject to dissolution. This means that the entire House does not cease to exist at the end of a fixed term.

However, this does not mean that individual members hold office permanently. Each member normally has a term of six years.

As nearly as possible, one-third of the members retire every second year. Vacancies are then filled through elections or nominations, as applicable.

Why is this arrangement important?

The staggered retirement system ensures continuity. Even when a new Lok Sabha is elected, the Rajya Sabha continues to function with members who have different lengths of parliamentary experience.

This reduces the possibility of a complete break in parliamentary functioning and preserves an institutional memory of legislative work.

Do not confuse: The Rajya Sabha is permanent, but its members are not permanent. The House is never dissolved, while individual members normally serve six-year terms.

6. Chairman and Deputy Chairman

Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

The Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha under Article 89. The Chairman presides over the sittings of the House and performs functions relating to the conduct of parliamentary proceedings.

Important functions include:

  • Presiding over sittings of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Maintaining order and decorum in the House.
  • Applying the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business.
  • Deciding points of order under the applicable rules.
  • Exercising a casting vote in case of equality of votes.

The Chairman is not a member of the Rajya Sabha merely by virtue of holding this office. The Vice-President does not vote in the first instance but may exercise a casting vote when the votes are equally divided.

Deputy Chairman

Article 89 provides for the Rajya Sabha to choose one of its members to be Deputy Chairman.

The Deputy Chairman performs the duties of the Chairman when the office is vacant or when the Chairman is absent from a sitting.

Removal of the Deputy Chairman

Under Article 90, the Deputy Chairman may resign by writing to the Chairman or be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members of the House, subject to the constitutional notice requirement.

Important distinction: The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. The Deputy Chairman, however, is elected by the Rajya Sabha from among its own members.

7. Powers and functions of the Rajya Sabha

7.1 Legislative powers

The Rajya Sabha participates in the ordinary legislative process. An ordinary Bill may generally be introduced in either House of Parliament.

It examines Bills, debates their provisions and may suggest amendments. Ordinarily, a Bill must be passed by both Houses in the same form before it can be presented to the President for assent.

If the two Houses disagree on an ordinary Bill, the Constitution provides for a possible joint sitting under Article 108, subject to the conditions and exceptions in that Article.

7.2 Financial powers

The Rajya Sabha participates in financial legislation, but its powers are more limited than those of the Lok Sabha.

A Money Bill cannot be introduced in the Rajya Sabha. After the Lok Sabha passes a Money Bill, it is transmitted to the Rajya Sabha for its recommendations.

The Rajya Sabha must return the Bill within 14 days. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject any or all of the recommendations. If the Rajya Sabha does not return the Bill within that period, it is deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form in which it was passed by the Lok Sabha.

The Rajya Sabha can discuss the Union Budget and matters of public finance, but it does not vote on Demands for Grants.

7.3 Executive accountability

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha under Article 75(3), not to the Rajya Sabha.

Nevertheless, the Rajya Sabha can question Ministers, hold discussions, raise matters of public importance and examine government policies. Ministers may be members of either House and are accountable to Parliament through parliamentary proceedings.

The Rajya Sabha cannot by itself remove the Council of Ministers through a no-confidence motion. The constitutional principle of collective responsibility is specifically linked to the Lok Sabha.

7.4 Constitutional amendment powers

The Rajya Sabha has an important role in amending the Constitution under Article 368. A Constitution Amendment Bill must be passed separately by each House with the prescribed special majority.

There is no provision for a joint sitting to resolve a disagreement between the two Houses on a Constitution Amendment Bill. Therefore, the Rajya Sabha can prevent an amendment from being passed if it does not approve the Bill with the required majority.

7.5 Electoral functions

Elected members of the Rajya Sabha participate in the election of the President of India. Members of both Houses of Parliament participate in the election of the Vice-President.

The Rajya Sabha also elects its Deputy Chairman from among its members.

7.6 Judicial functions

The Rajya Sabha participates in certain constitutional proceedings relating to the removal of high constitutional authorities.

For example, both Houses participate in the impeachment process of the President and in the prescribed process for removal of judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.

The Rajya Sabha also has a specific role in the removal of the Vice-President under Article 67(b), as explained later in this chapter.

8. Special powers of the Rajya Sabha

One of the most important features of the Rajya Sabha is that the Constitution grants it certain special powers that are not available to the Lok Sabha in the same form.

8.1 Article 249: Power to enable Parliament to legislate on a State List subject

Under Article 249, if the Rajya Sabha declares by a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest for Parliament to make laws with respect to a matter in the State List, Parliament becomes empowered to legislate on that matter.

The resolution remains in force for a period not exceeding one year, but it may be renewed for further periods of up to one year at a time.

This provision is significant because it allows Parliament to legislate on a State List matter in the national interest through a special constitutional mechanism.

8.2 Article 312: Creation of All India Services

Under Article 312, the Rajya Sabha may pass a resolution, supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting, declaring that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest to create one or more All India Services common to the Union and the States.

Once such a resolution is passed, Parliament may provide by law for the creation of the service or services.

This provision reflects the Rajya Sabha's role in protecting and coordinating the interests of the Union and the States.

8.3 Article 67(b): Removal of the Vice-President

A resolution for the removal of the Vice-President must be initiated in the Rajya Sabha. It must be passed by a majority of all the then members of the Rajya Sabha and agreed to by the Lok Sabha.

The resolution also requires at least 14 days' notice of the intention to move it.

This is a special role because the Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

UPSC must-remember Articles: Article 249 enables Parliament to legislate on State List matters in the national interest. Article 312 concerns the creation of All India Services. Article 67(b) concerns removal of the Vice-President.

9. Rajya Sabha and Indian federalism

Federalism involves the distribution of governmental powers between the Union and the States. A second chamber at the Union level can provide a forum through which State interests are represented in national law-making.

The Rajya Sabha performs this role by including representatives elected by State Legislative Assemblies. This creates an institutional link between State legislatures and the Union Parliament.

However, India's system does not provide equal representation to every State. The number of seats allocated to States varies broadly according to population. This makes the Indian model different from federations where every State has the same number of representatives in the upper chamber.

How does it protect federal interests?

  • It provides State representation in the Union legislature.
  • It participates in ordinary law-making on matters within Parliament's legislative competence.
  • It can authorise Parliament to legislate on State List matters under Article 249.
  • It can authorise the creation of All India Services under Article 312.
  • It provides continuity in parliamentary representation because it is not dissolved.
Conceptual point: The Rajya Sabha is an important federal institution, but it does not represent States through equal seat allocation. Its composition reflects India's own constitutional model of federalism.

11. Limitations of the Rajya Sabha

Although the Rajya Sabha is a full constitutional House of Parliament, it does not have equal powers with the Lok Sabha in every area.

Financial limitations

The Rajya Sabha cannot introduce a Money Bill. It can only recommend changes to a Money Bill passed by the Lok Sabha, and the Lok Sabha decides whether to accept those recommendations.

It also cannot vote on Demands for Grants. These provisions give the Lok Sabha a decisive role in financial control.

Limitations in executive control

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. Therefore, the Rajya Sabha cannot independently bring down the Union government by passing a no-confidence motion.

Limits on joint sittings

A joint sitting may resolve certain disagreements between the two Houses over ordinary legislation. However, the joint sitting mechanism does not apply to Money Bills or Constitution Amendment Bills.

12. Sessions and proceedings

The Rajya Sabha meets as part of Parliament. Article 85 provides for the summoning of Parliament and requires that no more than six months intervene between the last sitting in one session and the first sitting in the next session.

Its members participate in parliamentary questions, debates, discussions, legislative proceedings and committee work.

The Rajya Sabha follows its own Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business, subject to the Constitution.

Question Hour and discussions

Members may ask questions to Ministers and seek information about government policies and administration. Discussions allow members to examine public issues and express different viewpoints.

Committees

Rajya Sabha members participate in parliamentary committees. Some committees consist of members from both Houses, while others are associated with one House. Committee work allows detailed examination of legislation, expenditure and government activity.

  • Article 80 deals with its composition.
  • Its constitutional maximum strength is 250 members.
  • 12 members are nominated by the President.
  • State representatives are elected by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies.
  • The election uses proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.
  • The minimum age for membership is 30 years.
  • Members normally serve six-year terms.
  • One-third of the members retire every second year, as nearly as possible.
  • The Rajya Sabha is a permanent House and cannot be dissolved.
  • The Vice-President is its ex-officio Chairman.

15. Check your understanding

Question 1

Why is the Rajya Sabha called a permanent House?

Answer: Because it is not subject to dissolution. Individual members retire in rotation, but the House continues to exist.

Question 2

Who elects the representatives of States to the Rajya Sabha?

Answer: The elected members of the respective State Legislative Assemblies.

Question 3

Can the Rajya Sabha reject a Money Bill?

Answer: No. It can make recommendations and must return the Bill within 14 days.

Question 4

Which House can pass a resolution under Article 249?

Answer: The Rajya Sabha, with the support of not less than two-thirds of members present and voting.

Question 5

Can the Rajya Sabha remove the Union Council of Ministers through a no-confidence motion?

Answer: No. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
