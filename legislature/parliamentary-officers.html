Indian Legislature · Chapter 04

Parliamentary Officers

Parliamentary officers ensure that the two Houses of Parliament function in an orderly, impartial and constitutionally valid manner. Their responsibilities include presiding over sittings, interpreting procedural rules, maintaining discipline and supporting the functioning of Parliament.

1. Why does Parliament need presiding officers?

Parliament is a deliberative institution. Members belonging to different political parties and representing different viewpoints come together to debate public issues, examine legislation and hold the government accountable.

Without an authority to regulate proceedings, parliamentary discussions could become disorderly. The Constitution and parliamentary rules therefore provide for presiding officers who conduct the business of each House.

The Lok Sabha has a Speaker and a Deputy Speaker. The Rajya Sabha has a Chairman and a Deputy Chairman.

Basic distinction: The Speaker presides over the Lok Sabha. The Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. The two offices have different constitutional foundations.

2. Speaker of the Lok Sabha

Constitutional basis

Article 93 provides that the Lok Sabha shall, as soon as may be, choose two members of the House to be Speaker and Deputy Speaker.

The Speaker is elected by the Lok Sabha from among its own members. The Speaker is therefore a member of the House who occupies a special constitutional office.

Election of the Speaker

After a new Lok Sabha is constituted, it elects its Speaker. The election is conducted according to the applicable parliamentary procedure.

The Speaker ordinarily continues in office until immediately before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha, even after the dissolution of the existing House. This continuity is expressly recognised by Article 94.

Functions and powers of the Speaker

The Speaker performs several important functions.

Presiding over the House

The Speaker presides over sittings of the Lok Sabha and regulates the conduct of business. Members address the Chair while speaking and are expected to follow the Speaker's directions.

Maintaining order and discipline

The Speaker maintains order and decorum in the House. The Speaker may direct members to follow parliamentary rules and may take action in accordance with those rules when disorderly conduct occurs.

Interpreting parliamentary rules

The Speaker applies the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business of the Lok Sabha. The Speaker also gives rulings on points of order raised during proceedings.

Certifying Money Bills

Article 110 defines a Money Bill. When a question arises whether a Bill is a Money Bill, the decision of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha is constitutionally significant. Article 110(3) states that the Speaker's decision on whether a Bill is a Money Bill is final.

Presiding over joint sittings

Under Article 108, when a joint sitting of both Houses is summoned to resolve a legislative deadlock, the Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over it.

Deciding questions under the Tenth Schedule

The Speaker decides questions of disqualification of Lok Sabha members under the anti-defection provisions of the Tenth Schedule.

Such decisions are subject to judicial review, as recognised by the Supreme Court.

Committee-related functions

The Speaker has an important role in the parliamentary committee system, including the constitution of certain committees and the appointment of their members, according to the applicable rules and conventions.

UPSC focus: The Speaker is not merely a person who conducts debates. The office is central to legislative procedure, financial legislation, discipline and the functioning of parliamentary committees.

3. Independence and impartiality of the Speaker

The Speaker is elected from among the members of the Lok Sabha, who normally belong to political parties. However, once elected, the Speaker is expected to conduct proceedings impartially and protect the dignity of the House.

The Speaker's authority depends not only on constitutional provisions but also on parliamentary conventions, procedural fairness and the confidence of members.

Voting by the Speaker

Under Article 100, the Speaker ordinarily does not vote in the first instance. However, in the case of an equality of votes, the Speaker has and exercises a casting vote.

This arrangement is intended to maintain the Speaker's position as a neutral presiding authority rather than as an ordinary participant in voting.

Removal of the Speaker

Article 94 provides for the vacation, resignation and removal of the Speaker.

The Speaker may resign by writing to the Deputy Speaker. The Speaker may also be removed by a resolution of the Lok Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members of the House.

At least 14 days' notice of the intention to move such a resolution must be given.

When a resolution for the removal of the Speaker is under consideration, the Speaker may speak and participate in the proceedings and is entitled to vote in the first instance on that resolution. However, the Speaker cannot exercise a casting vote in the event of equality of votes on that resolution.

4. Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha

Article 93 provides for the election of a Deputy Speaker by the Lok Sabha from among its members.

The Deputy Speaker is an important constitutional office designed to ensure continuity in the conduct of the House when the Speaker is unavailable or the office is vacant.

Functions

  • Presides over the House when the Speaker is absent.
  • Performs the Speaker's duties when the office is vacant.
  • Exercises the powers of the presiding officer while presiding.

Resignation and removal

Under Article 94, the Deputy Speaker may resign by writing to the Speaker. The Deputy Speaker may be removed by a resolution of the Lok Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members of the House.

The resolution requires at least 14 days' notice.

Does the Deputy Speaker have to belong to the Opposition?

The Constitution does not require the Deputy Speaker to belong to the Opposition. The constitutional provision requires the House to elect the Deputy Speaker from among its members.

The expectation that the Deputy Speaker should belong to a party other than the ruling party is associated with parliamentary convention and political practice, not an express constitutional requirement.

5. Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

Constitutional basis

Article 89 provides that the Vice-President of India shall be ex-officio Chairman of the Council of States.

Unlike the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, the Chairman is not elected by the Rajya Sabha from among its members. The office is attached to the office of the Vice-President.

Functions of the Chairman

  • Presides over sittings of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Maintains order and decorum in the House.
  • Regulates debates and parliamentary proceedings.
  • Interprets and applies the Rules of Procedure.
  • Decides points of order under the applicable rules.
  • Exercises a casting vote in the event of equality of votes.

Voting by the Chairman

The Vice-President, while acting as Chairman, does not vote in the first instance. In the event of equality of votes, the Chairman exercises a casting vote.

This is different from the Vice-President's role as a member of the electoral college for the election of the President, where the Vice-President does not participate in voting merely by virtue of being Vice-President.

Removal of the Chairman

The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is the Vice-President of India. Therefore, removal from the office of Vice-President is governed by Article 67(b).

A resolution for removal must be moved in the Rajya Sabha and passed by a majority of all the then members of that House. It must then be agreed to by the Lok Sabha. At least 14 days' notice is required.

Important: The Chairman is not removed through the same procedure as the Deputy Chairman. The Chairman holds office as Vice-President, so Article 67(b) governs the removal process.

6. Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

Article 89 provides that the Rajya Sabha shall, as soon as may be, choose one of its members to be Deputy Chairman.

Unlike the Chairman, the Deputy Chairman is elected from among the members of the Rajya Sabha.

Functions

  • Presides over the House when the Chairman is absent.
  • Performs the Chairman's duties when the office is vacant.
  • Exercises the powers of the presiding officer while presiding.

Removal

Article 90 provides that the Deputy Chairman may resign by writing to the Chairman or be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members of the House.

At least 14 days' notice of the intention to move such a resolution is required.

8. Parliamentary Secretariat and Secretary-General

Parliamentary proceedings require extensive administrative and procedural support. The Constitution therefore provides for separate secretarial staff for each House of Parliament.

Article 98 deals with the Secretariat of Parliament. It provides for separate secretarial staff for each House, subject to the constitutional provisions and laws made by Parliament.

Role of the Secretary-General

The Secretary-General is the senior administrative and procedural officer of the respective House. The office supports the presiding officer and members in the conduct of parliamentary business.

Important responsibilities include:

  • Providing procedural and administrative support.
  • Assisting in the conduct of parliamentary sittings.
  • Supporting the preparation and circulation of parliamentary documents.
  • Maintaining official records of proceedings.
  • Assisting committees and members in parliamentary work.

The Secretary-General is not a presiding officer and does not replace the Speaker or Chairman. The role is primarily administrative and procedural.

9. Constitutional importance of parliamentary officers

Parliamentary officers help preserve the balance between majority rule and fair parliamentary procedure. Their work is particularly important when the House is discussing contentious Bills, financial matters or questions involving the conduct of members.

Their constitutional role is not limited to maintaining order. They help ensure that parliamentary business is conducted according to established rules and that members have an opportunity to participate in deliberation.

The effectiveness of these offices depends on constitutional authority, procedural knowledge, impartiality and respect for parliamentary conventions.

11. Quick revision

  • Article 93 provides for the Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
  • Article 89 provides for the Chairman and Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • The Speaker is elected by the Lok Sabha from among its members.
  • The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • The Deputy Speaker is elected by the Lok Sabha.
  • The Deputy Chairman is elected by the Rajya Sabha.
  • The Speaker certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill under Article 110.
  • The Speaker presides over a joint sitting under Article 108.
  • The Speaker decides disqualification questions under the Tenth Schedule for Lok Sabha members, subject to judicial review.
  • Article 98 provides for the Secretariat of Parliament.
  • The Deputy Speaker and Deputy Chairman are not required by the Constitution to belong to the Opposition.

12. Check your understanding

Question 1

Who is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?

Answer: The Vice-President of India.

Question 2

Can the Speaker vote in the first instance in the Lok Sabha?

Answer: Ordinarily, no. The Speaker may exercise a casting vote in the event of equality of votes.

Question 3

Which Article provides for the Speaker and Deputy Speaker?

Answer: Article 93.

Question 4

Does the Constitution require the Deputy Speaker to belong to the Opposition?

Answer: No. The Constitution requires election from among Lok Sabha members but does not prescribe party affiliation.

Question 5

Which Article deals with the Secretariat of Parliament?

Answer: Article 98.
