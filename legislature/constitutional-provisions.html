LEGISLATURE · CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Constitutional Provisions Relating to Legislatures

The Constitution establishes the structure, composition, powers and procedures of Parliament and State Legislatures. These provisions explain how legislative institutions are formed, how they function, and what constitutional limits govern their authority.

1. Introduction: Why These Provisions Matter

A legislature is not simply an assembly of elected representatives. It is a constitutional institution whose existence, composition, authority and procedures are defined by the Constitution.

The Constitution determines who may become a legislator, how representatives are elected, how long legislative Houses continue, how Bills become law, and how the executive is held accountable. It also places limits on legislative power through fundamental rights, federal distribution of powers and constitutional review.

The provisions relating to legislatures are primarily found in Part V, dealing with the Union, and Part VI, dealing with the States. Other important provisions appear in the Parts dealing with elections, finance, constitutional amendments and disqualification.

Important distinction: Part V deals with Parliament at the Union level, while Part VI deals with the legislatures of States. The two systems are similar in several respects, but they are not identical.

2. Constitutional Location of Legislative Provisions

The following Parts and chapters provide the main constitutional framework for legislative institutions.

Constitutional Part Subject Relevance to Legislatures
Part V The Union Establishes Parliament, its Houses, officers, legislative procedure and financial procedure.
Part VI The States Establishes State Legislatures, their composition, officers and legislative procedure.
Part XV Elections Provides the constitutional framework for elections and the Election Commission.
Part XI Relations between the Union and the States Defines legislative relations and the distribution of legislative powers.
Part XVIII Emergency Provisions Provides for changes in the exercise of legislative powers during emergencies.
Part XX Amendment of the Constitution Prescribes the procedure for constitutional amendments, including State ratification in specified cases.

5. Legislative Powers and Federal Distribution

Parliament and State Legislatures do not possess unlimited authority to legislate on every subject. The Constitution distributes legislative subjects between the Union and the States.

5.1 Article 245: Extent of laws

Article 245 deals with the territorial extent of laws made by Parliament and State Legislatures. Parliament may make laws for the whole or any part of the territory of India. A State Legislature may make laws for the whole or any part of that State, subject to constitutional provisions.

5.2 Article 246: Subject-matter of laws

Article 246 distributes legislative subjects with reference to the Seventh Schedule:

List Legislative authority Examples
Union List Parliament has exclusive authority, subject to the Constitution. Defence, foreign affairs, atomic energy and railways.
State List State Legislatures ordinarily legislate on these subjects, subject to constitutional exceptions. Police, public order, agriculture and public health.
Concurrent List Both Parliament and State Legislatures may legislate. Education, forests, marriage and criminal law.

5.3 Other important provisions

  • Article 247: Power of Parliament to provide for the establishment of additional courts for better administration of laws made by Parliament.
  • Article 248: Residuary powers of legislation vest in Parliament.
  • Article 249: Parliament may legislate on a State List matter in the national interest after a qualifying Rajya Sabha resolution.
  • Article 250: Parliament may legislate on State List matters while a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation.
  • Article 252: Parliament may legislate for two or more States by consent, with provision for other States to adopt that law.
  • Article 253: Parliament may make laws to implement international agreements and obligations.
  • Article 254: Deals with inconsistency between Union and State laws on Concurrent List subjects.
Remember: The Seventh Schedule contains the Union List, State List and Concurrent List. Article 246 explains the distribution of legislative authority, while Articles 249–253 provide important situations in which Parliament can legislate on State List matters.

6. Elections and Membership

Elections determine the representative character of legislatures. The Constitution establishes the institutional framework for elections and prescribes certain qualifications and disqualifications for membership.

  • Article 324: Vests the superintendence, direction and control of elections in the Election Commission of India.
  • Article 325: Provides for one general electoral roll and prohibits exclusion from electoral rolls on specified grounds.
  • Article 326: Provides for elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies on the basis of adult suffrage, subject to constitutional and legal provisions.
  • Article 327: Empowers Parliament to make laws concerning elections to legislatures.
  • Article 328: Allows State Legislatures to make laws concerning elections to their Houses, subject to the Constitution and parliamentary law.
  • Article 329: Places specified restrictions on judicial interference in electoral matters.

6.1 Disqualification and anti-defection

Membership is subject to constitutional and statutory conditions. The Constitution also provides for disqualification on grounds relating to defection.

  • Articles 102 and 191: Deal with disqualifications for membership of Parliament and State Legislatures respectively.
  • Tenth Schedule: Contains provisions relating to disqualification on the ground of defection.
  • Articles 103 and 192: Provide for decisions on specified disqualification questions concerning members of Parliament and State Legislatures.
Do not confuse: Articles 102 and 191 deal with constitutional disqualifications. The Tenth Schedule separately deals with disqualification on the ground of defection.

7. Parliamentary and Legislative Privileges

Legislatures need a degree of institutional independence to deliberate, debate and perform their constitutional functions. For this reason, the Constitution recognises certain privileges and immunities.

Article 105 deals with the powers, privileges and immunities of Parliament, its members and committees. Article 194 provides the corresponding framework for State Legislatures.

These provisions support freedom of speech within the legislative institution and protect the functioning of legislative proceedings. They do not mean that every action of a legislator is beyond the reach of the law or the Constitution.

Legislative privilege must be understood alongside constitutional supremacy, fundamental rights and the role of judicial review.

8. Special Provisions Affecting Legislative Functioning

8.1 Constitutional amendment — Article 368

Parliament exercises constituent power under Article 368 to amend the Constitution. Certain amendments require, in addition to the special majority in Parliament, ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures.

State Legislatures do not possess a general power to initiate or independently enact constitutional amendments. Their role in the amendment process is limited to ratification in the cases specified by Article 368.

8.2 Emergency provisions

During a National Emergency, Parliament's legislative authority over State List subjects expands under Article 250. The Constitution also provides special arrangements concerning legislative authority during President's Rule under Article 356.

These provisions alter the ordinary federal distribution of legislative powers in constitutionally specified circumstances.

8.3 President's Rule — Article 356

When a State government cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution and a Proclamation under Article 356 is in force, Parliament may exercise the legislative powers of the State Legislature, subject to the constitutional framework.

This is an exceptional arrangement. It does not permanently transfer State legislative authority to Parliament.

11. How to Study These Articles for UPSC

Memorising every Article in isolation is rarely an effective approach. A better method is to connect each provision with the institution or constitutional process it regulates.

  1. Start with the institution: Understand Parliament and State Legislatures before memorising their Articles.
  2. Group related Articles: Study composition, officers, membership, procedure and finance as separate clusters.
  3. Compare Union and State provisions: Many State provisions follow a pattern similar to the corresponding Union provisions.
  4. Connect Articles with Schedules: For example, study Article 246 together with the Seventh Schedule.
  5. Understand exceptions: Pay attention to provisions that allow Parliament to legislate on State List subjects.
  6. Use current examples carefully: Relate constitutional provisions to legislative events without confusing political practice with constitutional rules.

12. Quick Revision

  • Articles 79–122 deal with Parliament.
  • Articles 168–212 deal with State Legislatures.
  • Article 79 establishes Parliament as consisting of the President and two Houses.
  • Article 168 provides for the constitutional structure of State Legislatures.
  • Article 110 defines a Money Bill for Parliament; Article 199 does so for State Legislatures.
  • Article 246 distributes legislative subjects with reference to the Seventh Schedule.
  • Article 249 allows Parliament to legislate on a State List matter after a qualifying Rajya Sabha resolution.
  • Article 254 deals with repugnancy between Union and State laws on Concurrent List subjects.
  • Article 324 concerns the Election Commission of India.
  • Article 368 governs the constitutional amendment procedure.
  • The Tenth Schedule deals with disqualification on the ground of defection.

13. Check Your Understanding

1. Which Article provides for the constitution of Parliament?

Answer: Article 79.

2. Which Article provides for the creation or abolition of a State Legislative Council?

Answer: Article 169.

3. Which Article distributes legislative subjects between the Union and States?

Answer: Article 246, read with the Seventh Schedule.

4. Which Article allows Parliament to legislate on a State List subject in the national interest?

Answer: Article 249, following a qualifying resolution of Rajya Sabha.

5. Which Article deals with inconsistency between Union and State laws on Concurrent List matters?

Answer: Article 254.

6. Which Schedule contains the anti-defection provisions?

Answer: The Tenth Schedule.

7. Do State Legislatures have to ratify every constitutional amendment?

Answer: No. Ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures is required only for amendments affecting the specified federal features listed in Article 368.
