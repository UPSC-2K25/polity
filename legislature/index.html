INDIAN CONSTITUTION · PART V AND PART VI

The Legislature

How laws are made and governments are held accountable.

The legislature is the institution through which the people are represented in the law-making process. It makes laws, authorises public expenditure, debates matters of public importance and exercises control over the executive.

India's legislative system operates at two levels: Parliament at the Union level and State Legislatures at the State level. Their composition, powers and procedures are defined by the Constitution.

CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Where does the Constitution deal with legislatures?

Part V, Chapter II of the Constitution deals with Parliament. Part VI, Chapter III deals with State Legislatures. Other provisions address legislative powers, distribution of subjects, financial control and the law-making process.
