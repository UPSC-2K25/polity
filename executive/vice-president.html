UNION EXECUTIVE · ARTICLES 63–71

Vice-President of India The second-highest constitutional office and the ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

Article 63 provides that there shall be a Vice-President of India. The office performs two distinct constitutional functions: the Vice-President is the ex officio Chairman of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha), and acts as President or discharges the functions of the President when the circumstances specified in the Constitution arise.

The Vice-President is elected indirectly by an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament. Unlike the presidential electoral college, it includes both elected and nominated members of Parliament and does not include members of State Legislatures.

CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Articles 63–71

Part V, Chapter I of the Constitution deals with the Union Executive. Articles 63 to 71 establish the office of the Vice-President, prescribe the election process, qualifications, term, removal procedure and the mechanism for resolving disputes relating to the election.

The Vice-President is not a member of the Union Council of Ministers merely by virtue of holding this office. The principal regular legislative responsibility attached to the office is chairing the Rajya Sabha.

1. Constitutional position

Article 63 establishes the office of the Vice-President. The Constitution does not expressly describe the Vice-President as the "second citizen" of India; that expression is commonly used as a matter of protocol and public description.

The Vice-President occupies an important position in the Union's constitutional structure because the office links the executive framework with the functioning of the Rajya Sabha. The Vice-President ordinarily presides over the Rajya Sabha and exercises the powers and duties of its Chairman.

Two principal functions

  1. To act as the ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  2. To act as President or discharge the functions of the President in the situations specified under Article 65.

These two functions should be distinguished. When the Vice-President acts as President, the person does not simultaneously perform the duties of the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

2. Election of the Vice-President

Article 66: Electoral college

The Vice-President is elected by an electoral college consisting of the members of both Houses of Parliament. This includes:

  • Elected members of the Lok Sabha.
  • Nominated members of the Lok Sabha.
  • Elected members of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Nominated members of the Rajya Sabha.

Members of State Legislative Assemblies and Legislative Councils do not participate in the election of the Vice-President.

Method of election

The Vice-President is elected by proportional representation through the single transferable vote system. Voting takes place by secret ballot. Each elector has one vote, and preferences are marked in order.

Unlike the presidential election, the value of each elector's vote is one. There is no separate weighting of votes based on the population of States or the number of members in a State Legislative Assembly.

Why is the electoral college different from that of the President?

The Vice-President's election is linked primarily to the Union Parliament because the office has a direct constitutional relationship with the Rajya Sabha. The President, on the other hand, is the constitutional head of the entire Union and is elected through a wider electoral college representing both Parliament and the States.

3. Qualifications for election

Article 66, read with Article 84, prescribes the qualifications for election as Vice-President. A candidate must:

  1. Be a citizen of India.
  2. Have completed 35 years of age.
  3. Be qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha.
  4. Not hold any office of profit under the Government of India, the Government of any State, or any local or other authority subject to the control of these governments.

For this purpose, the offices of President, Vice-President, Governor of a State and Minister of the Union or of a State are not treated as offices of profit.

Nomination requirements

Under the Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952, a candidate must have at least 20 electors as proposers and at least 20 electors as seconders. The prescribed security deposit must also be made.

These are statutory nomination requirements. They should not be confused with the constitutional qualifications prescribed under Article 66.

4. Oath or affirmation

Under Article 69, before entering office, the Vice-President makes and subscribes to an oath or affirmation before the President, or a person appointed by the President for that purpose.

The Vice-President undertakes to bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India and faithfully discharge the duties of the office.

This is different from the oath of the President, which is administered by the Chief Justice of India or, in the Chief Justice's absence, the senior-most available Judge of the Supreme Court.

5. Term of office and conditions

Article 67: Term

The Vice-President holds office for a term of five years from the date of entering office. The Vice-President may resign by writing addressed to the President.

The Vice-President continues in office notwithstanding the expiry of the five-year term until the successor enters office. This avoids a constitutional vacuum merely because the term has ended.

Re-election

The Constitution does not prescribe a maximum number of terms for the Vice-President. A person who holds or has held the office is eligible for re-election, subject to the applicable constitutional qualifications.

Article 68: Election to fill a vacancy

An election to fill a vacancy caused by the expiry of the term must be completed before the term expires. If a vacancy arises due to death, resignation, removal or otherwise, an election must be held as soon as possible.

A person elected to fill a casual vacancy holds office for a full term of five years from the date of entering office.

6. Removal of the Vice-President

Article 67(b) provides a special procedure for removal of the Vice-President. The Constitution does not describe this procedure as impeachment. This is an important distinction from the removal of the President.

Procedure

  1. A resolution for removal must be passed by a majority of all the then members of the Rajya Sabha.
  2. The resolution must be agreed to by the Lok Sabha.
  3. At least 14 days' notice of the intention to move the resolution must be given.

The Constitution does not prescribe a specific ground that must be established for the removal of the Vice-President. The procedure nevertheless requires the prescribed majority and agreement of both Houses.

Role of the Vice-President in the Rajya Sabha

When a resolution for removal is under consideration in the Rajya Sabha, the Vice-President cannot preside over the proceedings, even though the Vice-President is ordinarily the Chairman of that House. The Vice-President may, however, be present and speak in the House and participate in its proceedings, without voting.

7. Vice-President as Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

Article 64 provides that the Vice-President shall be ex officio Chairman of the Council of States. The Vice-President is therefore the presiding officer of the Rajya Sabha by virtue of holding the constitutional office.

Major functions

  • Presides over meetings of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Maintains order and decorum in the House.
  • Interprets and applies the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business.
  • Decides points of order in accordance with parliamentary rules and practice.
  • Allows members to speak and regulates debate.
  • Puts questions to vote and announces the result.
  • Exercises disciplinary powers in accordance with the rules of the House.
  • Exercises a casting vote in the event of an equality of votes.

Voting rights

The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha is not ordinarily entitled to vote in the first instance. However, in the case of equality of votes, the Chairman exercises a casting vote.

This is different from an ordinary Rajya Sabha member, who has a vote in the normal voting process.

Deputy Chairman

The Rajya Sabha elects one of its members as Deputy Chairman under Article 89. The Deputy Chairman presides over the House when the Chairman's office is vacant or when the Vice-President is acting as President or otherwise unable to perform the duties of the Chairman.

8. Vice-President acting as President

Article 65 provides for the Vice-President to act as President or discharge the functions of the President in specified circumstances.

When the office of President becomes vacant

If a vacancy occurs in the office of President because of death, resignation, removal or otherwise, the Vice-President acts as President until a new President is elected and enters office.

When the President is temporarily unable to act

When the President is unable to discharge functions because of absence, illness or another cause, the Vice-President discharges those functions until the President resumes duties.

Rights and position during this period

While acting as President or discharging presidential functions, the Vice-President does not perform the duties of the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. During this period, the Vice-President is entitled to the emoluments, allowances and privileges prescribed for the President.

The Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha performs the duties of the Chairman during this period, subject to the constitutional and parliamentary provisions.

9. Disputes relating to election

Article 71 deals with matters relating to the election of the President and Vice-President. All doubts and disputes arising out of or in connection with the election of either office are decided by the Supreme Court.

The decision of the Supreme Court is final. If the Supreme Court declares the election of a President or Vice-President void, acts performed by that person before the date of the Court's decision are not invalidated merely because the election is subsequently declared void.

Parliament may regulate matters relating to the election of the President and Vice-President by law, subject to the constitutional framework.

CORE UNDERSTANDING

The Vice-President connects the Union Executive with the Rajya Sabha.

The office has a dual constitutional character. In ordinary circumstances, the Vice-President presides over the Rajya Sabha as its ex officio Chairman. In exceptional circumstances, the Vice-President acts as President or discharges presidential functions. The election and removal procedures are distinct from those applicable to the President.
