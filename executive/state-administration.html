State Administration

State administration is the institutional machinery through which the state government implements laws, formulates policies, delivers public services and maintains public order. It connects constitutional institutions and elected representatives with the everyday administration of the state.

The state executive consists of the Governor and the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister. Its administrative work is supported by the State Secretariat, departments, directorates, district administration and the permanent civil services.

1. Constitutional Framework of State Administration

The Constitution establishes the broad structure of state administration but leaves considerable scope for states to organise their departments and administrative machinery. The parliamentary system ensures that the political executive remains accountable to the State Legislature.

2. Executive Power of the State

Article 154 vests the executive power of the State in the Governor. The Governor may exercise this power directly or through officers subordinate to the Governor, in accordance with the Constitution.

Article 162 defines the extent of the State's executive power. Broadly, it extends to matters with respect to which the State Legislature has power to make laws. Where both Parliament and the State Legislature have legislative competence, the State's executive power is subject to the Constitution and laws made by Parliament or other competent authority.

In the ordinary parliamentary arrangement, the Governor exercises executive functions on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, except in constitutionally recognised areas of discretion.

**Important distinction:** Article 154 identifies where executive power is vested. Article 162 explains the extent of that power. Neither provision means that the Governor ordinarily governs independently of the elected Council of Ministers.

3. State Secretariat

The State Secretariat is the principal administrative headquarters of the state government. It assists the political executive in policy formulation, coordination, decision-making and supervision of government departments.

It is generally organised into departments such as Home, Finance, Education, Health, Revenue, Agriculture, Public Works and other areas determined by the state government.

Major functions

  • Assisting ministers in formulating government policies.
  • Preparing proposals for legislation and executive decisions.
  • Coordinating the work of different government departments.
  • Preparing budget proposals and monitoring expenditure.
  • Issuing government orders and administrative instructions.
  • Monitoring implementation of major programmes.
  • Maintaining records and providing administrative advice.
  • Coordinating communication between the state government and the Union Government.

Administrative hierarchy

A typical department is headed politically by a minister and administratively by a senior civil servant, often designated as the Additional Chief Secretary, Principal Secretary or Secretary, depending on the state's administrative structure.

The Secretary advises the minister, supervises departmental administration and ensures that government decisions are processed and implemented through the established system.

4. Chief Secretary

The Chief Secretary is generally the senior-most civil servant in a state and serves as the principal administrative adviser to the Chief Minister and the State Government. The office plays a central role in coordinating the state bureaucracy.

The Constitution does not expressly establish the office of Chief Secretary. Its position and functions have developed through administrative practice and the rules governing state government business.

Major functions

  • Advising the Chief Minister on important administrative matters.
  • Coordinating the functioning of state government departments.
  • Supervising interdepartmental administrative coordination.
  • Providing continuity in administration during political transitions.
  • Monitoring implementation of important government decisions.
  • Coordinating senior civil service matters, subject to applicable rules.
  • Assisting in crisis management and major administrative responses.
  • Representing the state administration in important official meetings.

The Chief Secretary's exact responsibilities and reporting arrangements may differ among states. The office is not constitutionally identical to the Cabinet Secretary at the Union level, although both have important coordinating functions.

5. Directorate and Field Administration

While the Secretariat primarily deals with policy and administrative coordination, directorates and field offices are generally responsible for technical supervision and implementation of government programmes.

For example, the Education Department may formulate policy through the Secretariat, while education directorates and field-level officers supervise schools, teachers and implementation of education schemes.

The precise division of responsibilities varies by state and department. In practice, Secretariat departments and directorates must coordinate closely to ensure that policies are implemented effectively.

6. District Administration

The district is a major unit of field administration. It acts as an important link between the state government and citizens. The District Magistrate, Collector or Deputy Commissioner commonly performs a central coordinating role, although designations and functions vary across states.

Role of the District Magistrate / Collector

  • Revenue administration and land-related responsibilities.
  • Coordination of district-level government departments.
  • Disaster management and emergency response.
  • Implementation and monitoring of government programmes.
  • Coordination of law-and-order administration with police authorities.
  • Supervision of elections and other statutory responsibilities when assigned.
  • Grievance redressal and coordination of public services.

District Magistrate and Superintendent of Police

The District Magistrate and Superintendent of Police have distinct statutory and administrative responsibilities. The District Magistrate performs specified executive-magisterial and district coordination functions, while the Superintendent of Police heads the district police administration.

Their working relationship is governed by applicable laws, state police arrangements and administrative rules. The Superintendent of Police is not simply a subordinate administrative officer of the District Magistrate for all purposes.

7. Civil Services in State Administration

Civil servants provide the permanent administrative framework through which governments function. They assist in policy formulation, maintain institutional continuity and implement decisions taken by the elected government.

State administration includes members of the All India Services serving in the state, officers of the State Civil Services and other departmental and technical services.

All India Services

Article 312 provides for the creation of All India Services. These services are common to the Union and the states and are intended to provide a national administrative framework.

The principal All India Services are:

  • Indian Administrative Service (IAS).
  • Indian Police Service (IPS).
  • Indian Forest Service (IFoS).

Members of these services are recruited through a national process and may serve in state cadres as well as in positions under the Union Government, subject to applicable service rules.

State Civil Services

State Civil Services are recruited and administered by the respective state governments under applicable constitutional and statutory provisions. Their officers serve in state departments and administrative positions.

8. Recruitment and Conditions of Service

Article 309 empowers the appropriate legislature to regulate recruitment and conditions of service of persons appointed to public services and posts in connection with the affairs of the Union or a State.

Until provision is made by the legislature, the President or Governor, as applicable, may make rules regulating recruitment and conditions of service within the constitutional framework.

State Public Service Commission

Article 315 provides for a Public Service Commission for each state, subject to the possibility of a Joint State Public Service Commission for two or more states.

Under Article 320, the State Public Service Commission performs functions including conducting examinations for appointments to state services and being consulted on specified recruitment, promotion and disciplinary matters, subject to constitutional provisions and applicable exceptions.

The Commission is an important constitutional institution supporting merit-based recruitment and professional public administration.

9. Political Executive and Permanent Executive

State administration operates through the interaction of two broad components: the political executive and the permanent executive.

Basis Political Executive Permanent Executive
Composition Chief Minister and other ministers. Civil servants and government officials.
Tenure Depends on political confidence and constitutional arrangements. Generally governed by service rules and applicable law.
Source of authority Democratic mandate and constitutional provisions. Constitution, statutes, service rules and delegated authority.
Main role Policy direction and political decision-making. Administrative advice and implementation.
Accountability Legislative and political accountability. Administrative, legal and disciplinary accountability.
Continuity May change following elections or loss of confidence. Provides continuity across changes in government.

The permanent executive is expected to provide impartial professional advice and implement lawful decisions of the elected government. The political executive is responsible for setting policy direction and answering to the legislature and the public.

10. Safeguards for Civil Servants

Article 310: Doctrine of Pleasure

Article 310 provides that members of the defence services and civil services of the Union and All India Services hold office during the pleasure of the President. Members of state civil services hold office during the pleasure of the Governor, subject to the constitutional provisions.

The doctrine is not absolute. It operates subject to constitutional safeguards, particularly Article 311.

Article 311: Protection against arbitrary dismissal

Article 311 provides safeguards to persons employed in civil capacities under the Union or a State against dismissal, removal or reduction in rank by an authority subordinate to the authority by which they were appointed.

It also generally requires an inquiry in which the government servant is informed of the charges and given a reasonable opportunity of being heard before dismissal, removal or reduction in rank.

Exceptions to the inquiry requirement

The second proviso to Article 311(2) recognises certain exceptions where a regular inquiry may be dispensed with:

  • Where the person is dismissed or removed or reduced in rank on the ground of conduct that has led to conviction on a criminal charge.
  • Where the authority empowered to dismiss or remove the person or reduce the person's rank is satisfied, for reasons to be recorded in writing, that it is not reasonably practicable to hold the inquiry.
  • Where the President or Governor, as applicable, is satisfied that holding the inquiry is not expedient in the interest of the security of the State.
**UPSC distinction:** Article 310 establishes the doctrine of pleasure. Article 311 provides procedural and constitutional safeguards against arbitrary dismissal, removal or reduction in rank.

11. State Administration and Cooperative Federalism

State administration does not function in isolation. The Union and state governments interact through constitutional provisions, centrally sponsored programmes, financial arrangements, intergovernmental institutions and administrative coordination.

The Chief Minister and senior state officials participate in important intergovernmental consultations. State administrations also coordinate with Union ministries and agencies on matters involving shared responsibilities.

The All India Services provide an institutional link between the Union and the states, while constitutional bodies and intergovernmental forums support coordination within India's federal system.

12. Administrative Accountability

Effective state administration requires accountability to the legislature, courts, independent constitutional institutions and citizens.

  • Legislative control: Questions, debates, committees, budgetary scrutiny and other legislative procedures hold the government accountable.
  • Judicial review: Courts can review administrative action for legality and constitutional compliance.
  • Audit: The Comptroller and Auditor General audits public expenditure in accordance with constitutional and statutory provisions.
  • Transparency: The Right to Information framework enables citizens to access public information, subject to legal exemptions.
  • Grievance redressal: Administrative mechanisms provide channels for citizens to raise complaints and seek remedies.

13. Important Prelims Points

  • Article 154 vests the executive power of the State in the Governor.
  • Article 162 defines the extent of the State's executive power.
  • Article 166 concerns the conduct of business of the State Government.
  • Article 167 lays down the duties of the Chief Minister towards the Governor.
  • The Constitution does not expressly establish the office of Chief Secretary.
  • Article 309 concerns recruitment and conditions of service.
  • Article 310 deals with the doctrine of pleasure.
  • Article 311 provides safeguards for civil servants.
  • Article 312 provides for the creation of All India Services.
  • Article 315 provides for Union and State Public Service Commissions.
  • Article 320 deals with the functions of Public Service Commissions.

Chapter Summary

State administration is the operational machinery of the state executive. The Governor is the constitutional head, while the elected Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister provides political direction. The State Secretariat, Chief Secretary, directorates, district administration and civil services support the formulation and implementation of government policies.

For UPSC preparation, focus on Articles 154, 162, 166, 167 and 309–320, the distinction between political and permanent executives, the role of the Chief Secretary, district administration and constitutional safeguards for civil servants.
