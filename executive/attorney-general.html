UNION EXECUTIVE · ARTICLE 76

Attorney-General of India The highest law officer of the Union Government and its principal constitutional legal adviser.

The Attorney-General of India is the chief legal adviser to the Government of India. The office is established under Article 76 of the Constitution and provides legal advice to the Union Government on constitutional, statutory and other legal matters.

The Attorney-General also performs legal duties assigned by the President and has the right to participate in parliamentary proceedings under Article 88, subject to constitutional limitations.

CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Articles 76 and 88

Article 76 establishes the office of the Attorney-General for India, specifies the qualification for appointment and provides for the duties and tenure of the office. Article 88 provides the right of the Attorney-General to speak and participate in parliamentary proceedings, without a right to vote merely by virtue of that office.

1. Constitutional position

The Attorney-General is the highest law officer of the Union. The office is a constitutional office created directly by Article 76.

The Attorney-General represents the Union Government in important legal matters and advises it on questions of law. The office plays an important role in maintaining legal consistency in government decision-making.

The Attorney-General is not a member of the Council of Ministers merely by virtue of holding this constitutional office. The office is distinct from the political executive.

2. Appointment

Article 76(1)

The President appoints a person who is qualified to be appointed as a Judge of the Supreme Court to be Attorney-General for India.

The appointment is formally made by the President. The Attorney-General serves as the principal legal adviser to the Government of India.

3. Qualifications

Article 76 requires the Attorney-General to possess the qualifications necessary for appointment as a Judge of the Supreme Court.

Under Article 124(3), a person is qualified for appointment as a Judge of the Supreme Court if the person is a citizen of India and satisfies at least one of the following conditions:

  • Has been for at least five years a Judge of a High Court or of two or more such courts in succession; or
  • Has been for at least ten years an advocate of a High Court or of two or more such courts in succession; or
  • Is, in the opinion of the President, a distinguished jurist.

Thus, the constitutional qualification for the Attorney-General is linked to the qualification prescribed for a Supreme Court judge.

4. Duties and functions

Article 76(2)

The Attorney-General has the duty to give advice to the Government of India upon legal matters and to perform such other duties of a legal character as may be referred or assigned by the President.

The Attorney-General also performs functions conferred by or under the Constitution or any other law for the time being in force.

Major functions

  • Advises the Union Government on constitutional and legal questions.
  • Represents the Government of India in legal proceedings where required.
  • Appears before the Supreme Court and other courts in matters concerning the Union.
  • Performs legal duties assigned by the President.
  • Provides legal opinions on important government policies and proposed legislation.
  • Assists the government in defending the constitutional validity of laws and executive action.

The Attorney-General's duties are primarily legal and advisory. The office does not independently determine government policy.

5. Right of audience in courts

Article 76 establishes the Attorney-General as the principal legal officer of the Union. In the performance of official duties, the Attorney-General represents the Union Government before courts.

The Attorney-General has a right of audience in all courts in the territory of India in the performance of official duties. This is also reflected in the law governing law officers and their professional functions.

6. Rights in Parliament

Article 88

Article 88 gives the Attorney-General the right to speak and otherwise take part in the proceedings of either House of Parliament, any joint sitting of Parliament and any committee of Parliament of which the Attorney-General may be named a member.

The Attorney-General does not have the right to vote in Parliament merely by virtue of holding this office.

Important distinction

Article 88 provides participation rights, not parliamentary membership. The Attorney-General is not automatically a member of either House of Parliament.

7. Tenure and removal

Article 76(4)

The Attorney-General holds office during the pleasure of the President. The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed tenure for the office.

Therefore, the Attorney-General may continue in office as long as the President's pleasure continues, subject to the constitutional framework.

Resignation

The Attorney-General may resign from office. In practice, the Attorney-General ordinarily submits a resignation when a new government assumes office, although the Constitution does not require automatic resignation after every general election.

8. Remuneration

Article 76(4) provides that the remuneration of the Attorney-General shall be such as the President may determine.

The Constitution does not specify a fixed salary amount for the office.

9. Restrictions and professional position

The Attorney-General is a constitutional legal adviser to the Union Government. The office is expected to function with professional integrity and in accordance with applicable rules governing law officers.

The Attorney-General is not a full-time government servant in the ordinary sense. The office has traditionally been permitted to undertake private legal practice, subject to restrictions and conditions applicable to the office.

These restrictions are intended to avoid conflicts between official responsibilities and private professional interests.

10. Attorney-General and Solicitor-General

The Attorney-General is the highest law officer of the Union and holds a constitutional position under Article 76. The Solicitor-General and Additional Solicitors-General assist the Attorney-General in representing the Union Government in legal matters.

11. Attorney-General and State Advocate-General

The Attorney-General is the highest law officer of the Union, while the Advocate-General is the highest law officer of a State.

CORE UNDERSTANDING

The Attorney-General is the constitutional legal adviser of the Union.

Article 76 establishes the Attorney-General as the highest law officer of India. The office combines legal advisory responsibilities, representation of the Union Government and limited participation rights in Parliament under Article 88.
