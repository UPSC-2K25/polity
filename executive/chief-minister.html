Chief Minister

The Chief Minister is the head of the elected government at the state level and the principal link between the Governor, the State Council of Ministers and the State Legislature. The office occupies a central position in India's parliamentary system of government.

The Constitution does not provide a separate, exhaustive description of the Chief Minister's powers. The position is understood through Articles 163, 164 and 167, read with the broader framework of responsible parliamentary government.

1. Constitutional Framework

The Chief Minister is the real executive head of the state government in the ordinary constitutional arrangement. The Governor is the constitutional head, while executive authority is generally exercised by the Governor on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister.

2. Appointment of the Chief Minister

Article 164(1) provides that the Chief Minister shall be appointed by the Governor. Other ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister.

In a parliamentary system, the Governor ordinarily appoints the person who commands the confidence of the Legislative Assembly. In normal circumstances, this is the leader of the majority party or coalition in the Assembly.

Appointment in different situations

  • Clear majority: The Governor ordinarily appoints the leader of the party or coalition that has a majority in the Legislative Assembly.
  • Hung Assembly: Where no party has a clear majority, the Governor must identify the person most likely to command the confidence of the House.
  • Coalition government: A coalition leader may be appointed if the Governor is satisfied that the leader can demonstrate majority support.
  • Loss of majority: If a serious and credible doubt arises about the government's majority, the appropriate test is ordinarily a floor test in the Legislative Assembly.

The Governor's discretion in appointing a Chief Minister in a hung Assembly is not unlimited. Constitutional conventions, democratic legitimacy and the requirement of proving majority support guide the decision.

Is there a prescribed oath?

Under Article 164(3), before entering office, the Chief Minister and other ministers must take the oaths of office and secrecy administered by the Governor or a person appointed by the Governor. The forms of oath are contained in the Third Schedule.

3. Qualifications and Membership of the Legislature

The Constitution does not prescribe a separate list of qualifications exclusively for the office of Chief Minister. A person must be qualified to become a member of the relevant State Legislature.

  • For the Legislative Assembly, the minimum age is 25 years.
  • For the Legislative Council, the minimum age is 30 years.
  • The person must satisfy the applicable constitutional and statutory qualifications.

Six-month rule

A person who is not a member of the State Legislature may be appointed Chief Minister. However, under Article 164(4), the person must become a member of the State Legislature within six consecutive months. Otherwise, the person ceases to be a minister at the end of that period.

The rule allows a person who has not yet been elected or nominated to enter the ministry temporarily, but it does not create a permanent exemption from legislative membership.

Important distinction: Article 164(4) applies to ministers, including the Chief Minister. It does not mean that a non-member can continue indefinitely in executive office without obtaining membership of the State Legislature.

4. Tenure and Removal

The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed five-year tenure for the Chief Minister personally. The Chief Minister normally remains in office as long as the person enjoys the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.

Article 164(1) states that ministers hold office during the pleasure of the Governor. In the parliamentary system, this provision operates subject to the principle of collective responsibility to the Legislative Assembly under Article 164(2).

When can the Chief Minister leave office?

  • Resignation of the Chief Minister.
  • Loss of confidence in the Legislative Assembly.
  • Defeat in a confidence motion or on an issue treated as a matter of confidence.
  • Failure to prove majority support in a constitutionally required floor test.
  • Death or other circumstances resulting in the office becoming vacant.

If the Chief Minister resigns, the Council of Ministers headed by that Chief Minister ordinarily goes out of office as well. The Governor then appoints a new Chief Minister in accordance with parliamentary principles.

The Governor cannot ordinarily dismiss a Chief Minister who continues to enjoy the confidence of the Legislative Assembly merely because of political disagreement.

5. Powers and Functions of the Chief Minister

A. Formation of the Council of Ministers

The Chief Minister recommends the names of persons to be appointed as ministers. Under Article 164(1), the Governor appoints the other ministers on the advice of the Chief Minister.

The Chief Minister also allocates portfolios, coordinates the work of different ministries and may recommend changes in ministerial responsibilities.

B. Leadership of the Council of Ministers

The Chief Minister presides over Cabinet meetings, coordinates government policy and provides overall political direction to the Council of Ministers.

Differences among ministers are ordinarily resolved through collective discussion and Cabinet decision-making. The Chief Minister plays a central role in maintaining unity and coherence within the government.

C. Link between the Governor and the Council of Ministers

The Chief Minister is the principal constitutional channel through which the Governor receives information about the administration and decisions of the Council of Ministers.

D. Leadership in the State Legislature

The Chief Minister normally leads the government in the Legislative Assembly, explains government policy, coordinates legislative business and ensures that the ministry retains the confidence of the House.

The Chief Minister may advise the Governor regarding the summoning and prorogation of the State Legislature and dissolution of the Legislative Assembly, subject to constitutional limitations.

E. Policy and Administration

The Chief Minister provides political leadership in formulating and implementing state policies. The office coordinates major programmes relating to development, public order, welfare, infrastructure and administration.

F. Role in Intergovernmental Relations

The Chief Minister represents the state in important discussions with the Union Government and other states. The office also plays a significant role in cooperative federal institutions and inter-state consultations.

6. Duties under Article 167

Article 167 expressly lays down the duties of the Chief Minister towards the Governor. These duties are important because they maintain a constitutional communication channel between the formal head of the state and the elected government.

The Chief Minister must:

  1. Communicate to the Governor all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the affairs of the state and proposals for legislation.
  2. Furnish information relating to the administration of the affairs of the state and proposals for legislation as the Governor may call for.
  3. If the Governor so requires, submit for consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by an individual minister but which has not been considered by the Council.
**UPSC focus:** Article 167 is specifically concerned with the Chief Minister's duties towards the Governor. It should not be confused with Article 78, which lays down corresponding duties of the Prime Minister towards the President.

7. Chief Minister and the State Council of Ministers

The Chief Minister heads the State Council of Ministers. The Council functions on the principle of collective responsibility to the Legislative Assembly under Article 164(2).

The Council ordinarily includes Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State and other categories of ministers as determined by the political and administrative structure of the government.

Collective responsibility

Collective responsibility means that the Council of Ministers functions as a unified body and is collectively answerable to the Legislative Assembly. If the government loses the confidence of the Assembly, the Council as a whole must ordinarily resign.

Individual ministers may have separate portfolios, but they are expected to support Cabinet decisions publicly. A minister who cannot accept a fundamental government decision may be expected to resign.

Individual responsibility

Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the Governor. In the parliamentary arrangement, the Chief Minister's advice is ordinarily central to the appointment and continuation of other ministers. The Chief Minister may seek the removal of a minister or recommend a change in the ministerial team.

8. Constitutional Limits on the Size of the Ministry

The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 inserted Article 164(1A), placing a constitutional limit on the size of the Council of Ministers in states.

  • The total number of ministers, including the Chief Minister, must not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Legislative Assembly.
  • The number of ministers, including the Chief Minister, must not be less than 12.

These limits are intended to prevent excessively large ministries and promote administrative discipline.

Disqualification under Article 164(1B)

Article 164(1B), inserted by the 91st Amendment, provides that a member of a State Legislature who is disqualified under the Tenth Schedule on the ground of defection is also disqualified from being appointed as a minister for the constitutionally specified period.

The disqualification generally continues from the date of disqualification until the date on which the member's term would expire or the member is re-elected, whichever is earlier.

**Remember:** Article 164(1A) concerns the size of the State Council of Ministers. Article 164(1B) concerns ministerial disqualification linked to defection.

9. Chief Minister and Governor: Relationship

The Governor is the constitutional head of the state, whereas the Chief Minister leads the elected government. Their relationship is governed by the principles of parliamentary democracy, federalism and constitutional responsibility.

Under Article 163, the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister aids and advises the Governor, except in matters where the Constitution requires the Governor to exercise discretion.

The Governor is not expected to function as an independent political executive. In ordinary matters, the Governor acts according to ministerial advice.

Important areas of interaction

  • Appointment of ministers on the advice of the Chief Minister.
  • Communication of Cabinet decisions under Article 167.
  • Summoning and prorogation of the State Legislature.
  • Consideration of Bills passed by the State Legislature.
  • Seeking information about state administration.
  • Constitutional situations involving uncertainty about legislative majority.

A dispute concerning the government's majority should ordinarily be resolved through a floor test in the Legislative Assembly, rather than through subjective political assessments.

10. Important Supreme Court Judgments

1. Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)

The Supreme Court held that the President and Governor are constitutional heads who ordinarily exercise their powers on the aid and advice of their respective Councils of Ministers, except where the Constitution provides otherwise.

Significance: This judgment is fundamental to understanding the constitutional position of the Governor and the real executive authority of the elected government.

2. S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

The Supreme Court emphasised the importance of floor tests in determining whether a government enjoys the confidence of the Legislative Assembly. It also strengthened the constitutional safeguards surrounding the use of Article 356.

Significance: Legislative majority should ordinarily be demonstrated on the floor of the House.

3. Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006)

The Supreme Court examined the dissolution of the Bihar Legislative Assembly and held that the constitutional action in that case was legally unsustainable.

Significance: Constitutional authorities cannot act on speculative or unsupported assumptions about political developments.

4. S.R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001)

The Supreme Court considered the appointment of a person as minister who was not a member of the legislature and the constitutional six-month limit under Article 164(4).

Significance: The six-month provision cannot be used to repeatedly reappoint a non-member as minister without obtaining legislative membership.

12. Important Prelims Distinctions

  • The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor under Article 164(1).
  • Other ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister.
  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly under Article 164(2).
  • Article 167 specifies the duties of the Chief Minister towards the Governor.
  • A non-member may be appointed Chief Minister, but must obtain membership of the State Legislature within six months.
  • The Chief Minister does not have a fixed personal five-year term independent of the confidence of the Assembly.
  • Article 164(1A) limits the size of the State Council of Ministers.
  • A floor test is the normal constitutional method of establishing majority support in the Legislative Assembly.

Chapter Summary

The Chief Minister is the central figure in the state parliamentary executive. The office derives its authority from the confidence of the Legislative Assembly and its constitutional position as head of the State Council of Ministers. Articles 163, 164 and 167 form the core constitutional framework governing the office.

For UPSC preparation, special attention should be given to the appointment of the Chief Minister in a hung Assembly, the six-month membership rule, collective responsibility, the 91st Amendment, the duties under Article 167 and the constitutional relationship between the Governor and the elected government.
