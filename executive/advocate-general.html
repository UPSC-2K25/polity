Advocate-General of the State

The Advocate-General is the highest law officer of a state government. The office is established under Article 165 of the Constitution and provides legal advice to the state government on constitutional, statutory and administrative matters.

The Advocate-General represents the state in legal proceedings and performs duties of a legal character assigned by the Governor. The office is the state-level counterpart of the Attorney-General of India.

1. Constitutional Basis: Article 165

Article 165 provides for the appointment of an Advocate-General for each state. The Governor appoints a person who is qualified to be appointed as a Judge of a High Court.

The Advocate-General holds office during the pleasure of the Governor and receives remuneration determined by the Governor.

2. Appointment

Under Article 165(1), the Governor appoints the Advocate-General. The appointment is made by the Governor in the constitutional framework of the state executive.

The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed term of office. The Advocate-General continues during the pleasure of the Governor, subject to the constitutional arrangement.

3. Qualifications

A person appointed as Advocate-General must be qualified to be appointed as a Judge of a High Court.

Under Article 217(2), a person is qualified for appointment as a High Court Judge if the person:

  • Is a citizen of India; and
  • Has for at least ten years held a judicial office in the territory of India; or
  • Has for at least ten years been an advocate of a High Court or of two or more such courts in succession.

The constitutional qualification for the Advocate-General is therefore linked to the qualification for appointment as a High Court Judge.

4. Tenure and Remuneration

Article 165(3) provides that the Advocate-General holds office during the pleasure of the Governor and receives such remuneration as the Governor may determine.

  • The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed tenure.
  • There is no constitutionally fixed retirement age for the office.
  • The remuneration is determined by the Governor.
  • The office is not a permanent civil service post.

The Advocate-General may resign from office. The office may also become vacant when the Governor withdraws the appointment in accordance with the constitutional framework.

5. Duties and Functions

Article 165(2) provides that it is the duty of the Advocate-General to give advice to the Government of the State upon legal matters and to perform such other duties of a legal character as may from time to time be referred or assigned by the Governor.

A. Legal advice to the State Government

The Advocate-General advises the state government on constitutional interpretation, statutory provisions, administrative decisions, litigation strategy and other legal matters referred to the office.

B. Representation of the State

The Advocate-General represents or appears on behalf of the state government in legal proceedings, subject to applicable laws, professional rules and the arrangements governing government litigation.

C. Legal scrutiny

The Advocate-General may examine the legal validity of proposed government actions, draft legislation, rules, notifications and administrative decisions.

D. Duties assigned by the Governor

The Advocate-General performs other legal duties referred or assigned by the Governor under Article 165(2).

E. Constitutional and legislative assistance

The Advocate-General may assist the state government in matters involving constitutional questions and may participate in legislative proceedings within the limits of Article 177.

6. Rights in the State Legislature: Article 177

Article 177 provides that every minister and the Advocate-General of the State has the right to speak in, and otherwise take part in the proceedings of, the Legislative Assembly and, where the state has a Legislative Council, either House.

The Advocate-General may also participate in the proceedings of a committee of the Legislature of which the Advocate-General may be named a member.

However, the Advocate-General does not have the right to vote merely by virtue of this constitutional position.

**Important for Prelims:** Article 177 grants the Advocate-General the right to speak and participate in legislative proceedings, but does not confer voting rights.

7. Rights of the Advocate-General in Courts

Article 165(2) gives the Advocate-General the right of audience in any court in the state in the performance of official duties.

This enables the Advocate-General to appear and present the state's legal position before courts within the state, subject to the applicable legal framework.

8. Independence and Limitations

The Advocate-General is the highest law officer of the state, but the office is not a constitutional court or an independent adjudicatory institution. Its principal role is to advise and represent the state government.

The Advocate-General must comply with professional standards governing legal practice. The office must also function within the constitutional framework and applicable rules of professional conduct.

The Constitution does not expressly establish a separate fixed-term security of tenure for the Advocate-General. Article 165(3) specifically provides for tenure during the pleasure of the Governor.

10. Advocate-General and Other Law Officers

The Advocate-General is the highest constitutional law officer of the state. Other government law officers may assist in representing the state and conducting government litigation.

The titles, appointment arrangements and functions of these officers may differ between states. They may include senior government advocates, public prosecutors and law officers attached to government departments.

The Advocate-General's constitutional status under Article 165 distinguishes the office from other legal positions created through administrative arrangements or state legislation.

11. Important Prelims Points

  • Article 165 provides for the Advocate-General of the State.
  • The Governor appoints the Advocate-General.
  • The appointee must be qualified to be a High Court Judge.
  • The Advocate-General holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.
  • Remuneration is determined by the Governor.
  • Article 165(2) specifies the duties of the Advocate-General.
  • Article 177 provides the right to participate in legislative proceedings.
  • The Advocate-General has no voting right merely by virtue of the office.
  • Article 217(2) contains the relevant qualification framework for High Court Judges.
  • The Attorney-General of India is provided for under Article 76.

Chapter Summary

The Advocate-General is the highest law officer of a state and is appointed by the Governor under Article 165. The office provides legal advice to the state government, represents the state in legal proceedings and performs other legal duties assigned within the constitutional framework.

For UPSC preparation, focus on Article 165, qualifications under Article 217(2), tenure, remuneration, the right of audience and the distinction between Articles 165 and 177. A direct comparison with the Attorney-General under Articles 76 and 88 is especially important.
