Executive: Constitutional Provisions

This chapter provides an article-wise reference to the constitutional provisions governing the Union and State Executives. It brings together the provisions relating to the President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, Governor, Chief Minister, Advocate-General and civil services.

The provisions are arranged by constitutional sequence and subject so that they can be used for revision, comparison and quick reference during UPSC preparation.

1. Union Executive: Articles 52–78

Part V, Chapter I of the Constitution deals with the Union Executive. It establishes the offices of the President and Vice-President and provides for the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.

6. Important Amendments Affecting the Executive

42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976

Made it explicit in Article 74 that the President shall act in accordance with the advice of the Council of Ministers. It also introduced other major constitutional changes.

44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978

Modified Article 74 to allow the President to require the Council of Ministers to reconsider its advice. The President must act in accordance with the advice tendered after reconsideration.

52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985

Introduced the Tenth Schedule, establishing the anti-defection framework, with consequences for legislative membership and eligibility for ministerial office.

91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003

Limited the size of the Union Council of Ministers to 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha and introduced a corresponding limit for State Councils of Ministers. It also strengthened provisions relating to disqualification on the ground of defection.

7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956

Made significant changes to the constitutional framework of states and Union Territories, including provisions relating to Governors and the administration of states.

How to Use This Reference

Read the individual chapters for conceptual understanding and use this article-wise reference for revision. Pay particular attention to the corresponding Union and State provisions, constitutional amendments and distinctions between powers that appear similar but operate under different limitations.

For examination purposes, always read the exact constitutional text alongside the relevant case law. Article summaries are revision aids and should not replace the constitutional provisions themselves.
