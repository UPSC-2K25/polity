UNION EXECUTIVE · ARTICLES 74, 75 AND 78

Prime Minister of India The head of the Union Council of Ministers and the central figure of parliamentary government.

The Prime Minister is the head of the Union Council of Ministers and the principal political executive authority in the Indian parliamentary system. The Prime Minister leads the government, coordinates the work of ministries, advises the President on ministerial appointments and provides political direction to national administration.

Although the Constitution does not provide a separate detailed chapter exclusively on the Prime Minister, Articles 74, 75 and 78 establish the essential constitutional framework for the office. The Prime Minister's position is also shaped by parliamentary conventions and the functioning of the Cabinet system.

CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Articles 74, 75 and 78

Article 74 provides for a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at its head to aid and advise the President. Article 75 deals with the appointment, tenure, responsibility and other conditions relating to ministers. Article 78 prescribes the duties of the Prime Minister in relation to the President.

Together, these provisions make the Prime Minister the central link between the President, the Council of Ministers, Parliament and the administration of the Union Government.

1. Constitutional position of the Prime Minister

India follows the parliamentary system of government, in which the executive is drawn from the legislature and remains politically responsible to the elected House.

The President is the constitutional head of the Union, while the Prime Minister leads the real political executive. The Prime Minister exercises leadership over the Council of Ministers and is responsible for coordinating government policy and administration.

Why is the Prime Minister considered the real executive?

The President generally acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers. The Prime Minister heads that Council and plays the principal role in determining its composition, priorities and functioning.

The Prime Minister also serves as the main channel of communication between the Council of Ministers and the President under Article 78. This gives the office a central constitutional position within the Union Executive.

Constitutional and political dimensions

The Prime Minister's authority has both constitutional and political dimensions. Constitutionally, the office is linked to Articles 74, 75 and 78. Politically, the strength of the Prime Minister depends on factors such as parliamentary majority, party leadership, coalition arrangements and political circumstances.

2. Appointment of the Prime Minister

Article 75(1)

The Prime Minister is appointed by the President. Other ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.

The Constitution does not prescribe a detailed procedure for selecting the Prime Minister in every possible political situation. Parliamentary conventions and the requirement of majority support in the Lok Sabha guide the appointment.

When a political party has a clear majority

When a single political party obtains a clear majority in the Lok Sabha, the President ordinarily appoints the leader of that party as Prime Minister.

When no party obtains a clear majority

In a hung Parliament, the President appoints a person who is most likely to command the confidence of the Lok Sabha. This may be the leader of a coalition or a person supported by several parties.

The appointee may be required to demonstrate majority support through a floor test in the Lok Sabha. The floor of the House is the constitutionally appropriate forum for establishing confidence.

Can a person who is not a member of Parliament become Prime Minister?

Yes. The Constitution does not require a person to be a member of either House of Parliament at the moment of appointment as Prime Minister. However, under Article 75(5), a minister who is not a member of either House for six consecutive months ceases to be a minister at the end of that period.

This rule applies to the Prime Minister as a member of the Council of Ministers. Therefore, a non-member appointed Prime Minister must become a member of either House of Parliament within six months.

3. Qualifications and membership

The Constitution does not provide a separate list of qualifications specifically for the Prime Minister. Since the Prime Minister is a member of the Council of Ministers under Article 75, the relevant constitutional provisions governing ministerial membership and parliamentary qualifications apply.

A person must satisfy the relevant qualifications for membership of the Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha, depending on the House of which the person is a member.

A Prime Minister may belong to either the Lok Sabha or the Rajya Sabha. However, the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha under Article 75(3).

4. Oath and term of office

Article 75(4): Oath

Before entering office, the Prime Minister and other ministers take the oath of office and secrecy before the President. The forms of oath are set out in the Third Schedule of the Constitution.

Article 75(2): Pleasure of the President

Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President. In a parliamentary system, this provision must be understood alongside the collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the Lok Sabha.

The Prime Minister ordinarily remains in office as long as the person enjoys the confidence of the Lok Sabha. If the government loses that confidence, it is expected to resign or seek a fresh mandate in accordance with constitutional practice.

No fixed five-year personal tenure

The Prime Minister does not have an independent fixed five-year term. The duration of the government is connected with the life of the Lok Sabha and the government's ability to retain majority support.

5. Powers and functions of the Prime Minister

The Prime Minister's powers arise from constitutional provisions, parliamentary conventions and the leadership role attached to the office.

A. In relation to the Council of Ministers

  • Recommends persons for appointment as ministers.
  • Allocates and reshuffles ministerial portfolios.
  • Coordinates the functioning of different ministries.
  • Provides political leadership to the Council of Ministers.
  • Chairs Cabinet meetings and guides collective decision-making.
  • May advise the resignation or removal of a minister.
  • Plays a central role in maintaining collective responsibility.

The Prime Minister's position is especially important because the Council of Ministers functions as a collective body. The Prime Minister provides overall direction and seeks to maintain coordination among ministers with different portfolios.

B. In relation to the President

Article 78 establishes the Prime Minister's constitutional duties towards the President. These include:

  1. Communicating to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of Union affairs and proposals for legislation.
  2. Furnishing information relating to the administration of Union affairs and proposals for legislation when the President calls for it.
  3. Submitting for consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a minister has taken a decision but which has not been considered by the Council, if the President so requires.

Article 78 ensures that the President remains constitutionally informed about the administration of the Union Government.

C. In relation to Parliament

  • Leads the government in Parliament.
  • Explains major government policies and decisions.
  • Coordinates legislative priorities of the government.
  • Plays a major role in determining the government's legislative agenda.
  • Represents the government in important parliamentary debates.
  • Advises the President on summoning and proroguing Parliament and, where appropriate, dissolution of the Lok Sabha.

The Prime Minister's ability to secure parliamentary support is essential for the continued survival of the government.

D. In relation to national administration

  • Provides overall political direction to the Union administration.
  • Coordinates the work of central ministries and departments.
  • Supervises major policy initiatives through the Cabinet system.
  • Represents the government in major national and international matters.
  • Plays a central role in crisis management and inter-ministerial coordination.

E. In relation to foreign affairs

The Prime Minister plays an important political role in foreign policy. The office represents India's political leadership in international summits, bilateral discussions and multilateral forums. Formal diplomatic acts are undertaken within the constitutional and administrative framework of the Union.

6. Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers

The Prime Minister heads the Council of Ministers, which is the central constitutional institution of the parliamentary executive.

Article 74 provides for the Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at its head to aid and advise the President. Article 75 deals with the appointment and responsibility of ministers.

Collective responsibility

Article 75(3) provides that the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

This means that ministers stand or fall together as a political team. If the government loses the confidence of the Lok Sabha, the Council of Ministers is expected to resign collectively.

Individual responsibility

Under Article 75(2), ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President. In parliamentary practice, the President ordinarily acts on the advice of the Prime Minister in matters relating to the continuation of individual ministers.

Cabinet leadership

The Cabinet is the smaller and central decision-making body within the wider Council of Ministers. The Prime Minister chairs Cabinet meetings and coordinates its decisions.

The Cabinet provides political direction to the government, considers major policy matters and coordinates the work of important ministries.

7. Relationship between the Prime Minister and President

The relationship between the Prime Minister and President is central to the functioning of India's parliamentary system. The President is the constitutional head, while the Prime Minister leads the political executive.

Can the President ask the Prime Minister for information?

Yes. Article 78 requires the Prime Minister to communicate specified decisions and furnish information to the President. The President may also require a matter decided by an individual minister to be placed before the Council of Ministers for consideration.

8. Prime Minister and the Lok Sabha

The Prime Minister may be a member of either House of Parliament, but the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

This is one of the defining features of the Indian parliamentary system. The government must maintain the confidence of the popularly elected House to continue in office.

What happens when the government loses a majority?

If the government loses a vote of confidence or a motion of no-confidence is carried against it, the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers are ordinarily expected to resign.

Where the position of majority support is uncertain, a floor test is the appropriate means of establishing whether the government commands the confidence of the House.

9. Deputy Prime Minister

The Constitution does not create a separate constitutional office of Deputy Prime Minister. The position has existed as a political arrangement in different governments.

A Deputy Prime Minister remains a minister under the constitutional framework. The designation does not independently confer constitutional powers or a separate succession right to the office of Prime Minister.

10. Prime Minister and parliamentary conventions

Several important aspects of the Prime Minister's office are shaped by parliamentary conventions rather than by detailed constitutional text.

  • The leader who can command the confidence of the Lok Sabha is ordinarily appointed Prime Minister.
  • The Prime Minister normally resigns when the government loses the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
  • The Prime Minister coordinates the collective functioning of the Council of Ministers.
  • The Prime Minister ordinarily advises the President on the appointment and removal of ministers.
  • The Prime Minister is the principal political spokesperson for the Union Government.

These conventions operate within the framework of the Constitution and cannot override express constitutional provisions.

11. Important Supreme Court judgments

Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)

The Supreme Court explained that the President and Governor are constitutional heads who ordinarily exercise their powers on the aid and advice of their respective Councils of Ministers, subject to constitutionally recognised exceptions.

S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

The Supreme Court emphasised the importance of testing legislative majority on the floor of the House rather than relying on subjective assessments outside the legislature. This principle is important in understanding parliamentary confidence.

Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006)

The Supreme Court examined the constitutional limits on the use of Article 356 and emphasised constitutional standards in matters relating to the formation and functioning of State Governments.

CORE UNDERSTANDING

The Prime Minister is the central political authority in the Union parliamentary executive.

The Prime Minister leads the Council of Ministers, coordinates government policy, maintains the relationship between the executive and Parliament and communicates the decisions of the government to the President. The office is sustained by parliamentary confidence and operates within the constitutional framework of collective responsibility.
