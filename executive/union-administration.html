UNION EXECUTIVE · ARTICLES 77 AND 78

Union Administration Organisation, coordination and functioning of the Government of India.

The Union Government operates through a network of ministries, departments, offices and administrative institutions. These institutions translate political decisions into government policies, programmes and public services.

While the President is the formal constitutional head and the Prime Minister leads the political executive, the permanent administrative machinery provides continuity, expertise and implementation capacity to the Union Government.

CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Articles 77 and 78

Article 77 deals with the conduct of business of the Government of India. Article 78 specifies the duties of the Prime Minister in relation to furnishing information to the President, communicating decisions of the Council of Ministers and submitting certain matters for consideration.

1. Organisation of the Union Government

The Government of India is organised into ministries and departments, each responsible for a particular area of public administration. Examples include finance, defence, home affairs, external affairs, education and health.

A ministry is generally headed politically by a minister and administratively by a senior civil servant, commonly the Secretary to the Government of India. The minister provides political leadership and is accountable to Parliament, while the civil service assists in policy formulation, administration and implementation.

Broad structure

  • President: Formal constitutional head of the Union.
  • Prime Minister: Head of the Council of Ministers and principal political executive leader.
  • Council of Ministers: Political executive responsible for government policy and administration.
  • Cabinet: Principal collective decision-making body of senior ministers.
  • Ministries and departments: Organisational units responsible for specific subjects.
  • Civil services: Permanent administrative machinery that supports governance and implementation.

2. Article 77: Conduct of business of the Government of India

Article 77 provides the constitutional framework for the conduct of government business in the name of the President. It contains provisions relating to authentication of executive orders and allocation of government business.

Article 77(1): Executive action in the name of the President

All executive action of the Government of India shall be expressed to be taken in the name of the President.

This reflects the constitutional position of the President as the formal head of the Union executive. It does not mean that the President personally makes every administrative decision. Executive government functions through the Council of Ministers and the administrative machinery.

Article 77(2): Authentication of orders

Orders and other instruments made and executed in the name of the President shall be authenticated in the manner specified by rules made by the President.

Once an order is properly authenticated in accordance with the prescribed rules, its validity cannot be questioned on the ground that it was not made or executed by the President personally.

Article 77(3): Rules for transaction of business

The President makes rules for the more convenient transaction of the business of the Government of India and for the allocation of such business among ministers.

These rules provide the administrative framework through which government responsibilities are distributed, coordinated and processed.

3. Article 78: Duties of the Prime Minister

Article 78 lays down the duties of the Prime Minister in relation to the President. It establishes an important constitutional channel of communication between the formal head of the Union and the political executive.

Three principal duties

1. Communication of decisions:

The Prime Minister must communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation.

2. Furnishing information:

The Prime Minister must furnish such information relating to the administration of Union affairs and proposals for legislation as the President may call for.

3. Submission of matters for consideration:

If the President so requires, the Prime Minister must submit for consideration by the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by an individual minister but which has not been considered by the Council.

These duties help ensure that the President remains informed about important government decisions and that significant matters can receive collective consideration where required.

4. Rules of Business

The transaction of government business is regulated through rules framed under Article 77(3). These are commonly known as the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules and the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules.

Allocation of Business Rules

These rules distribute subjects among the ministries and departments of the Union Government. They clarify which ministry is responsible for a particular field of administration.

Transaction of Business Rules

These rules regulate how government business is processed and how decisions are taken. They also identify matters that must be submitted to the Prime Minister, Cabinet or other designated authorities.

Rules Primary purpose
Allocation of Business Rules Distribute subjects and responsibilities among ministries and departments.
Transaction of Business Rules Regulate the processing, consultation and decision-making of government business.

5. Ministries and departments

Ministries and departments form the principal organisational units of the Union Government. Their structure enables the government to divide complex administrative responsibilities into manageable areas.

Ministry

A ministry is a major administrative organisation responsible for a broad subject or group of related subjects. It is generally headed politically by a minister.

Department

A department is an organisational unit within a ministry or, where separately structured, an administrative unit assigned specific functions. Departments help divide the work of a ministry into specialised areas.

Secretary to the Government of India

The Secretary is ordinarily the senior-most civil servant in a ministry or department. The Secretary provides administrative leadership, advises the minister, coordinates departmental work and ensures implementation of government decisions.

The Secretary is not a political minister. The distinction between political executive and permanent executive is fundamental to parliamentary administration.

6. Cabinet Secretariat

The Cabinet Secretariat is an important institution for coordination within the Union Government. It functions under the direct charge of the Prime Minister.

It assists the Cabinet and Cabinet Committees in conducting their business and promotes coordination among ministries and departments.

Major functions

  • Provides secretarial assistance to the Cabinet and Cabinet Committees.
  • Prepares and circulates Cabinet agendas and related documents.
  • Records Cabinet decisions and communicates them to concerned ministries.
  • Monitors implementation of important Cabinet decisions.
  • Facilitates coordination among ministries and departments.
  • Supports inter-ministerial consultation on important policy matters.

Cabinet Secretary

The Cabinet Secretary is the senior-most civil servant in the Central Government and heads the Cabinet Secretariat. The office plays an important role in administrative coordination and in supporting the functioning of the Cabinet system.

The Cabinet Secretary is a civil servant, not a member of the Council of Ministers merely by virtue of holding this office.

7. Central Secretariat

The Central Secretariat consists of the ministries and departments of the Government of India and their supporting administrative structures.

It assists the political executive in policy formulation, legislative work, coordination, budgeting, administration and supervision of government programmes.

Principal functions

  • Assisting ministers in policy formulation.
  • Preparing legislative and policy proposals.
  • Coordinating administrative work across departments.
  • Preparing budgets and monitoring expenditure.
  • Providing information and advice to the political executive.
  • Monitoring implementation of government decisions.

Central Secretariat and field administration

The Central Secretariat primarily performs policy, coordination and supervisory functions. Field organisations and attached or subordinate offices often undertake implementation, technical and operational responsibilities.

9. Political executive and permanent executive

A central feature of modern parliamentary administration is the distinction between the political executive and the permanent executive.

Political executive

The political executive consists of the Prime Minister and ministers. They provide political direction, determine government priorities and are accountable to Parliament and ultimately to the electorate.

Permanent executive

The permanent executive consists of civil servants and other career public officials. They provide administrative expertise, continuity and institutional knowledge.

Political executive Permanent executive
Usually consists of elected political leaders and appointed ministers. Consists of career public officials.
Changes with political circumstances and elections. Provides continuity across successive governments.
Determines political priorities and policy direction. Provides advice and implements approved policies.
Politically accountable to Parliament. Administratively accountable under applicable service and legal rules.

The distinction does not mean that civil servants independently determine the political direction of government. They advise and assist the elected executive while carrying out their administrative responsibilities within the constitutional framework.

10. Civil services and administrative continuity

The civil services provide the institutional capacity required for the functioning of the Union Government. They support policy formulation, administration, regulation and delivery of public services.

All India Services and Central Civil Services form important components of the public administration system.

The constitutional framework relating to services is primarily contained in Part XIV of the Constitution, particularly Articles 308–323.

The detailed constitutional position of the Union Public Service Commission and the All India Services is dealt with separately in the relevant chapters of Indian Polity.

11. Parliamentary control over Union administration

The Union administration functions under the principle of parliamentary responsibility. Ministries are answerable to Parliament for their policies, decisions and administrative performance.

Major instruments of control

  • Question Hour: Members ask ministers questions about government actions and policies.
  • Zero Hour: Members raise matters of public importance under parliamentary practice.
  • Debates: Parliament discusses government policies and administrative performance.
  • Budgetary control: Parliament examines demands for grants and authorises public expenditure.
  • Parliamentary committees: Committees scrutinise government departments, expenditure and policy matters.
  • No-confidence motion: The Lok Sabha can withdraw its confidence from the Council of Ministers.

Parliamentary control ensures that administrative authority is exercised within the framework of democratic accountability.

13. UPSC examination: Important distinctions

All executive action is personally taken by the President. It is formally expressed in the President's name under Article 77.
Article 77(3) deals with appointment of ministers. It deals with rules for transaction of government business and allocation among ministers.
Article 78 concerns duties of the President. It specifies duties of the Prime Minister in relation to the President.
Cabinet Secretariat and Central Secretariat are identical. The Cabinet Secretariat is a specialised coordinating institution; the Central Secretariat encompasses ministries and departments.

Ministers and civil servants belong to the same executive category. Ministers form the political executive; civil servants form the permanent executive.
Article 78 allows the President to independently run ministries. It establishes the Prime Minister's duties of communication and information to the President.
CORE UNDERSTANDING

Political direction and permanent administration work together.

Articles 77 and 78 establish the constitutional framework for conducting Union Government business and maintaining communication between the Prime Minister and the President. Ministries, departments, the Cabinet Secretariat and the civil services provide the institutional machinery through which government decisions are administered.
