The Executive

The Executive is the branch of government responsible for implementing laws, administering public affairs and carrying out the policies of the State. The Indian Constitution establishes a parliamentary system in which the executive is formally headed by the President at the Union level and the Governor at the State level, while the real executive authority is exercised by the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister or Chief Minister.

Constitutional Framework

The Union Executive is primarily dealt with in Part V of the Constitution, particularly Articles 52 to 78. The State Executive is dealt with in Part VI, particularly Articles 153 to 167.

India follows the parliamentary form of government. The President and Governor are constitutional heads, while the Prime Minister and Chief Minister lead the respective Councils of Ministers and exercise executive functions in accordance with the constitutional scheme.

Important Conceptual Distinction

The constitutional head and the real executive must not be confused. Under the parliamentary system, executive action is formally expressed in the name of the President or Governor, but the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister or Chief Minister ordinarily exercises the real executive authority and is collectively responsible to the elected House of the legislature.
