INDIAN CONSTITUTION · PART V AND PART VI

The Executive How constitutional authority is exercised and government is administered.

The executive is the branch of government responsible for implementing laws, administering public affairs and carrying out government policies. It translates legislative decisions into administrative action and is responsible for the day-to-day governance of the country.

India's constitutional system has an executive at both the Union and State levels. At the Union level, the President is the constitutional head, while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise political leadership and aid and advise the President. At the State level, a corresponding arrangement exists between the Governor and the Chief Minister-led Council of Ministers.

CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Where does the Constitution deal with the Executive?

Part V, Chapter I (Articles 52–78) deals with the Union Executive. It covers the President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers and Attorney-General of India. Part VI, Chapter II (Articles 153–167) deals with the State Executive, including the Governor, Council of Ministers and Advocate-General of the State.

The constitutional framework establishes parliamentary government at both levels. The nominal executive head generally acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, which is collectively responsible to the elected House of the legislature.
