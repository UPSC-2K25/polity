Parts of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India is organised into Parts, each dealing with a broad area of constitutional governance. These Parts group related Articles and provide a systematic framework for understanding citizenship, rights, Union and State institutions, federal relations, public finance, elections and other constitutional subjects.

The original Constitution contained 22 Parts. Constitutional amendments subsequently inserted new Parts, repealed an existing Part and altered several provisions. The present constitutional structure retains Parts numbered I to XXII, including lettered insertions such as IVA, IXA, IXB and XIVA. Part VII has been repealed.

This chapter is a structural reference. The substantive meaning of rights, institutions and procedures is examined in their respective chapters.

2. Parts I–IV: Constitutional Foundations

Part I — The Union and its Territory (Articles 1–4)

Part I deals with the name and territory of India and Parliament's authority concerning the admission, establishment and reorganisation of States.

  • Article 1: India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.
  • Article 2: Parliament may admit into the Union, or establish, new States.
  • Article 3: Parliament may form new States and alter the areas, boundaries or names of existing States, subject to the prescribed procedure.
  • Article 4: Laws under Articles 2 and 3 may contain supplemental, incidental and consequential provisions and are not treated as constitutional amendments under Article 368.

Article 3 requires the President to refer a proposal affecting the area, boundaries or name of a State to the legislature of that State for its views within the period specified or extended. Parliament is not constitutionally bound to accept those views.

See Federalism and Decentralisation for the Union's territorial and federal structure.

Part II — Citizenship (Articles 5–11)

Part II contains provisions concerning citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution. It addresses citizenship by domicile, migration, residence and specified circumstances involving persons of Indian origin.

Article 11 empowers Parliament to regulate citizenship by law. Parliament subsequently enacted the Citizenship Act, 1955, which provides the statutory framework for acquisition and termination of citizenship.

Part III — Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)

Part III guarantees Fundamental Rights and establishes constitutional protections for individuals and, in specified circumstances, groups. These rights limit State action and protect equality, liberty, religious freedom and other constitutional interests.

The Part includes provisions concerning the definition of the State, equality, freedoms, protection against exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural and educational rights and constitutional remedies.

See Fundamental Rights for the detailed treatment of individual rights, restrictions and remedies.

Part IV — Directive Principles of State Policy (Articles 36–51)

Part IV contains principles intended to guide the State in making laws and formulating public policy. Article 37 declares that these principles are not enforceable by any court, but are fundamental in the governance of the country.

They address social and economic welfare, distribution of resources, village panchayats, public health, education, environmental protection, labour welfare and international peace, among other objectives.

See Directive Principles of State Policy for their classification, implementation and relationship with Fundamental Rights.

3. Parts IVA–VIII: Duties and Governmental Institutions

Part IVA — Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)

Part IVA contains the Fundamental Duties of citizens. It was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, following the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee.

The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 added the duty of a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to a child or ward between six and fourteen years of age.

See Fundamental Duties.

Part V — The Union (Articles 52–151)

Part V establishes the principal institutions of the Union government. Its chapters address:

  • The President and Vice-President.
  • The Council of Ministers and the Attorney-General for India.
  • Parliament, its officers, legislative procedure and financial matters.
  • The Union judiciary, including the Supreme Court.
  • The Comptroller and Auditor-General of India.

Part V is central to the parliamentary system and separation of institutional responsibilities at the Union level.

Part VI — The States (Articles 152–237)

Part VI provides the principal constitutional framework for State governments. It deals with Governors, State executive authorities, State legislatures, High Courts and subordinate courts.

Its provisions do not apply uniformly to every Union Territory. Special constitutional arrangements govern certain territories and States.

Part VII — The States in Part B of the First Schedule (Repealed)

Part VII originally dealt with States classified as Part B States under the Constitution as it stood in 1950. It contained Article 238.

The Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956 repealed Part VII as part of the reorganisation of States and the replacement of the earlier Part A, Part B and Part C classification.

Part VIII — The Union Territories (Articles 239–242)

Part VIII deals with the administration of Union Territories. Article 239 provides for their administration by the President through an administrator appointed by the President, subject to the Constitution and applicable law.

Article 239AA contains special provisions concerning the National Capital Territory of Delhi. Article 239AB provides for specified constitutional arrangements in relation to Delhi. Puducherry is governed by Article 239A.

4. Parts IX–X: Local Government and Tribal Areas

Part IX — The Panchayats (Articles 243–243O)

Part IX gives constitutional recognition to Panchayati Raj institutions. It was inserted by the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 and came into force on 24 April 1993.

It addresses the constitution, composition, reservation, duration, elections, powers and finances of Panchayats. The Eleventh Schedule lists 29 subjects that may be entrusted to them.

Part IXA — The Municipalities (Articles 243P–243ZG)

Part IXA was inserted by the 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 and came into force on 1 June 1993. It gives constitutional recognition to urban local bodies.

It addresses the constitution, composition, reservation, duration, elections and responsibilities of Municipalities. The Twelfth Schedule lists 18 subjects that may be entrusted to them.

Part IXB — The Co-operative Societies (Articles 243ZH–243ZT)

Part IXB was inserted by the 97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011. It contains provisions concerning the incorporation, regulation, elections, audit and functioning of co-operative societies.

In Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah (2021), the Supreme Court held that the amendment required ratification by at least half of the State legislatures insofar as it affected co-operative societies operating within States. As that ratification had not been obtained, the relevant provisions were invalidated in that application. The provisions concerning multi-State co-operative societies and Union Territories were not invalidated on that ground.

Part X — The Scheduled and Tribal Areas (Articles 244–244A)

Part X deals with the administration of Scheduled Areas and Tribal Areas. Article 244 connects this Part with the Fifth and Sixth Schedules.

  • The Fifth Schedule provides for the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in specified States.
  • The Sixth Schedule provides special arrangements for certain tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.

Article 244A provides for the formation of an autonomous State comprising certain tribal areas in Assam and the creation of a legislature or Council of Ministers or both, as provided by law.

5. Parts XI–XIII: Federal Relations and Public Finance

Part XI — Relations between the Union and the States (Articles 245–263)

Part XI establishes the constitutional framework for legislative and administrative relations between the Union and the States.

  • Articles 245–255: Primarily concern legislative relations, including the territorial extent of laws and distribution of legislative subjects.
  • Articles 256–263: Primarily concern administrative relations, including Union directions and inter-State coordination.

The Seventh Schedule contains the Union List, State List and Concurrent List. The constitutional distribution of powers must be read with the relevant provisions of Part XI.

See Federalism and Decentralisation for legislative, administrative and financial relations.

Part XII — Finance, Property, Contracts and Suits (Articles 264–300A)

Part XII deals with public finance and aspects of government property and legal responsibility. It includes provisions concerning:

  • Taxation and the authority to levy taxes.
  • Consolidated Funds and public accounts.
  • Distribution of revenues between the Union and States.
  • Grants-in-aid and borrowing.
  • Government property, contracts and suits.
  • Protection of property under Article 300A.

Article 300A provides that no person shall be deprived of property save by authority of law. The right to property is a constitutional right, not a Fundamental Right.

Part XIII — Trade, Commerce and Intercourse (Articles 301–307)

Part XIII provides the constitutional framework for freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse throughout the territory of India, subject to constitutionally permitted restrictions and conditions.

These provisions seek to support economic unity while recognising the constitutional authority to impose certain restrictions in the public interest.

6. Parts XIV–XVI: Public Services, Tribunals and Elections

Part XIV — Services under the Union and the States (Articles 308–323)

Part XIV deals with public services under the Union and the States. It contains provisions concerning recruitment, conditions of service, tenure and protection of civil servants.

Articles 315–323 provide for the Union Public Service Commission and State Public Service Commissions.

Part XIVA — Tribunals (Articles 323A–323B)

Part XIVA was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.

  • Article 323A: Enables Parliament to provide for administrative tribunals dealing with specified service matters.
  • Article 323B: Enables the appropriate legislature to provide for tribunals dealing with specified subjects identified in the Article.

Tribunal decisions remain subject to constitutional judicial review by the High Courts and Supreme Court within the framework explained in the relevant case law.

Part XV — Elections (Articles 324–329)

Part XV provides the constitutional framework for elections. Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President in the Election Commission of India.

Article 326 provides for elections to the House of the People and State Legislative Assemblies on the basis of adult suffrage, subject to constitutional and statutory qualifications.

Part XVI — Special Provisions Relating to Certain Classes (Articles 330–342A)

Part XVI contains provisions concerning political representation and specified constitutional safeguards. These include provisions relating to reservation of seats and the identification and protection of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other constitutionally recognised groups.

The provisions have been modified through constitutional amendments. Their present operation must be read with the current constitutional text and relevant legislation.

7. Parts XVII–XXII: Language, Emergencies and Constitutional Amendment

Part XVII — Official Language (Articles 343–351)

Part XVII deals with the official language of the Union, regional languages, language used in courts and legislative proceedings, and special directives concerning language.

Article 343 provides that the official language of the Union shall be Hindi in Devanagari script. The constitutional provisions and legislation also govern the continued use of English.

Part XVIII — Emergency Provisions (Articles 352–360)

Part XVIII provides for emergency powers under the Constitution.

  • Article 352: Proclamation of National Emergency.
  • Article 356: Provisions in case of failure of constitutional machinery in States.
  • Article 360: Financial Emergency.

These provisions alter the ordinary distribution or exercise of constitutional powers in specified circumstances and are subject to constitutional safeguards.

Part XIX — Miscellaneous (Articles 361–367)

Part XIX contains miscellaneous provisions, including specified immunities and protections for constitutional office-holders, provisions concerning claims relating to treaties and agreements, definitions and interpretation.

Part XX — Amendment of the Constitution (Article 368)

Part XX consists of Article 368. It provides the procedure and constituent power of Parliament to amend the Constitution. Certain amendments require ratification by at least half of the State legislatures.

Parliament's amending power is subject to the basic structure doctrine established by the Supreme Court.

See Constitutional Amendment and Basic Structure Doctrine.

Part XXI — Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions (Articles 369–392)

Part XXI contains provisions concerning transitional arrangements, temporary provisions and special arrangements for particular States and circumstances.

Article 370, concerning Jammu and Kashmir, was originally placed in this Part. Its operation was substantially altered through constitutional measures taken in 2019. In December 2023, the Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of the principal measures relating to Article 370 and addressed the constitutional status of the provision.

Part XXI also contains special provisions for certain States, including provisions in Articles 371 and related Articles. These arrangements reflect India's asymmetric federal structure.

Part XXII — Short Title, Commencement, Authoritative Text in Hindi and Repeals (Articles 393–395)

Part XXII contains the concluding provisions of the Constitution. It deals with the short title, commencement, authoritative text in Hindi and repeals.

8. Parts Inserted by Constitutional Amendments

New Parts were added to address constitutional developments and institutional requirements after the Constitution commenced.

IXA Municipalities 74th Amendment Act, 1992 Constitutional recognition of urban local government.
IXB Co-operative Societies 97th Amendment Act, 2011 Constitutional provisions concerning co-operative societies, subject to the Supreme Court's ruling.

9. Repealed Part: Part VII

Part VII originally dealt with States classified as Part B States under the original constitutional arrangement. It contained Article 238.

The Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956 repealed Part VII as part of the reorganisation of States. The earlier classification of States into Parts A, B and C was replaced by the revised constitutional arrangement of States and Union Territories.

The repeal of Part VII illustrates that constitutional organisation has evolved in response to political, administrative and federal requirements.

10. Important Distinctions

  • Parts: Broad subject-based divisions of the Constitution.
  • Articles: Numbered constitutional provisions that establish rights, powers, duties and institutional arrangements.
  • Schedules: Supplementary lists, details and arrangements referred to in constitutional provisions.
  • Constitutional amendments: Changes to the constitutional text made through the applicable amendment procedure.

A Part may contain multiple chapters and Articles. A Schedule may contain material relevant to Articles across different Parts. The location of a provision in a particular Part should therefore not be confused with its substantive relationship to other constitutional provisions.

11. How to Use the Structure for Constitutional Analysis

The arrangement of Parts helps identify relationships across the Constitution. For example:

  • Fundamental Rights in Part III and Directive Principles in Part IV together inform the constitutional vision of justice.
  • Parts V and VI establish Union and State institutions, while Part XI defines important aspects of their relationship.
  • Parts IX and IXA provide a constitutional basis for democratic decentralisation, while Part XII concerns public finance and related financial arrangements.
  • Parts XIV and XIVA address public services and tribunals, while Part XV provides the constitutional framework for elections.
  • Part XVIII provides exceptional emergency powers, which must be understood in relation to federalism, rights and constitutional safeguards.

For analytical study, the Constitution should be approached as an interconnected framework rather than as a collection of isolated Parts.

Important Points for Revision

  • The original Constitution contained 22 Parts.
  • Part VII was repealed by the Seventh Amendment Act, 1956.
  • Part IVA contains Fundamental Duties.
  • Part IX deals with Panchayats; Part IXA deals with Municipalities.
  • Part IXB concerns Co-operative Societies; its application was limited by the Supreme Court's 2021 judgment.
  • Part X deals with Scheduled and Tribal Areas.
  • Part XI concerns Union–State legislative and administrative relations.
  • Part XII concerns finance, property, contracts and suits.
  • Part XIV concerns public services; Part XIVA concerns tribunals.
  • Part XV concerns elections.
  • Part XVIII contains emergency provisions.
  • Part XX contains Article 368 on constitutional amendment.
  • Part XXI contains temporary, transitional and special provisions.
