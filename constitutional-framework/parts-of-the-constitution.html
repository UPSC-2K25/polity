Parts of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India is organised into Parts, each dealing with a broad area of constitutional governance. The Parts group related Articles and provide a systematic framework for understanding the Union, the States, citizenship, rights, institutions and other constitutional subjects.

The original Constitution contained 22 Parts. Subsequent amendments inserted new Parts, repealed an existing Part and changed the arrangement of several provisions. The present constitutional text includes Parts numbered I to XXII, with certain lettered insertions such as IVA, IXA and XIVA.

2. Important Constitutional Parts

Part I – The Union and its Territory

Part I deals with the name and territory of India and the admission, establishment or alteration of States.

  • Article 1: India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.
  • Article 2: Parliament may admit into the Union, or establish, new States.
  • Article 3: Parliament may form new States and alter the areas, boundaries or names of existing States, subject to the prescribed procedure.
  • Article 4: Laws made under Articles 2 and 3 may make supplemental, incidental and consequential provisions.

Part II – Citizenship

Part II contains provisions relating to citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution. Articles 5–11 address citizenship in the context of commencement, migration, foreign citizenship and Parliament's power to regulate citizenship.

Parliament subsequently enacted the Citizenship Act, 1955 to provide a statutory framework governing acquisition and termination of citizenship.

Part III – Fundamental Rights

Part III guarantees Fundamental Rights and establishes constitutional protections for individuals and, in specified cases, groups. These rights limit State action and protect liberty, equality and other constitutional freedoms.

Articles 12–35 cover the definition of the State, equality, freedoms, protection against exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural and educational rights, constitutional remedies and related provisions.

See the detailed chapter on Fundamental Rights.

Part IV – Directive Principles of State Policy

Part IV contains principles intended to guide the State in making laws and formulating policies. Article 37 declares that these principles are not enforceable by any court, but are fundamental in the governance of the country.

Articles 36–51 cover social and economic welfare, organisation of village panchayats, public health, environmental protection, international peace and other objectives.

See the detailed chapter on Directive Principles of State Policy.

Part IVA – Fundamental Duties

Part IVA contains Article 51A, which lists the Fundamental Duties of citizens. It was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976. An additional duty relating to educational opportunities for children was added by the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002.

See the detailed chapter on Fundamental Duties.

Part V – The Union

Part V establishes the principal institutions of the Union government. It deals with the President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers, Parliament, legislative procedure, Union judiciary and the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India.

Its provisions extend from Articles 52 to 151. They form the constitutional foundation of Union-level executive, legislative and judicial institutions.

Part VI – The States

Part VI provides the constitutional framework for State governments. It deals with Governors, State executive authorities, State legislatures, High Courts and subordinate courts.

Its provisions extend from Articles 152 to 237. Part VI does not apply to certain Union Territories in the same manner as it applies to States.

Part VIII – The Union Territories

Part VIII deals with the administration of Union Territories. Article 239 provides for their administration by the President through an appointed administrator, subject to the Constitution and applicable laws.

Article 239AA contains special provisions concerning the National Capital Territory of Delhi. Article 239AB provides for constitutional arrangements in specified circumstances relating to Delhi.

Part IX – The Panchayats

Part IX gives constitutional recognition to Panchayati Raj institutions. It was inserted by the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992, which came into force on 24 April 1993.

It provides for the structure, composition, elections, reservation, duration, powers and finances of Panchayats. The Eleventh Schedule lists 29 subjects that may be entrusted to Panchayats.

Part IXA – The Municipalities

Part IXA was inserted by the 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992, which came into force on 1 June 1993. It provides constitutional recognition to urban local bodies.

It deals with the constitution, composition, reservation, duration, elections and responsibilities of Municipalities. The Twelfth Schedule lists 18 subjects that may be entrusted to them.

Part IXB – The Co-operative Societies

Part IXB was inserted by the 97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011. It contains provisions concerning the incorporation, regulation, elections, audit and functioning of co-operative societies.

In Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah (2021), the Supreme Court held that the amendment required ratification by at least half of the State legislatures insofar as it affected co-operative societies operating within States. As that ratification had not been obtained, the relevant provisions were invalidated in that application. The provisions relating to multi-State co-operative societies and Union Territories were not invalidated on that ground.

Part X – The Scheduled and Tribal Areas

Part X deals with the administration of Scheduled Areas and Tribal Areas. Article 244 connects this Part with the Fifth and Sixth Schedules.

The Fifth Schedule deals with the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in specified States. The Sixth Schedule provides special arrangements for certain tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.

Part XI – Relations between the Union and the States

Part XI deals with legislative and administrative relations between the Union and the States.

  • Articles 245–255 deal primarily with legislative relations.
  • Articles 256–263 deal primarily with administrative relations.

The distribution of legislative powers is closely connected with the Union List, State List and Concurrent List in the Seventh Schedule.

Part XII – Finance, Property, Contracts and Suits

Part XII deals with financial relations and the management of public resources. It includes provisions concerning taxation, distribution of revenues, borrowing, government property, contracts and legal proceedings.

Article 300A, which protects persons from deprivation of property except by authority of law, is also located in this Part.

Part XIII – Trade, Commerce and Intercourse

Part XIII provides the constitutional framework for freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse throughout the territory of India, subject to the restrictions and conditions permitted by the Constitution.

Articles 301–307 address the constitutional regulation of inter-State and intra-State trade and commerce.

Part XIV – Services under the Union and the States

Part XIV deals with public services under the Union and the States. It contains provisions relating to recruitment, conditions of service, tenure and Public Service Commissions.

Articles 315–323 provide for the Union Public Service Commission and State Public Service Commissions.

Part XIVA – Tribunals

Part XIVA was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.

  • Article 323A: Provides for administrative tribunals dealing with service matters.
  • Article 323B: Provides for tribunals dealing with specified other matters.

Part XV – Elections

Part XV deals with elections and the constitutional framework for electoral administration. Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President in the Election Commission of India.

Part XVI – Special Provisions Relating to Certain Classes

Part XVI contains provisions concerning the political representation and constitutional safeguards of specified classes. These include provisions relating to reservation of seats and representation of Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other specified groups.

Part XVII – Official Language

Part XVII deals with the official language of the Union, regional languages, language used in courts and legislative proceedings, and special directives concerning language.

Article 343 provides that the official language of the Union shall be Hindi in Devanagari script, with the constitutional provisions governing the use of English.

Part XVIII – Emergency Provisions

Part XVIII provides for emergency powers under the Constitution. It includes:

  • Article 352: Proclamation of National Emergency.
  • Article 356: Provisions in case of failure of constitutional machinery in States.
  • Article 360: Financial Emergency.

Part XIX – Miscellaneous

Part XIX contains miscellaneous constitutional provisions, including protections and immunities for certain constitutional office-holders, and provisions concerning definitions and interpretation.

Part XX – Amendment of the Constitution

Part XX consists of Article 368. It provides the procedure and constituent power of Parliament to amend the Constitution, subject to the basic structure limitation laid down by the Supreme Court.

Part XXI – Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions

Part XXI contains provisions relating to transitional arrangements, temporary provisions and special arrangements for certain States and circumstances.

Article 370, concerning Jammu and Kashmir, was originally placed in this Part. Its constitutional operation was substantially altered through measures taken in 2019, which were considered by the Supreme Court in its 2023 judgment on Article 370.

Part XXII – Short Title, Commencement, Authoritative Text in Hindi and Repeals

Part XXII contains the concluding provisions of the Constitution. It deals with the short title, commencement, authoritative text in Hindi and repeals.

Certain Parts were added after the Constitution came into force to address new constitutional requirements.

4. Repealed Part of the Constitution

Part VII – The States in Part B of the First Schedule

Part VII originally dealt with the States classified as Part B States under the original constitutional arrangement. It contained Article 238.

The Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956 reorganised the constitutional classification of States and repealed Part VII. The earlier distinction between Part A, Part B and Part C States was replaced by a revised arrangement of States and Union Territories.

5. Important Distinctions

  • Parts: Organise the Constitution into broad subject-based divisions.
  • Articles: Contain the actual constitutional provisions within those Parts.
  • Schedules: Provide supplementary details, lists and arrangements referred to in constitutional provisions.
  • Amendments: Change the constitutional text through the procedure prescribed under Article 368, where applicable.

Important Points for Revision

  • The original Constitution contained 22 Parts.
  • Part IVA deals with Fundamental Duties.
  • Part IX deals with Panchayats; Part IXA deals with Municipalities.
  • Part IXB deals with Co-operative Societies, subject to the Supreme Court's ruling in 2021.
  • Part X deals with Scheduled and Tribal Areas.
  • Part XIV deals with public services; Part XIVA deals with tribunals.
  • Part XVIII deals with Emergency Provisions.
  • Part XX contains Article 368, concerning constitutional amendment.
  • Part VII was repealed by the Seventh Amendment Act, 1956.
