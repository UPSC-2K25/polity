Constitutional Framework of India

The Constitution of India is the supreme law of the country. It establishes the institutions of government, distributes powers between the Union and the States, defines the rights and duties of citizens, and lays down the principles that guide the governance of the Republic.

The Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. Understanding its historical background, philosophical foundations, institutional design and provisions is essential to the study of Indian Polity.

Constitutional Structure at a Glance

The Constitution combines parliamentary government, federal features, fundamental rights, Directive Principles of State Policy, an independent judiciary and a framework for democratic representation. Its provisions establish both the structure of the State and the relationship between the State and the individual.

Understanding the Three Constitutional Directives

Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles of State Policy and Fundamental Duties serve different but complementary purposes. Fundamental Rights protect individual liberty and equality; Directive Principles guide the State towards social and economic justice; and Fundamental Duties express the responsibilities expected of citizens. Together, they reflect the constitutional vision of democratic governance.
