Sources of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India draws upon constitutional experience from several countries as well as India's own administrative and political history. The Constituent Assembly studied different constitutional systems and adapted provisions considered suitable for Indian conditions.

The Government of India Act, 1935 was a particularly important source of the administrative and federal framework. Other constitutional systems contributed ideas relating to parliamentary government, rights, judicial review, federalism and social policy.

1. Government of India Act, 1935

The Government of India Act, 1935 was the most substantial source of many administrative and structural provisions of the Indian Constitution. The Constitution-makers adapted several of its arrangements to suit a democratic republic.

  • Federal scheme and distribution of legislative powers.
  • Office of the Governor.
  • Public Service Commissions.
  • Emergency-related administrative framework.
  • Federal Court-related institutional legacy.
  • Administrative details of provincial governance.

2. United Kingdom

The British constitutional system influenced the parliamentary form of government and several conventions and institutional practices.

  • Parliamentary form of government.
  • Cabinet system and collective responsibility.
  • Rule of law.
  • Legislative procedure and parliamentary privileges.
  • Single citizenship.
  • Writ jurisdiction and related legal traditions.
  • Bicameralism in the legislature.

India's parliamentary system differs from the British model in important respects because India has a written and supreme Constitution, judicial review and a republican head of State.

3. United States of America

The Constitution of the United States influenced several provisions relating to constitutional rights, judicial institutions and the amendment framework.

  • Fundamental Rights.
  • Independence of the judiciary.
  • Judicial review.
  • Impeachment of the President.
  • Removal procedure for judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
  • Written Constitution and constitutional supremacy.
  • Vice-President as ex-officio Chairman of the Council of States.

4. Ireland

The Irish Constitution of 1937 influenced the Directive Principles of State Policy and certain institutional arrangements.

  • Directive Principles of State Policy.
  • Method of nomination of members to the Rajya Sabha.
  • Method of election of members of the Rajya Sabha by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies, in the constitutional context.

The Directive Principles were intended to guide the State in promoting social and economic welfare. They are not enforceable by courts but are fundamental in the governance of the country.

5. Canada

The Canadian Constitution influenced India's federal arrangement, particularly the distribution of powers and the position of the Union.

  • Federation with a strong Centre.
  • Residuary powers vested in the Union.
  • Appointment of State Governors by the Centre.
  • Advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.

India's federal arrangement gives the Union significant powers while constitutionally recognising the States as constituent units.

6. Australia

The Australian Constitution influenced India's legislative distribution and certain parliamentary arrangements.

  • Concurrent List.
  • Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse within the country.
  • Joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament to resolve specified legislative deadlocks.

The Indian Constitution provides for a Concurrent List in which both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate, subject to the constitutional rules governing repugnancy.

7. Germany (Weimar Constitution)

The Weimar Constitution of Germany influenced provisions concerning emergency powers.

  • Suspension of certain Fundamental Rights during an emergency, subject to constitutional safeguards.

India's emergency provisions are contained in Part XVIII. Their operation is governed by specific constitutional conditions and safeguards.

8. Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR)

The former Soviet constitutional system influenced the emphasis on social and economic justice and certain civic and institutional ideas.

  • Fundamental Duties.
  • Emphasis on social, economic and political justice in the Preamble.

Fundamental Duties were inserted into the Indian Constitution by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.

9. France

The French Revolution and French constitutional tradition influenced the ideals expressed in the Preamble.

  • Republican form of government.
  • Ideals of liberty, equality and fraternity.

These ideals reflect the constitutional commitment to individual freedom, equal civic status and fraternity among citizens.

10. South Africa

The South African Constitution influenced aspects of India's constitutional amendment procedure and the electoral method for the Council of States.

  • Procedure for amendment of the Constitution.
  • Election of members of the Rajya Sabha by State Legislative Assemblies through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.

11. Japan

The Japanese Constitution influenced the expression used in Article 21 concerning the procedure established by law.

  • Procedure established by law.

Article 21 of the Japanese Constitution is not the source of the Indian Article 21 itself; the constitutional phrase and concept are associated with the Japanese model. The Supreme Court has interpreted Article 21 of the Indian Constitution to require a fair, just and reasonable procedure.

12. Summary of Constitutional Sources

Source Important features adopted or adapted
Government of India Act, 1935 Federal scheme, distribution of powers, Governors, Public Service Commissions and administrative provisions.
United Kingdom Parliamentary government, cabinet system, collective responsibility, rule of law, single citizenship and parliamentary privileges.
United States of America Fundamental Rights, judicial review, judicial independence, impeachment and constitutional supremacy.
Ireland Directive Principles of State Policy and nomination of members to the Rajya Sabha.
Canada Strong Centre, residuary powers with the Union, Governors appointed by the Centre and advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
Australia Concurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce, and joint sitting of Parliament.
Germany (Weimar) Emergency provisions relating to suspension of certain Fundamental Rights.
USSR Fundamental Duties and emphasis on social, economic and political justice.
France Republican ideals of liberty, equality and fraternity.
South Africa Constitutional amendment procedure and proportional representation for Rajya Sabha elections.
Japan Procedure established by law.

13. Important Clarifications

  • The Constitution is not a mere copy of foreign constitutions. Its provisions were adapted to Indian conditions and constitutional objectives.
  • The Government of India Act, 1935 was a major source of administrative and federal provisions, but the Constitution established a fundamentally different democratic and sovereign political order.
  • The parliamentary system was influenced by the United Kingdom, but India's written Constitution and judicial review place constitutional limits on parliamentary power.
  • The Directive Principles are associated with the Irish Constitution, while Fundamental Rights were influenced by the American constitutional tradition.
  • The Constitution's amendment procedure is not identical to that of any single foreign country. It combines different procedures to balance stability and adaptability.
