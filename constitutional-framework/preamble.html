The Preamble to the Indian Constitution

The Preamble is the introductory statement of the Constitution. It expresses the source of constitutional authority, the nature of the Indian State and the objectives that the Constitution seeks to achieve.

It reflects the ideals of the freedom struggle and the vision of the Constituent Assembly. Although it is not a source of independent legislative power, it is an important aid to understanding and interpreting the Constitution.

1. Text of the Preamble

WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:

JUSTICE, social, economic and political;

LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;

EQUALITY of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all

FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;

IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

The Preamble records the adoption of the Constitution on 26 November 1949. The Constitution came fully into force on 26 January 1950.

2. Source of the Preamble

The Preamble is based on the Objectives Resolution moved by Jawaharlal Nehru in the Constituent Assembly on 13 December 1946. The Resolution was adopted on 22 January 1947.

The Objectives Resolution declared the intention to establish an independent sovereign republic in which authority would derive from the people. It also emphasised justice, equality, freedom and safeguards for minorities and disadvantaged groups.

These ideals were incorporated into the Preamble and provide a philosophical foundation for the Constitution.

3. Components of the Preamble

The Preamble can be understood through four constitutional elements:

  1. Source of authority: The words "We, the People of India" establish that constitutional authority ultimately derives from the people.
  2. Nature of the State: India is described as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic.
  3. Constitutional objectives: Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity are identified as the values to be secured and promoted.
  4. Date of adoption: The Preamble records that the Constitution was adopted, enacted and given to the people on 26 November 1949.

4. Meaning of the Key Terms

A. Sovereign

Sovereignty means that India is independent in its internal and external affairs. No external authority controls the country's constitutional or political decisions.

  • Internal sovereignty: The authority of the State operates throughout its territory under the Constitution.
  • External sovereignty: India is independent in its relations with other countries and international organisations.

India's membership of international organisations does not by itself compromise its sovereignty. International commitments are undertaken through the exercise of its sovereign authority.

B. Socialist

The term Socialist was added to the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976. It expresses the objective of reducing social and economic inequalities and promoting a welfare-oriented State.

  • Promotion of social and economic justice.
  • Reduction of inequalities in income and status.
  • Protection and advancement of disadvantaged groups.
  • Public action for social welfare and equitable development.

Indian socialism is pursued within a democratic constitutional framework. The Constitution does not mandate one particular economic system.

C. Secular

The term Secular was added to the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976. The Constitution does not establish an official state religion and protects freedom of conscience and religion.

  • Article 14 guarantees equality before the law and equal protection of the laws.
  • Article 15 prohibits discrimination on specified grounds, including religion.
  • Article 25 protects freedom of conscience and the right freely to profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to constitutional limitations.
  • Articles 26–28 provide further protections concerning religious affairs and religious instruction.

Indian secularism operates through constitutional equality, religious freedom and State regulation where permitted by the Constitution.

D. Democratic

Democracy means that political authority ultimately belongs to the people. India follows representative democracy, in which citizens elect representatives to legislative institutions.

  • Universal adult franchise.
  • Periodic elections.
  • Political participation and representation.
  • Government accountability to elected legislatures.
  • Protection of constitutional rights and freedoms.

Democracy in India includes political democracy and the broader constitutional objective of social and economic justice.

E. Republic

A republic is a form of government in which the head of State is not a hereditary monarch. India has an elected President who holds office for a constitutionally specified term.

  • The President is elected indirectly by an electoral college under Article 54.
  • The President holds office for five years, subject to the Constitution.
  • Public offices are not restricted to a hereditary ruling class.

5. Objectives of the Preamble

A. Justice

The Preamble promises social, economic and political justice.

  • Social justice: Removal of social inequalities, discrimination and unjust hierarchies.
  • Economic justice: Reduction of economic inequalities and promotion of fair opportunities and welfare.
  • Political justice: Equal political rights and opportunities to participate in public affairs.

B. Liberty

The Preamble promises liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship. These freedoms are protected through Fundamental Rights, subject to constitutional restrictions.

  • Freedom of thought and expression.
  • Freedom of belief and conscience.
  • Freedom of faith and worship.

C. Equality

The Preamble seeks equality of status and opportunity. Equality is reflected in constitutional guarantees against arbitrary discrimination and in provisions allowing measures for substantive equality.

  • Equality before the law.
  • Equal protection of the laws.
  • Equal opportunity in public employment, subject to constitutional provisions.
  • Abolition of untouchability.
  • Abolition of titles, subject to constitutional exceptions.

D. Fraternity

Fraternity means a sense of common brotherhood and solidarity among citizens. It seeks to strengthen national unity while respecting individual dignity and diversity.

  • Assurance of the dignity of the individual.
  • Promotion of national unity.
  • Protection of the unity and integrity of India.

The words "unity and integrity of the Nation" were incorporated into the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.

6. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment and the Preamble

The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 amended the Preamble. It made three textual changes:

  • Added the word Socialist before Democratic.
  • Added the word Secular before Democratic.
  • Changed "unity of the Nation" to "unity and integrity of the Nation".

No other amendment has been made to the Preamble. The 42nd Amendment did not replace the original constitutional objectives; it expressly added these terms to the Preamble.

7. Is the Preamble Part of the Constitution?

The constitutional status of the Preamble was considered by the Supreme Court in important decisions.

Berubari Union Case, 1960

In the In Re: Berubari Union reference, the Supreme Court observed that the Preamble was not a part of the Constitution. It recognised its importance as a guide to understanding the general purposes of the Constitution.

Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973

In Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, the Supreme Court held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution. The Court also recognised that Parliament's power to amend the Constitution under Article 368 is subject to the basic structure doctrine.

Later constitutional position

The Preamble is treated as an integral part of the Constitution. It is not independently enforceable in court and does not confer substantive powers, but it can assist in interpreting constitutional provisions.

8. Can the Preamble Be Amended?

The Supreme Court in Kesavananda Bharati recognised that the Preamble can be amended under Article 368, provided the amendment does not damage or destroy the basic structure of the Constitution.

The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 is the only amendment that has altered the text of the Preamble.

9. Legal Significance of the Preamble

  • It identifies the people as the ultimate source of constitutional authority.
  • It expresses the nature and objectives of the Indian State.
  • It provides a guide to interpreting ambiguous constitutional provisions.
  • It reflects the foundational values of the constitutional order.
  • It helps explain the relationship between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles.

The Preamble cannot override clear constitutional provisions or independently create legal powers. Its role is interpretative and foundational.

10. Important Supreme Court Cases

Case Year Significance
In Re: Berubari Union 1960 Initially held that the Preamble was not part of the Constitution; treated it as an aid to interpretation.
Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala 1973 Held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution and recognised the basic structure limitation on the amending power.
Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain 1975 Reaffirmed the importance of constitutional principles, including democracy and the rule of law.
Minerva Mills v. Union of India 1980 Emphasised the balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles as part of the constitutional framework.
S. R. Bommai v. Union of India 1994 Recognised secularism as a basic feature of the Constitution.

  • The Preamble was adopted on 26 November 1949.
  • The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 added Socialist, Secular and the word Integrity.
  • The Berubari case (1960) initially held that the Preamble was not part of the Constitution.
  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution.
