Making of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India was framed by the Constituent Assembly, a representative body established under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946. The Assembly debated the constitutional framework, examined reports of specialised committees and considered provisions clause by clause.

The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. Its making involved extensive deliberation, negotiation and the adaptation of constitutional ideas to India's social, political and administrative circumstances.

1. Formation of the Constituent Assembly

The demand for a Constituent Assembly to frame India's Constitution developed during the freedom movement. The idea received increasing support from Indian political leaders and became an important constitutional demand.

Background to the demand

  • M. N. Roy is generally credited with first putting forward the idea of a Constituent Assembly in 1934.
  • The Indian National Congress formally demanded a Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise in 1935.
  • The demand was accepted in principle by the British Government in the August Offer of 1940.
  • The Cripps Mission of 1942 proposed a constitution-making body after the Second World War, but its proposals were not accepted by Indian political leaders.
  • The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 provided the framework for establishing the Constituent Assembly.

2. Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946

The British Cabinet Mission arrived in India in March 1946. It consisted of Lord Pethick-Lawrence, Sir Stafford Cripps and A. V. Alexander. Its proposals provided for the constitution-making process and the formation of an interim government.

  • The Constituent Assembly was to consist of 389 members.
  • Of these, 292 seats were allocated to British Indian provinces, four to Chief Commissioners' provinces and 93 to princely states.
  • Representatives of British Indian provinces were to be elected indirectly by the provincial legislative assemblies through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.
  • Representatives of princely states were to be nominated by their rulers.
  • Seats were broadly allocated among the communities of General, Muslim and Sikh in the provinces, in proportion to their population.

Composition after Partition

The partition of India in 1947 changed the composition of the Assembly. Members representing areas that became part of Pakistan ceased to be members of the Indian Constituent Assembly. Its strength was consequently reduced from 389 to 299.

3. First Meeting and Election of Office-Bearers

  • The Constituent Assembly held its first meeting on 9 December 1946 in New Delhi.
  • Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha, the oldest member, served as the temporary Chairman.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President of the Constituent Assembly on 11 December 1946.
  • H. C. Mukherjee was elected Vice-President.
  • B. N. Rau served as the Constitutional Adviser to the Assembly.

The Muslim League initially boycotted the Assembly. After Partition, the Assembly continued as the constitution-making body for independent India.

4. Objectives Resolution

Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution in the Constituent Assembly on 13 December 1946. It was adopted on 22 January 1947 and provided the philosophical foundation for the Constitution.

The Resolution declared that:

  • India would be an independent sovereign republic.
  • All power and authority of the sovereign independent India and its constituent parts would derive from the people.
  • The people of India would be guaranteed justice, equality and freedom.
  • Adequate safeguards would be provided for minorities, backward and tribal areas and other disadvantaged groups.
  • India would work towards world peace and the welfare of humanity.

Significance: The Objectives Resolution shaped the ideals expressed in the Preamble, including popular sovereignty, justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.

6. Drafting Committee

The Drafting Committee was appointed on 29 August 1947 to scrutinise the draft prepared with the assistance of the Constitutional Adviser and to prepare a text for consideration by the Constituent Assembly.

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar was appointed Chairman of the Drafting Committee. He played a leading role in explaining, defending and coordinating the Committee's work during the Assembly debates.

B. L. Mitter resigned and was replaced by N. Madhava Rau. D. P. Khaitan died in 1948 and was replaced by T. T. Krishnamachari.

Role of B. N. Rau

Sir Benegal Narsing Rau was the Constitutional Adviser to the Constituent Assembly. He prepared an initial draft based on the decisions of the Assembly's committees and studied constitutional systems from different countries. His work provided an important foundation for the Drafting Committee's deliberations. He was not a member of the Drafting Committee.

7. Drafting and Deliberations

The drafting process involved several stages of preparation, publication, examination and debate.

  • The Drafting Committee examined the proposals and reports of the Constituent Assembly's committees.
  • The Draft Constitution was published in February 1948 to invite public discussion and suggestions.
  • Dr. Ambedkar introduced the Draft Constitution in the Assembly on 4 November 1948.
  • The Assembly considered the provisions in detail, debated amendments and examined the text clause by clause.
  • A large number of amendments were proposed during the deliberations. The Assembly considered 7,635 amendments that were tabled, of which 2,473 were moved and discussed.

The debates covered the nature of the Union, distribution of powers, fundamental rights, minority safeguards, parliamentary government, the judiciary, emergency provisions and the relationship between the Union and the States.

8. Adoption and Commencement

After extensive deliberations, the Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949. Some provisions came into force immediately, while the remaining provisions commenced on 26 January 1950.

  • 26 November 1949: The Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly.
  • 24 January 1950: Members of the Constituent Assembly signed the Constitution.
  • 26 January 1950: The Constitution came fully into force and India became a Republic.

The date 26 January was chosen to honour the declaration of Purna Swaraj, or complete independence, adopted by the Indian National Congress in 1929. Independence Day had been observed on 26 January 1930.

9. Time Taken and Important Figures

The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months and 18 days to complete the Constitution. It held 11 sessions, with 165 days spent in sessions; the Draft Constitution was discussed for 114 of those days.

10. Original Constitution

At the time of its adoption, the Constitution contained 395 Articles arranged in 22 Parts and 8 Schedules. It was one of the most detailed written constitutions in the world.

The original handwritten copies were prepared in English and Hindi. The calligraphy was done by Prem Behari Narain Raizada, and the pages were decorated by artists associated with the Santiniketan tradition under the guidance of Nandalal Bose.

The Constitution has since been amended many times. Its present structure therefore differs from the original text adopted in 1949.

Important Facts for Revision

  • The Constituent Assembly was constituted under the Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946.
  • Its original strength was 389, reduced to 299 after Partition.
  • Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was the temporary Chairman; Dr. Rajendra Prasad was its permanent President.
  • The Objectives Resolution was moved by Jawaharlal Nehru and adopted on 22 January 1947.
  • The Drafting Committee was appointed on 29 August 1947, with Dr. B. R. Ambedkar as its Chairman.
  • B. N. Rau was the Constitutional Adviser, not a member of the Drafting Committee.
  • The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950.
  • The original Constitution contained 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules.
