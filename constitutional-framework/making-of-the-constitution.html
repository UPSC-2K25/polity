Making of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India was framed by the Constituent Assembly, a constitution-making body established under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946. Its members examined constitutional models, considered reports of specialised committees, debated proposed provisions and adopted the final text on 26 November 1949.

The Constitution came fully into force on 26 January 1950. Its making was not simply a process of borrowing constitutional provisions: it involved adapting institutional ideas to India's historical experience, social diversity, territorial conditions and political aspirations.

1. Evolution of the Demand for a Constituent Assembly

The demand that Indians should frame their own Constitution developed alongside the national movement. It reflected the principle that constitutional legitimacy should rest on the people rather than unilateral decisions of the colonial government.

  • 1934: M. N. Roy is generally credited with first proposing the idea of a Constituent Assembly for India.
  • 1935: The Indian National Congress formally demanded a Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise.
  • 1938: Jawaharlal Nehru articulated the position that India's Constitution should be framed by a Constituent Assembly elected without external interference.
  • 1940 — August Offer: The British Government accepted, in principle, that Indians should participate in framing a new constitutional framework after the war.
  • 1942 — Cripps Proposals: The proposals envisaged a constitution-making body after the Second World War. They were rejected by major Indian political organisations.
  • 1946 — Cabinet Mission Plan: It provided the framework through which the Constituent Assembly was constituted.

The demand therefore evolved from a political claim for self-government into a concrete institutional arrangement for constitution-making.

2. Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946

The British Cabinet Mission arrived in India in March 1946. It comprised Lord Pethick-Lawrence, Sir Stafford Cripps and A. V. Alexander. Its plan proposed a Constituent Assembly and outlined the method for selecting its members.

Composition and method of selection

  • The Assembly was to have an initial strength of 389 members.
  • 292 seats were allocated to British Indian provinces.
  • 4 seats were allocated to Chief Commissioners' provinces.
  • 93 seats were allocated to princely states.
  • Representatives of British Indian provinces were elected indirectly by the provincial legislative assemblies.
  • The provincial representatives were elected through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.
  • Seats in the provinces were broadly allocated among the General, Muslim and Sikh communities in proportion to their population.
  • Representatives of princely states were to be nominated by their rulers.

The Assembly was therefore not directly elected through universal adult franchise. Its provincial members were indirectly elected, while princely-state representatives were nominated.

Composition after Partition

Partition altered the territorial composition of the Assembly. Members representing territories that became part of Pakistan ceased to belong to the Indian Constituent Assembly. Its strength consequently fell from 389 to 299.

Stage Total strength Composition
Initial composition 389 292 provincial representatives, 4 representatives of Chief Commissioners' provinces and 93 princely-state representatives.
After Partition 299 229 representatives of provinces and 70 representatives of princely states.

Significance and limitations

The Assembly brought together representatives from different regions, communities and political backgrounds. However, it was not elected by universal adult suffrage. Its representative character must therefore be understood in the context of colonial political institutions and the constraints of the period.

3. First Meeting and Organisation of the Assembly

  • The Constituent Assembly held its first meeting on 9 December 1946 in New Delhi.
  • Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha, the senior-most member present, served as the temporary Chairman.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on 11 December 1946.
  • H. C. Mukherjee was elected Vice-President.
  • B. N. Rau served as the Constitutional Adviser.

The Muslim League initially boycotted the Assembly. Following Partition and independence, the Assembly continued as the constitution-making body for independent India.

On 15 August 1947, the Assembly became a sovereign body. It also functioned as the Dominion Legislature until the Constitution came into force. Its constitution-making and legislative functions were distinct, even though they were performed by the same body.

4. Objectives Resolution

Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946. The Assembly adopted it on 22 January 1947. It articulated the political and constitutional principles that would guide the framing process.

Core principles

  • India would be an independent sovereign republic.
  • All power and authority of the sovereign independent India and its constituent parts would derive from the people.
  • The people would be guaranteed justice, equality and freedom.
  • Adequate safeguards would be provided for minorities, backward and tribal areas and other disadvantaged groups.
  • India would contribute to world peace and the welfare of humanity.

Constitutional significance: The Resolution supplied the philosophical foundation for the Preamble. Its emphasis on popular sovereignty, justice, liberty, equality and fraternity is reflected in the constitutional vision.

The Assembly relied on specialised committees to examine different constitutional questions. Their reports helped convert broad political principles into detailed institutional and legal provisions.

Why the committee system mattered

  • It divided a complex constitution-making exercise into manageable subject areas.
  • It enabled detailed examination of federal relations, rights, minority safeguards and institutional design.
  • It provided a structured basis for debate and decision-making by the full Assembly.

6. Drafting Committee

The Drafting Committee was appointed on 29 August 1947. It was tasked with examining the decisions and reports of the Assembly, scrutinising the draft prepared with the assistance of the Constitutional Adviser and preparing a text for consideration by the Constituent Assembly.

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar was appointed Chairman. He played a central role in explaining and defending the draft provisions and coordinating the Committee's work during the debates.

Role of B. N. Rau

Sir Benegal Narsing Rau was the Constitutional Adviser to the Constituent Assembly, not a member of the Drafting Committee. He prepared an initial draft based on the decisions of the Assembly's committees and examined constitutional systems in other countries. His work provided an important foundation for the Drafting Committee's subsequent examination and drafting.

7. Drafting, Public Consultation and Debates

The preparation of the Constitution involved successive stages of research, drafting, publication, public discussion, amendment and deliberation by the Assembly.

  • The Constitutional Adviser prepared an initial draft drawing on committee decisions and comparative constitutional study.
  • The Drafting Committee examined the proposals and reports and prepared a draft Constitution.
  • The Draft Constitution was published in February 1948 to invite public comments and suggestions.
  • Dr. Ambedkar introduced the Draft Constitution in the Assembly on 4 November 1948.
  • The Assembly debated the provisions and considered the text clause by clause.
  • Members proposed amendments to examine, clarify and alter the draft provisions.

The Assembly considered 7,635 amendments that were tabled, of which 2,473 were moved and discussed.

Major themes of deliberation

  • The nature of the Indian Union and the distribution of legislative, administrative and financial powers.
  • Fundamental rights, individual liberty and the scope of state action.
  • Minority safeguards, social justice and representation.
  • Parliamentary government and executive accountability.
  • Judicial independence, constitutional remedies and judicial institutions.
  • Emergency powers, national unity and constitutional safeguards.

The debates reveal that the Constitution emerged through negotiation and reasoned disagreement. The final text reflected attempts to reconcile liberty with social transformation, national unity with diversity, and effective government with institutional accountability.

8. Adoption, Signing and Commencement

The Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949. Members signed it on 24 January 1950. It came fully into force on 26 January 1950.

Date Event
26 November 1949 Constitution adopted by the Constituent Assembly.
24 January 1950 Members signed the Constitution.
26 January 1950 Constitution came fully into force; India became a Republic.

Why 26 January?

The date was chosen to honour the declaration of Purna Swaraj, or complete independence, adopted by the Indian National Congress at its Lahore session in December 1929. 26 January 1930 was observed as Independence Day by nationalists.

Provisions that commenced earlier

Not every provision waited until 26 January 1950 to become operative. Article 394 specified that certain provisions came into force immediately on 26 November 1949. These included provisions relating to citizenship, the transitional arrangements for the President and other constitutional matters, as well as provisions necessary for bringing the remaining Constitution into operation.

9. Time Taken and Important Figures

The Constituent Assembly took 2 years, 11 months and 18 days to complete the Constitution. It held 11 sessions, spending 165 days in session. The Draft Constitution was discussed for 114 of those days.

10. The Original Constitution

At the time of its adoption, the Constitution contained 395 Articles, arranged in 22 Parts and 8 Schedules. It was among the most detailed written constitutions in the world.

The original handwritten copies were prepared in English and Hindi. The English calligraphy was done by Prem Behari Narain Raizada. The pages were decorated by artists associated with the Santiniketan tradition under the guidance of Nandalal Bose.

The Constitution has since been amended. Its present structure and provisions therefore differ from the original text adopted in 1949.

11. Constitutional Significance of the Making Process

Popular sovereignty

The Objectives Resolution established the principle that constitutional authority ultimately derives from the people. This principle is expressed in the opening words of the Preamble: "We, the People of India".

Deliberative constitution-making

Committee reports, public suggestions, amendment proposals and clause-by-clause debate made the process deliberative rather than a unilateral exercise by a small drafting group.

Adaptation rather than mechanical borrowing

The Assembly examined institutions and ideas from different constitutional systems but adapted them to India's conditions. Comparative influence did not mean that provisions were adopted without modification or debate.

Balancing competing constitutional objectives

The debates addressed tensions between individual rights and social reform, central authority and regional autonomy, parliamentary responsibility and executive stability, and national integration and cultural diversity.

Representative legitimacy and its limitations

The Assembly was not elected through universal adult franchise. Nevertheless, its debates, broad political participation and efforts to accommodate diverse interests contributed to the legitimacy of the Constitution. Its representativeness should be assessed historically rather than treated as identical to that of a directly elected legislature.
