State Human Rights Commissions (SHRCs)

State Human Rights Commissions are statutory institutions established to protect and promote human rights at the State level. They examine complaints of human rights violations, review safeguards and recommend measures to the State Government and other concerned authorities.

They form an important part of India's human rights protection framework alongside the National Human Rights Commission. Their jurisdiction, however, is limited by the subject matters assigned to State Legislatures and by the provisions of the Protection of Human Rights Act.

1. Establishment and Legal Basis

State Human Rights Commissions are provided for under Chapter V of the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. Section 21 empowers a State Government to constitute a State Human Rights Commission for the State.

The Act does not make the establishment of a State Commission automatic. A State Government may constitute one through the statutory process. The Commission exercises the powers and performs the functions assigned to it under the Act.

2. Meaning and Scope of Human Rights

Section 2(1)(d) of the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 defines human rights as rights relating to life, liberty, equality and dignity of the individual, guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in the International Covenants and enforceable by courts in India.

This definition provides the legal foundation for the work of both the National Human Rights Commission and State Human Rights Commissions.

The State Commission may examine allegations involving violations of these rights, subject to the jurisdictional limits and procedural restrictions prescribed by the Act.

3. Composition of the State Commission

Section 21(2) specifies the composition of a State Human Rights Commission. The statutory composition consists of a Chairperson and two Members.

Position Qualification
Chairperson A person who has been the Chief Justice or a Judge of a High Court.
Member 1 A person who is, or has been, a Judge of a High Court or a District Judge in the State with a minimum of seven years' experience as a District Judge.
Member 2 A person appointed from among persons having knowledge of, or practical experience in, matters relating to human rights.

3.1 Secretary and Administrative Structure

Section 21 also provides for a Secretary who acts as the Chief Executive Officer of the State Commission. The Secretary exercises administrative and financial powers subject to the control of the Chairperson.

The headquarters of the State Commission is located at the place specified by the State Government through a notification.

3.2 No Separate Category of Deemed Members

The statutory composition of a State Human Rights Commission is different from that of the NHRC. Section 21 does not provide an equivalent list of chairpersons of other State commissions as deemed members of the SHRC.

4. Appointment of Chairperson and Members

Under Section 22, the Chairperson and Members of a State Human Rights Commission are appointed by the Governor by warrant under the Governor's hand and seal.

The Governor makes the appointments on the recommendation of a selection committee constituted under the Act.

4.1 Selection Committee

Member Position

Where a State has a Legislative Council, its Chairman and the Leader of the Opposition in that Council are also included in the selection committee.

The committee recommends names for appointment. The formal appointment is made by the Governor.

5. Tenure and Removal

5.1 Tenure

Section 24 prescribes the term of office of the Chairperson and Members of a State Human Rights Commission. The term is three years, subject to the applicable age limit of seventy years.

Office Term Age limit
Chairperson Three years Until attaining 70 years, whichever is earlier
Member Three years Cannot continue after attaining 70 years

The Chairperson and Members are eligible for reappointment, subject to the statutory conditions and age limit.

5.2 Resignation

Under Section 23, the Chairperson or a Member may resign by giving written notice under their hand addressed to the Governor.

5.3 Removal

The Chairperson or a Member of a State Commission can be removed from office by the President of India on the grounds and through the procedure specified in Section 23.

In cases of proved misbehaviour or incapacity, the President makes a reference to the Supreme Court. If the Supreme Court, after an inquiry conducted according to the prescribed procedure, reports that the person ought to be removed, the President may issue the removal order.

The Act also provides for removal on specified additional grounds, including insolvency, engaging in paid employment outside the duties of office, infirmity of mind or body, unsoundness of mind declared by a competent court and conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude.

6. Functions of State Human Rights Commissions

Section 29 makes the functions specified in Section 12 applicable to State Human Rights Commissions, subject to the provisions of the Act and the State Commission's jurisdiction.

6.1 Inquiry into Human Rights Violations

A State Commission may inquire into complaints of human rights violations, abetment of such violations or negligence in preventing them by a public servant.

It may take up matters through:

  • A complaint presented by a victim or another person on the victim's behalf.
  • A direction or order of a court.
  • Its own initiative, commonly known as suo motu action.

6.2 Intervention in Court Proceedings

With the approval of the concerned court, the State Commission may intervene in proceedings involving allegations of human rights violations that are pending before that court.

6.3 Visits to Prisons and Institutions

The Commission may visit jails and other institutions under the control of the State Government where persons are detained or lodged for treatment, reformation or protection.

It examines the living conditions of persons held in these institutions and makes recommendations to the government concerned.

6.4 Review of Constitutional and Legal Safeguards

The Commission reviews the safeguards provided by the Constitution and laws for the protection of human rights and recommends measures for their effective implementation.

6.5 Review of Factors Inhibiting Human Rights

It examines factors, including acts of terrorism, that inhibit the enjoyment of human rights and recommends appropriate remedial measures.

6.6 Research and Human Rights Awareness

The Commission undertakes and promotes research in the field of human rights and spreads human rights literacy among different sections of society.

6.7 International Human Rights Instruments

It studies international treaties and other instruments on human rights and makes recommendations for their effective implementation.

6.8 Cooperation with Non-Governmental Organisations

The Commission encourages the efforts of non-governmental organisations and institutions working in the field of human rights.

7. Powers of the State Commission

Through Section 29, the inquiry-related provisions of the Act, including Section 13, apply to State Human Rights Commissions. During an inquiry, the Commission has certain powers of a civil court.

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of witnesses.
  • Examining witnesses on oath.
  • Discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning public records or copies from courts or offices.
  • Issuing commissions for examination of witnesses or documents.

These powers assist the Commission in gathering evidence and examining complaints. They do not convert the Commission into an ordinary civil or criminal court.

8. Jurisdiction of State Human Rights Commissions

The jurisdiction of a State Human Rights Commission is an important distinction between the SHRC and the NHRC.

Under Section 21(5), a State Commission may inquire into violations of human rights only in respect of matters relatable to entries in the State List and the Concurrent List of the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution.

8.1 State List and Concurrent List

List Jurisdictional significance
State List (List II) Matters on which the State Legislature ordinarily has legislative competence.
Concurrent List (List III) Matters on which both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate, subject to constitutional rules.
Union List (List I) Not included in the ordinary subject-matter jurisdiction specified for State Commissions under Section 21(5).

Therefore, the State Commission's jurisdiction is connected to the distribution of legislative powers under the Seventh Schedule.

8.2 Restriction on Parallel Inquiries

Section 21(5) provides that where a matter is already being inquired into by the NHRC or another Commission duly constituted under a law in force, the State Commission shall not inquire into that matter.

This restriction helps avoid overlapping inquiries by multiple human rights commissions.

8.3 One-Year Limitation

Section 36(2) provides that the Commission or a State Commission shall not inquire into a matter after the expiry of one year from the date on which the act constituting the alleged human rights violation is said to have been committed.

9. Recommendations and Nature of Powers

State Human Rights Commissions perform primarily investigative, recommendatory and promotional functions. They are not regular criminal courts and do not ordinarily issue binding judicial decrees.

After completing an inquiry, a State Commission may recommend:

  • Payment of compensation or damages to the victim or the victim's family.
  • Initiation of prosecution or other suitable action against persons responsible.
  • Interim relief to the victim or family.
  • Further action by the concerned State Government or authority.
  • Appropriate proceedings before the Supreme Court or a High Court for directions, orders or writs.

The concerned government or authority is required to provide its comments on the Commission's report and indicate the action taken or proposed within the period prescribed by the Act.

The recommendations are generally not equivalent to enforceable judicial orders. Their implementation depends on the statutory framework and the response of the concerned authorities.

10. Reports and Accountability

Section 28 provides for annual and special reports of the State Commission.

The Commission submits its reports to the State Government. The State Government is required to place these reports before the State Legislature, along with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed to be taken on the recommendations.

Where any recommendation is not accepted, the memorandum is also required to include the reasons for its non-acceptance.

This reporting arrangement provides legislative oversight and enables elected representatives to examine the Commission's work and the government's response.

13. Important Distinctions for UPSC

13.1 SHRC and NHRC

Both institutions are statutory bodies under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. The principal differences concern their composition, appointment authorities, selection committees and jurisdiction.

13.2 Appointment and Removal

A frequently tested distinction is that the Governor appoints the Chairperson and Members of a State Commission, whereas the President has the statutory authority to remove them under Section 23.

13.3 Constitutional or Statutory Body

State Human Rights Commissions are statutory bodies. They are not established directly by the Constitution. Their legal existence and functions arise from the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.

  • The Commission has two Members in addition to the Chairperson.
  • The Governor appoints the Chairperson and Members.

  • The President has the statutory authority to remove the Chairperson or a Member.
  • The normal term is three years, subject to the age limit of seventy years.
  • Section 29 applies specified NHRC functions and inquiry provisions to SHRCs.
  • Section 21(5) limits the SHRC's subject-matter jurisdiction to State List and Concurrent List matters.
  • Section 36(2) provides a one-year limitation for inquiry into alleged violations.
  • Section 28 deals with annual and special reports of State Commissions.
  • The recommendations of SHRCs are generally recommendatory in nature.
