Statutory Bodies

Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)

SEBI is the statutory regulator of India's securities market. It protects investors, regulates securities markets and promotes their orderly development.

1. Introduction and Evolution

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) was initially established as a non-statutory body through a Government of India resolution on 12 April 1988. It was given statutory status through the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992.

  • Established: 12 April 1988.
  • Statutory status: 1992.
  • Parent legislation: SEBI Act, 1992.
  • Headquarters: Mumbai.
  • Nature: Statutory regulatory body.

The growth of India's securities market and the need to protect investors from unfair practices led to the creation of a specialised regulator.

2. Objectives of SEBI

The SEBI Act identifies three broad objectives:

  1. Protecting the interests of investors in securities.
  2. Promoting the development of the securities market.
  3. Regulating the securities market and matters connected with or incidental to it.

These objectives seek to improve transparency, fairness, accountability and confidence in the securities market.

3. Organisation and Composition

SEBI is managed by a Board constituted under Section 4 of the SEBI Act, 1992.

The Board consists of:

  • A Chairperson.
  • Two members from among the officials of the Ministry of the Central Government dealing with finance and administration of the Companies Act.
  • One member from among the officials of the Reserve Bank of India.
  • Five other members, of whom at least three must be whole-time members.

The Chairperson and other members are appointed by the Central Government in accordance with the Act. The Board exercises the powers and performs the functions assigned to SEBI under the legislation.

4. Functions of SEBI

Section 11 of the SEBI Act, 1992, lays down the functions of SEBI. These can be grouped into protective, regulatory and developmental functions.

A. Protective Functions

  • Protects investors against unfair and fraudulent trade practices.
  • Prevents insider trading in securities.
  • Promotes investor education and awareness.
  • Regulates substantial acquisition of shares and takeovers.
  • Addresses market manipulation and misleading disclosures.

B. Regulatory Functions

  • Regulates stock exchanges and other securities markets.
  • Registers and regulates market intermediaries such as brokers, merchant bankers and portfolio managers.
  • Regulates mutual funds and collective investment schemes.
  • Regulates venture capital funds and other specified investment entities.
  • Regulates credit rating agencies and other notified market participants.
  • Prescribes disclosure and governance requirements for listed companies.
  • Conducts inspections, inquiries and audits of regulated entities.

C. Developmental Functions

  • Promotes investor education and training of intermediaries.
  • Encourages the development of securities market infrastructure.
  • Promotes fair and efficient market practices.
  • Supports innovation and improvements in market systems, subject to investor protection.

5. Powers of SEBI

SEBI exercises statutory powers to regulate securities markets and enforce compliance.

Quasi-Legislative Powers

SEBI can frame regulations and issue guidelines under the authority of the SEBI Act and other applicable laws. These regulations govern market intermediaries, listed entities and securities market activities.

Quasi-Executive Powers

SEBI can inspect regulated entities, investigate suspected violations, call for information, conduct inquiries and take enforcement action within its statutory authority.

Quasi-Judicial Powers

SEBI can conduct adjudication proceedings and impose monetary penalties for violations covered by the Act. Its orders are subject to statutory appellate and judicial review mechanisms.

Important Statutory Powers

Section Subject
Section 11 Functions of the Board.
Section 11(2) Specified measures that SEBI may take in furtherance of its functions.
Section 11B Power to issue directions and exercise specified regulatory powers.
Section 11C Investigation powers in specified circumstances.
Section 11D Power to issue directions in relation to specified unfair trade practices.
Section 12 Registration of specified intermediaries and other regulated entities.
Section 15A onwards Penalties for specified failures and contraventions.

6. Regulation of Securities Markets

SEBI regulates the primary and secondary markets for securities.

Primary Market

The primary market is where securities are issued to investors for the first time or through subsequent public issues. SEBI prescribes disclosure, eligibility and procedural requirements for public issues and other regulated securities offerings.

Secondary Market

The secondary market enables investors to buy and sell securities after their initial issue. SEBI regulates stock exchanges, trading practices, clearing and settlement arrangements, and market intermediaries.

Stock Exchanges

Stock exchanges provide organised platforms for trading securities. SEBI supervises recognised stock exchanges and prescribes standards relating to transparency, investor protection, market integrity and risk management.

7. Regulation of Market Intermediaries

Market intermediaries facilitate the functioning of securities markets. SEBI registers and regulates entities within its statutory jurisdiction.

Intermediary Role
Stock Brokers Execute securities transactions on behalf of clients or as permitted by regulations.
Merchant Bankers Manage public issues and provide specified financial and advisory services.
Mutual Funds Pool investments from investors and invest in securities according to scheme objectives.
Portfolio Managers Manage client portfolios under the applicable regulatory framework.
Credit Rating Agencies Assess and communicate credit risk associated with specified debt instruments.
Depositories Provide electronic holding and transfer infrastructure for securities.
Investment Advisers Provide investment advice under SEBI's regulatory framework.

8. Investor Protection

Investor protection is a central responsibility of SEBI. It seeks to ensure that investors receive accurate information and are protected against fraud, manipulation and unfair market practices.

Major measures include:

  • Disclosure requirements for listed companies and securities issuers.
  • Regulation against insider trading and fraudulent practices.
  • Regulation of intermediaries and market conduct.
  • Investor awareness and financial education initiatives.
  • Grievance redressal mechanisms, including the SEBI Complaints Redress System (SCORES).
  • Regulatory action against violations and misconduct.

SEBI's investor grievance mechanisms complement, but do not replace, the legal remedies available to investors.

9. Insider Trading and Market Manipulation

Insider trading involves dealing in securities while in possession of unpublished price-sensitive information, in violation of applicable regulations.

SEBI regulates insider trading through the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015, as amended from time to time.

Market manipulation includes conduct intended to create misleading market activity, distort prices or deceive investors. SEBI acts against fraudulent and unfair trade practices under the applicable law and regulations.

10. Regulation of Mutual Funds

Mutual funds collect money from investors and invest it in securities and other permitted assets according to the objectives of each scheme.

SEBI regulates mutual funds through the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996, as amended.

  • Prescribes registration and eligibility requirements.
  • Regulates the structure and governance of mutual funds.
  • Requires disclosure of scheme information and investment risks.
  • Prescribes rules relating to valuation, investment restrictions and expenses.
  • Supervises trustees, asset management companies and other relevant entities.

11. Substantial Acquisition and Takeovers

SEBI regulates substantial acquisition of shares and takeovers of listed companies through the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011, as amended.

The framework seeks to ensure transparency, protect public shareholders and provide appropriate exit opportunities in specified circumstances.

Disclosure obligations and open-offer requirements apply when specified thresholds and conditions under the regulations are triggered.

12. Appeals Against SEBI Orders

Orders passed by SEBI can be challenged through the appellate mechanism established under the SEBI Act.

  • Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT): Section 15T provides for appeals to SAT against specified orders of SEBI and other authorities covered by the provision.
  • Supreme Court: Section 15Z provides for an appeal to the Supreme Court from an order of SAT on a question of law, within the statutory framework.

The appellate structure provides legal oversight and an avenue for persons aggrieved by specified regulatory decisions.

13. Coordination with Other Regulators

India's financial system is regulated by several specialised institutions. Their jurisdictions may intersect in areas involving financial products, institutions and markets.

Regulator Principal Area
RBI Monetary policy, banking regulation and specified financial institutions.
SEBI Securities markets and securities market intermediaries.
IRDAI Insurance sector regulation.
PFRDA Pension sector regulation within its statutory jurisdiction.
IFSCA Financial products, services and institutions in International Financial Services Centres.

Coordination among regulators is important to address regulatory gaps, overlapping jurisdictions and risks to financial stability.

14. Challenges Before SEBI

  • Market volatility: Maintaining fair and orderly markets during periods of uncertainty.
  • Digital fraud: Addressing online investment scams, impersonation and misleading financial promotions.
  • Insider trading: Detecting misuse of confidential and unpublished price-sensitive information.
  • Corporate governance: Ensuring transparency and accountability in listed companies.
  • New financial products: Responding to complex instruments and rapidly changing market practices.
  • Investor awareness: Improving financial literacy and helping investors understand risk.
  • Regulatory capacity: Keeping pace with technological change and increasingly complex financial markets.

15. Significance for Indian Governance

SEBI contributes to the integrity and stability of India's financial system by regulating securities markets and protecting investors. Its work supports capital formation, corporate accountability and public confidence in financial markets.

As a statutory regulator, SEBI illustrates the role of specialised institutions in modern economic governance. Its effectiveness depends on regulatory capacity, transparency, accountability and fair enforcement.

16. Prelims Facts

  • SEBI was established in 1988 as a non-statutory body.
  • It received statutory status through the SEBI Act, 1992.
  • Its headquarters is in Mumbai.
  • Section 4 of the SEBI Act deals with the composition of the Board.
  • Section 11 deals with the functions of SEBI.
  • Section 11B provides important powers to issue directions.
  • Section 11C deals with investigation powers in specified circumstances.
  • The Securities Appellate Tribunal hears appeals against specified SEBI orders.
  • SEBI regulates stock exchanges and securities market intermediaries.
  • SEBI is a statutory body, not a constitutional body.
