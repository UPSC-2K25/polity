National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)

The National Human Rights Commission is a statutory, autonomous public institution established to protect and promote human rights in India. It examines alleged violations of human rights, reviews safeguards provided by the Constitution and laws, and recommends measures to improve their protection.

The Commission forms an important part of India's institutional framework for accountability, dignity, equality and protection against abuse of public power.

1. Establishment and Legal Basis

The NHRC was established on 12 October 1993 under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993.

It was created in the context of growing national and international attention to the protection of human rights and the need for an independent institution to investigate complaints and promote awareness.

2. Meaning of Human Rights

Under Section 2(1)(d) of the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993, human rights refer to rights relating to life, liberty, equality and dignity of the individual, guaranteed by the Constitution or embodied in the International Covenants and enforceable by courts in India.

The definition connects human rights protection with constitutional guarantees, statutory safeguards and India's obligations under recognised international human rights instruments.

3. Composition of the Commission

The composition of the NHRC is governed by Section 3 of the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993, as amended. It consists of a Chairperson, specified judicial members, members with knowledge or practical experience in human rights and certain deemed members.

3.1 Chairperson and Members

Position Qualification / Basis
Chairperson A person who has been the Chief Justice of India or a Judge of the Supreme Court.
Judicial Member A person who is, or has been, a Judge of the Supreme Court.
Judicial Member A person who is, or has been, the Chief Justice of a High Court.
Other Members Three persons having knowledge of, or practical experience in, matters relating to human rights, subject to the statutory requirement that at least one is a woman.

3.2 Deemed Members

Certain national commissions are represented in the NHRC through their chairpersons, who are deemed members for the purposes specified in the Act.

These include the chairpersons of:

  • National Commission for Scheduled Castes.
  • National Commission for Scheduled Tribes.
  • National Commission for Backward Classes.
  • National Commission for Minorities.
  • National Commission for Women.
  • National Commission for Protection of Child Rights.
  • Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities.

Their participation helps connect human rights concerns with the work of institutions dealing with vulnerable and disadvantaged groups.

4. Appointment of Chairperson and Members

The Chairperson and members are appointed by the President of India by warrant under his or her hand and seal. The appointments are made on the recommendation of a committee constituted under the Protection of Human Rights Act.

The selection committee recommends names for appointment. The President makes the formal appointments under the Act.

5. Tenure and Removal

5.1 Tenure

Under the amended Act, the Chairperson and members hold office for a term of three years or until they attain the age of seventy years, whichever is earlier.

They are eligible for reappointment, subject to the statutory age limit and other applicable conditions.

5.2 Resignation

The Chairperson or a member may resign by writing addressed to the President of India.

5.3 Removal

The President may remove the Chairperson or a member on the grounds and through the procedure specified in Section 5 of the Protection of Human Rights Act.

In the case of proved misbehaviour or incapacity, removal requires an inquiry by the Supreme Court following a reference made by the President. The President may remove the person after receiving the Court's report establishing the relevant ground.

The Act also specifies certain other grounds on which the President may remove a member, including insolvency, paid employment outside the duties of office, infirmity of mind or body, unsoundness of mind declared by a competent court, and conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude.

6. Functions of the NHRC

Section 12 of the Protection of Human Rights Act specifies the functions of the Commission.

6.1 Inquiry into Human Rights Violations

The Commission may inquire into complaints of human rights violations, abetment of such violations or negligence in preventing them by a public servant.

It may act on:

  • A complaint presented to it by a victim or another person on the victim's behalf.
  • A direction or order of a court.
  • Its own initiative, known as suo motu action.

6.2 Intervention in Court Proceedings

With the approval of the court, the Commission may intervene in proceedings involving allegations of human rights violations that are pending before a court.

6.3 Visit to Prisons and Institutions

The Commission may visit jails and other institutions under the control of State Governments where persons are detained or lodged for treatment, reformation or protection.

It examines living conditions and makes recommendations to the government concerned.

6.4 Review of Constitutional and Legal Safeguards

It reviews the safeguards provided by the Constitution and laws for the protection of human rights and recommends measures for their effective implementation.

6.5 Review of Factors Inhibiting Human Rights

The Commission examines factors, including acts of terrorism, that inhibit the enjoyment of human rights and recommends appropriate remedial measures.

6.6 Research and Awareness

It undertakes and promotes research in the field of human rights and spreads human rights literacy among different sections of society.

6.7 International Conventions

The Commission studies international treaties and other instruments on human rights and makes recommendations for their effective implementation.

6.8 Non-Governmental Organisations

It encourages the efforts of non-governmental organisations and institutions working in the field of human rights.

7. Powers of the Commission

While conducting an inquiry, the NHRC has certain powers of a civil court under Section 13 of the Act. These powers help it gather evidence and examine matters within its jurisdiction.

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of witnesses.
  • Examining witnesses on oath.
  • Discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning public records or copies from courts or offices.
  • Issuing commissions for examination of witnesses or documents.

The Commission may also require information from governments and authorities in accordance with the provisions of the Act.

8. Recommendations and Nature of Powers

The NHRC is not a regular criminal court. Its principal role is investigative, recommendatory and promotional.

After completing an inquiry, the Commission may:

  • Recommend payment of compensation or damages to the victim or family members.
  • Recommend initiation of prosecution or other suitable action against the persons responsible.
  • Recommend interim relief to the victim or family.
  • Approach the Supreme Court or a High Court for appropriate directions, orders or writs.
  • Recommend further action to the concerned government or authority.

The government or authority concerned is required to indicate its comments on the Commission's report and the action taken or proposed within the period prescribed by the Act.

The Commission's recommendations are generally not equivalent to binding judicial decrees. Their implementation depends on the statutory framework and the response of the concerned authorities.

9. Limitations of the NHRC

9.1 One-Year Limitation

Under Section 36(2), the Commission shall not inquire into a matter after the expiry of one year from the date on which the act constituting the alleged human rights violation is said to have been committed.

9.2 Armed Forces

In cases involving the armed forces, Section 19 provides a special procedure. The Commission may seek a report from the Central Government and, after considering the report, either refrain from proceeding further or make recommendations to the government.

9.3 Recommendatory Character

The Commission generally makes recommendations rather than issuing enforceable judicial orders. This can affect the effectiveness of remedies where authorities do not act on its recommendations.

9.4 Jurisdictional Limitations

The Commission's jurisdiction is governed by the Protection of Human Rights Act. It is not a substitute for ordinary courts, criminal investigation agencies or other statutory authorities.

10. Relationship with State Human Rights Commissions

The Protection of Human Rights Act also provides for State Human Rights Commissions. These bodies address human rights matters within their respective State-level jurisdiction.

The NHRC and State Commissions form part of a broader institutional framework. Their jurisdiction and the handling of matters are subject to the provisions of the Act, including restrictions relating to matters already being examined by another commission.

11. NHRC and International Standards

National human rights institutions are assessed internationally with reference to the Paris Principles, adopted by the United Nations General Assembly in 1993.

These principles emphasise:

  • Independence from government interference.
  • A broad human rights mandate.
  • A pluralistic composition.
  • Adequate powers and resources.
  • Accessibility and cooperation with civil society.

The NHRC's structure and functioning are often examined in the context of these international standards.
