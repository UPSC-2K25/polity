National Development Council (NDC)

The National Development Council was an apex forum for deliberations on national development and planning, bringing together the Union Government, State Governments and the Planning Commission.

1. Introduction

The National Development Council (NDC) was established on 6 August 1952 by an executive resolution of the Government of India. It was created to strengthen cooperation between the Union and States in the formulation and implementation of national development plans.

  • Established: 6 August 1952.
  • Nature: Non-constitutional and non-statutory executive body.
  • Created by: Executive resolution of the Government of India.
  • Chairperson: Prime Minister of India.
  • Primary role: Deliberation and coordination on national development planning.

The NDC was closely associated with India's Five-Year Plan framework and functioned as an important forum for Centre-State consultation on development policy.

2. Background and Evolution

After Independence, India adopted planned economic development as an important instrument for reconstruction, industrialisation and social transformation.

The Planning Commission was established in 1950 to formulate development plans. The NDC was subsequently created in 1952 to provide a broader forum for discussion and coordination involving the States.

Its institutional significance arose from the need to:

  • Build national consensus on development priorities.
  • Associate State Governments with national planning.
  • Review the implementation of development plans.
  • Coordinate efforts between the Union and States.

3. Objectives of NDC

  • Secure cooperation between the Union and State Governments in national development.
  • Strengthen and mobilise national efforts and resources.
  • Promote common economic policies across the country.
  • Ensure balanced and rapid development of all parts of India.
  • Build consensus on national plans and development priorities.
  • Review progress and discuss problems arising during plan implementation.

4. Composition of NDC

The NDC was designed as a high-level forum representing the Union and State Governments.

Its composition included:

  • The Prime Minister of India as Chairperson.
  • Chief Ministers of all States.
  • Chief Ministers or representatives of Union Territories, as provided in its institutional arrangements.
  • Members of the Union Cabinet.
  • Members of the Planning Commission.

The broad composition enabled national development issues to be discussed by political and administrative leadership from different levels of government.

5. Functions of NDC

A. Review of National Plans

  • Considered the draft national plans prepared by the Planning Commission.
  • Discussed development priorities and broad policy objectives.
  • Reviewed the progress of national development plans.

B. Centre-State Coordination

  • Provided a forum for consultation between the Union and States.
  • Discussed problems affecting the implementation of national plans.
  • Encouraged cooperation in the mobilisation of resources.

C. Resource Mobilisation

  • Discussed measures to strengthen national development efforts.
  • Encouraged efficient use of available financial and material resources.
  • Considered the resource requirements of development programmes.

D. Balanced Regional Development

  • Discussed measures to reduce regional disparities.
  • Promoted attention to less-developed areas.
  • Encouraged more balanced development across States.

6. Role in the Planning Process

The NDC played an important consultative role in the preparation and review of Five-Year Plans.

The broad planning process involved:

  1. The Planning Commission prepared the draft plan.
  2. The draft was discussed and considered through the national planning framework, including consultation with States.
  3. The NDC provided a forum for deliberation and consensus-building on the plan.
  4. The plan was placed before the appropriate governmental institutions for approval and implementation.
  5. Progress and implementation issues were reviewed through the planning framework.

The NDC was an important forum for deliberation, but it was not a constitutional legislature or an independent fund-allocation authority.

7. NDC and Indian Federalism

The NDC was an important institution in the evolution of cooperative federalism in India.

Its federal role included:

  • Bringing Chief Ministers into national development discussions.
  • Providing opportunities for States to express regional priorities.
  • Encouraging coordination in the implementation of national plans.
  • Promoting dialogue on resource mobilisation and development challenges.
  • Building consensus on national economic and social objectives.

However, its role was primarily consultative and deliberative. It did not replace the constitutional distribution of powers between the Union and States.

The Planning Commission and NDC had complementary roles in the earlier national planning system.

9. Limitations of NDC

  • Non-statutory character: It was established by executive resolution and did not derive its authority from a dedicated Act of Parliament.
  • Consultative role: Its deliberations did not have the force of legislation.
  • Centralised planning environment: The broader planning system was often criticised for insufficient flexibility and excessive central direction.
  • Implementation challenges: Decisions and recommendations depended on action by the relevant governments and departments.
  • Changing economic conditions: The shift towards a more liberalised and market-oriented economy changed the requirements of national planning.
  • Institutional overlap: Its functions were closely connected with those of the Planning Commission and other intergovernmental forums.

10. Changes After the Establishment of NITI Aayog

The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015. This marked a major change in the institutional framework for national policy planning.

The earlier Five-Year Plan system came to an end, and the Government adopted a more flexible approach to strategic policy formulation.

The NDC's role was tied to the earlier planning framework. It has not functioned as the regular national planning forum in the post-Planning Commission institutional arrangement.

NITI Aayog's Governing Council provides a contemporary platform for Centre-State consultation on development priorities. However, the Governing Council and the former NDC are distinct institutions.

12. Significance in Indian Polity

  • It provided an institutional forum for Centre-State dialogue on economic development.
  • It helped build consensus on national development priorities.
  • It brought State leadership into the national planning process.
  • It encouraged discussion of regional disparities and resource requirements.
  • It represented an important stage in the evolution of cooperative federalism.

13. Prelims Facts

  • The NDC was established on 6 August 1952.
  • It was created through an executive resolution.
  • It was neither a constitutional nor a statutory body.
  • The Prime Minister was its Chairperson.
  • Chief Ministers of States were members.
  • It was associated with the Five-Year Plan framework.
  • It provided a forum for Centre-State consultation on national development.
  • It was closely associated with the Planning Commission.
  • NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015.
