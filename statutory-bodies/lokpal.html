Statutory Bodies

Lokpal

The Lokpal is a statutory anti-corruption institution at the Union level, established to inquire into allegations of corruption against specified public functionaries.

1. Introduction

The Lokpal is an independent statutory body established under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013. It is intended to provide an institutional mechanism for receiving and examining complaints of corruption against certain public functionaries of the Union Government.

The institution of an ombudsman originated in Sweden. The term "Lokpal" is derived from the idea of a protector of the people. At the Union level, the Lokpal performs an anti-corruption oversight role, while the Act also provides for the establishment of Lokayuktas in the states.

The Lokpal is not a constitutional body. Its legal status, composition, jurisdiction and powers are governed primarily by the 2013 Act.

2. Background and Evolution

  • 1960s: The idea of an ombudsman for India gained prominence through administrative reform discussions.
  • 1966: The First Administrative Reforms Commission recommended the establishment of institutions of Lokpal at the Union level and Lokayukta in the states.
  • 1968: The first Lokpal Bill was introduced in the Lok Sabha. It was passed by the Lok Sabha but lapsed with the dissolution of the House.
  • Subsequent decades: Several Lokpal Bills were introduced, but a permanent institution was not established.
  • 2011: The India Against Corruption movement brought renewed public attention to the demand for an effective anti-corruption ombudsman.
  • 2013: Parliament enacted the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013, which received presidential assent in January 2014.
  • 2019: The first Chairperson of the Lokpal was appointed.

3. Legal Framework

The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 provides for the establishment of the Lokpal for the Union and Lokayuktas for states. It also lays down provisions relating to appointments, jurisdiction, inquiries, investigations, prosecution and other institutional arrangements.

The Act came into force on 16 January 2014. It works alongside other anti-corruption laws, particularly the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 and the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946.

The Lokpal does not replace the criminal justice system. Its role is to facilitate and supervise the statutory process for dealing with corruption complaints within its jurisdiction.

4. Composition

The Lokpal consists of a Chairperson and a maximum of eight Members.

Position Composition
Chairperson A person who is or has been the Chief Justice of India, a person who is or has been a Judge of the Supreme Court, or an eminent person meeting the qualifications prescribed by the Act.
Members Up to eight Members, of whom 50% must be Judicial Members.
Social representation At least 50% of the Members must be from among SCs, STs, OBCs, minorities and women.

A Judicial Member must be or have been a Judge of the Supreme Court or a Chief Justice of a High Court. Non-Judicial Members must fulfil the prescribed requirements relating to expertise and experience in areas such as anti-corruption policy, public administration, vigilance, finance, law and management.

The composition requirements are intended to combine judicial experience, professional expertise and representation of different sections of society.

5. Appointment of the Chairperson and Members

The President appoints the Chairperson and Members on the recommendation of a Selection Committee.

Selection Committee

Member Position
Prime Minister Chairperson
Speaker of the Lok Sabha Member
Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha Member
Chief Justice of India or a Supreme Court Judge nominated by the Chief Justice Member
Eminent jurist nominated by the President on the basis of the recommendations of the other members of the Selection Committee Member

For the purposes of the Act, the Leader of Opposition includes the leader of the single largest opposition party in the Lok Sabha where no opposition party has been recognised as having the status of Leader of Opposition.

Search Committee

The Selection Committee may constitute a Search Committee to prepare a panel of eligible candidates. The Search Committee must include at least 50% persons belonging to SCs, STs, OBCs, minorities and women. The Selection Committee is not bound to select only from the panel recommended by the Search Committee.

6. Tenure and Conditions of Service

  • The Chairperson and Members hold office for a term of five years from the date on which they enter upon their office or until they attain the age of 70 years, whichever is earlier.
  • They are not eligible for reappointment.
  • After ceasing to hold office, the Chairperson and Members are subject to restrictions on further employment as prescribed by the Act.
  • Their salaries, allowances and other conditions of service are governed by the Act.
  • Their conditions of service cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment.

7. Removal of the Chairperson and Members

The President may remove the Chairperson or a Member on the grounds and through the procedure prescribed under the Act.

  • Proved misbehaviour or incapacity: Removal requires an inquiry by the Supreme Court following a reference made by the President. The President may remove the office-holder if the Supreme Court reports that the person ought to be removed.
  • Other specified grounds: The Act provides for removal in circumstances such as insolvency, engaging in paid employment outside official duties, infirmity of mind or body, and conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude.
  • Conflict of interest: The Act also addresses circumstances in which a Chairperson or Member is concerned or interested in a contract or agreement involving the government.

The statutory safeguards are intended to protect the independence of the institution while providing a legal mechanism for removal in specified circumstances.

8. Jurisdiction of the Lokpal

The jurisdiction of the Lokpal extends to specified categories of public functionaries under the Union Government. The Act also covers certain entities that receive government assistance or are involved in activities of public importance, subject to the conditions and exclusions laid down in the law.

Persons covered

  • The Prime Minister, subject to the special conditions and exclusions in the Act.
  • Union Ministers.
  • Members of Parliament.
  • Group A, B, C and D officers and officials of the Central Government.
  • Persons serving in or associated with bodies, boards, corporations, authorities, companies, societies, trusts and other organisations covered by the Act.

Special provisions concerning the Prime Minister

The Lokpal may inquire into allegations against the Prime Minister, but the Act provides special safeguards. Matters relating to international relations, external and internal security, public order, atomic energy and space are excluded from the Lokpal's inquiry jurisdiction in relation to the Prime Minister.

An inquiry against the Prime Minister requires consideration by the full Bench of the Lokpal and approval by at least two-thirds of its Members. Such proceedings are to be held in camera. If the Lokpal dismisses the complaint, the records of the inquiry are not to be published or made available to anyone.

Members of Parliament

The Act contains an exclusion concerning anything said or a vote given by a Member of Parliament in Parliament or a parliamentary committee, reflecting the protection provided under Article 105 of the Constitution.

9. Complaint and Inquiry Mechanism

The Lokpal receives complaints alleging offences punishable under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, against public functionaries within its jurisdiction.

The broad process under the Act includes the following stages:

  1. Receipt of complaint: A complaint is received in the prescribed manner and examined for maintainability and jurisdiction.
  2. Preliminary inquiry: Where appropriate, the Lokpal orders a preliminary inquiry through its Inquiry Wing or refers the matter to an agency authorised under the Act.
  3. Investigation: Where the preliminary inquiry indicates a case for further action, the Lokpal may order an investigation by an appropriate agency, including the Central Bureau of Investigation, as permitted by law.
  4. Consideration of the report: The Lokpal considers the inquiry or investigation report and gives the concerned public servant an opportunity to respond in accordance with the Act.
  5. Further action: Depending on the findings, the Lokpal may take steps such as authorising prosecution, recommending departmental action or directing other action permitted by the statute.

The Act prescribes timelines and procedural safeguards for different stages. The exact procedure depends on the nature of the complaint and the category of public servant involved.

10. Powers and Functions

The Lokpal has powers and functions designed to enable it to examine corruption complaints and ensure that appropriate action is taken.

  • Receive and examine complaints of corruption against public functionaries covered by the Act.
  • Order preliminary inquiries and investigations in accordance with the statutory procedure.
  • Refer matters to the Central Vigilance Commission or other authorised agencies where the Act so provides.
  • Exercise superintendence over, and give directions to, the CBI in respect of cases referred to it by the Lokpal, subject to the Act.
  • Approve or authorise prosecution in cases where the statutory requirements are fulfilled.
  • Recommend or direct appropriate departmental action as permitted by law.
  • Exercise powers of a civil court for specified purposes during an inquiry.
  • Recommend measures for preventing corruption and improving vigilance systems.

11. Inquiry Wing and Investigation Agencies

Inquiry Wing

The Act provides for an Inquiry Wing to conduct preliminary inquiries into offences alleged to have been committed by public servants within the Lokpal's jurisdiction. The Wing functions under the administrative control of the Lokpal.

Investigation

The Lokpal may direct an investigation by an agency, including the CBI, in accordance with the Act. The agency conducting the investigation must follow the applicable criminal procedure and other relevant laws.

Relationship with the CBI

Where the Lokpal refers a case to the CBI, the Act provides for the Lokpal's superintendence over the CBI in respect of that case and for directions concerning the investigation. This does not mean that the Lokpal exercises general administrative control over the CBI in all its cases.

Relationship with the CVC

The Central Vigilance Commission has an important role in the preliminary inquiry and vigilance process concerning specified categories of Central Government public servants. The Lokpal may refer complaints to the CVC in accordance with the statutory framework. The CVC and Lokpal therefore have distinct but complementary roles.

12. Prosecution Wing and Special Courts

The Act provides for a Prosecution Wing to conduct prosecution in cases arising from matters investigated under the Lokpal framework. The Lokpal may take steps for prosecution where the statutory requirements are met.

The Act also provides for the designation of Special Courts to deal with cases arising from offences within its scope. These arrangements are intended to support timely adjudication of corruption cases.

The Lokpal itself is not a criminal court that determines guilt and imposes criminal punishment. Criminal liability is determined by the competent court through the prescribed judicial process.

13. Other Important Provisions

  • Declaration of assets: The Act contains provisions relating to declarations of assets and liabilities by public servants covered by the law.
  • Protection against victimisation: The Act provides for safeguards concerning complaints and actions taken against persons who make complaints or assist in inquiries, subject to the applicable legal framework.
  • Confiscation of assets: The Act provides for attachment and confiscation of assets alleged to have been acquired through corrupt means, subject to the prescribed legal process.
  • Annual reports: The Lokpal is required to prepare reports on its activities and submit them to the President. The reports are laid before Parliament in accordance with the Act.
  • Transparency and accountability: The institution is subject to statutory reporting, procedural requirements and financial accountability provisions.

14. Lokpal and Lokayukta: Distinction

Basis Lokpal Lokayukta
Level Union level State level
Legal framework Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 State Lokayukta laws, within the framework of the 2013 Act
Jurisdiction Specified public functionaries and entities under the Union Government Specified public functionaries and entities under the respective state law

Institutional framework Common statutory framework under the 2013 Act Structure, powers and procedures may differ among states

15. Significance

  • Provides an institutional mechanism to examine corruption complaints against specified public functionaries.
  • Strengthens accountability in public administration.
  • Promotes integrity and transparency in government institutions.
  • Creates a statutory framework for coordination among inquiry, vigilance and investigation agencies.
  • Provides a formal channel through which citizens can raise allegations of corruption within the Lokpal's jurisdiction.
  • Complements other anti-corruption institutions and laws.

16. Challenges and Limitations

  • Institutional capacity: Effective functioning depends on adequate staffing, expertise, infrastructure and resources.
  • Procedural delays: Preliminary inquiries, investigations and prosecution may take considerable time.
  • Coordination: The Lokpal depends on effective coordination with agencies such as the CBI and CVC.
  • Jurisdictional limits: The Act covers specified public functionaries and entities; it is not a general forum for every grievance or allegation of misconduct.
  • Implementation: The effectiveness of the institution depends on appointments, operational independence and consistent enforcement of the law.
  • Public awareness: Citizens need clear information about the Lokpal's jurisdiction, complaint process and limitations.

17. Measures to Strengthen the Lokpal

  • Ensure timely appointments and adequate institutional capacity.
  • Provide sufficient financial, administrative and investigative resources.
  • Strengthen coordination with the CBI, CVC and other relevant agencies.
  • Improve transparency in complaint handling and publication of institutional performance data, consistent with legal safeguards.
  • Ensure timely preliminary inquiries, investigations and prosecution.
  • Protect the independence and impartiality of the institution.
  • Increase public awareness about the complaint mechanism and its jurisdiction.

19. Important Facts for Prelims

  • The Lokpal is a statutory body, not a constitutional body.
  • It is established under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013.
  • The Act came into force on 16 January 2014.
  • The Lokpal consists of a Chairperson and a maximum of eight Members.
  • At least 50% of the Members must be Judicial Members.
  • At least 50% of the Members must be from SCs, STs, OBCs, minorities and women.
  • The Chairperson and Members are appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Selection Committee.
  • The term of office is five years or until the age of 70 years, whichever is earlier.
  • The Lokpal's jurisdiction includes the Prime Minister, subject to special exclusions and safeguards.
  • The Lokpal and the CVC are separate statutory institutions.
  • The Lokpal can exercise superintendence over the CBI in cases referred to it, as provided by the Act.
  • The Lokpal does not itself function as a criminal court.

20. Mains Perspective

Role of the Lokpal in strengthening accountability

The Lokpal represents an effort to strengthen institutional accountability by creating a statutory mechanism for examining corruption allegations against specified public functionaries. Its significance lies in providing an independent forum, enabling coordination with investigative agencies and reinforcing public confidence in governance.

Issues affecting effectiveness

Its effectiveness depends on timely appointments, institutional capacity, investigative independence, coordination with other agencies and timely completion of proceedings. The existence of a statutory institution alone cannot eliminate corruption without wider administrative reforms, transparency and effective enforcement.

Way forward

The Lokpal should be supported by adequate resources, transparent procedures, professional investigation, strong safeguards against political interference and effective coordination with vigilance and law-enforcement institutions. It should complement broader reforms in public administration and the criminal justice system.

21. Practice Questions

Prelims

  1. The Lokpal is established under which legislation?

    Answer: Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013.

  2. Who appoints the Chairperson and Members of the Lokpal?

    Answer: The President of India, on the recommendation of the Selection Committee.

  3. What is the maximum number of Members, excluding the Chairperson, in the Lokpal?

    Answer: Eight.

  4. Is the Lokpal a constitutional body?

    Answer: No. It is a statutory body.

  5. Does the Lokpal have jurisdiction over the Prime Minister?

    Answer: Yes, subject to the special exclusions and safeguards prescribed by the Act.

Mains

  1. Examine the role of the Lokpal in strengthening accountability and transparency in public administration.
  2. Discuss the institutional and operational challenges affecting the effectiveness of the Lokpal.
  3. Differentiate between the Lokpal and the Central Vigilance Commission.
  4. Suggest measures to strengthen India's institutional framework for combating corruption.
