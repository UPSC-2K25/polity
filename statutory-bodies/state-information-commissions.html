State Information Commissions

State Information Commissions are statutory institutions established under the Right to Information Act, 2005. They perform an important role in enforcing the right to information in relation to State public authorities. They provide an independent statutory forum for complaints and second appeals and help promote transparency and accountability in State administration.

The Act establishes a Central Information Commission and State Information Commissions. Their jurisdiction is determined by the public authority and the appropriate Government under the Act, rather than merely by the geographical location of an office.

1. Establishment and Legal Basis

State Information Commissions are constituted under Section 15 of the Right to Information Act, 2005. The provisions concerning their term, service conditions and removal are contained in Sections 16 and 17.

Nature Statutory body
Establishing law Right to Information Act, 2005
Constitution Section 15
Term and service conditions Section 16 and the RTI Rules, 2019
Removal Section 17
Principal role Complaints, second appeals and enforcement of the RTI Act within its jurisdiction

2. Constitution of the State Information Commission

Under Section 15(1), every State Government shall, by notification in the Official Gazette, constitute a body to be known as the Information Commission of that State.

2.1 Composition

Under Section 15(2), the State Information Commission consists of:

  • One State Chief Information Commissioner (SCIC).
  • Such number of State Information Commissioners (SICs), not exceeding ten, as may be deemed necessary.

Therefore, the maximum composition is one State Chief Information Commissioner and ten State Information Commissioners.

2.2 Appointment

Under Section 15(3), the State Chief Information Commissioner and State Information Commissioners are appointed by the Governor on the recommendation of a selection committee.

Member of the selection committee Position
Chief Minister Chairperson
Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly Member
A Cabinet Minister nominated by the Chief Minister Member

Where the Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly has not been recognised as such, the leader of the single largest group in opposition to the Government in that Assembly is deemed to be the Leader of Opposition for this purpose.

The selection committee recommends appointments. The formal appointment is made by the Governor.

2.3 Qualifications

Under Section 15(5), the State Chief Information Commissioner and State Information Commissioners must be persons of eminence in public life with wide knowledge and experience in one or more of the following fields:

  • Law
  • Science and technology
  • Social service
  • Management
  • Journalism
  • Mass media
  • Administration and governance

2.4 Restrictions on holding office

Section 15(6) provides that the State Chief Information Commissioner or a State Information Commissioner shall not:

  • Be a Member of Parliament.
  • Be a member of the Legislature of any State or Union Territory.
  • Hold any other office of profit.
  • Be connected with any political party.
  • Carry on any business.
  • Pursue any profession.

2.5 Headquarters and offices

Under Section 15(7), the headquarters of the State Information Commission is at the place specified by the State Government through notification in the Official Gazette.

The Commission may establish offices at other places within the State with the previous approval of the State Government.

2.6 Autonomy and administration

Under Section 15(4), the general superintendence, direction and management of the affairs of the Commission vest in the State Chief Information Commissioner.

The State Chief Information Commissioner is assisted by the State Information Commissioners. The provision enables the Commission to exercise its statutory powers autonomously without being subjected to directions by any other authority under the Act.

3. Term of Office and Conditions of Service

Section 16 governs the term of office, resignation and service conditions of the State Chief Information Commissioner and State Information Commissioners. The Right to Information (Amendment) Act, 2019 changed the statutory framework for tenure and service conditions.

3.1 Term of office

Section 16 provides for a term prescribed by the Central Government. Rule 12 of the Right to Information Rules, 2019 prescribes a term of three years from the date on which the State Chief Information Commissioner or State Information Commissioner enters upon office.

Office Term and age limit
State Chief Information Commissioner Three years under Rule 12, subject to the statutory ceiling of 65 years of age
State Information Commissioner Three years under Rule 12 or until attaining 65 years of age, whichever is earlier

3.2 Reappointment and appointment as SCIC

  • The State Chief Information Commissioner is not eligible for reappointment to that office.
  • A State Information Commissioner is not eligible for reappointment as a State Information Commissioner.
  • A State Information Commissioner is eligible for appointment as the State Chief Information Commissioner through the procedure specified in Section 15(3).
  • Where a State Information Commissioner is appointed as State Chief Information Commissioner, the aggregate term in both offices cannot exceed five years.

3.3 Oath or affirmation

Section 16(3) expressly requires the State Chief Information Commissioner and every State Information Commissioner to make and subscribe an oath or affirmation before entering office.

The oath or affirmation is made before the Governor or a person appointed by the Governor for this purpose, according to the form set out in the First Schedule to the Act.

3.4 Resignation

Under Section 16(4), the State Chief Information Commissioner or a State Information Commissioner may resign at any time by writing under their hand addressed to the Governor.

3.5 Salaries and conditions of service

Following the 2019 amendment, Section 16(5) provides that salaries, allowances and other conditions of service are prescribed by the Central Government. These conditions cannot be varied to the disadvantage of a commissioner after appointment.

Rule 14 of the RTI Rules, 2019 prescribes the pay of the State Chief Information Commissioner and State Information Commissioners. The Rules also address other service conditions, including allowances, leave, medical facilities and accommodation.

The Act contains a transitional protection for commissioners appointed before the commencement of the 2019 Amendment: they continue to be governed by the earlier applicable provisions and rules.

3.6 Staff and administrative support

Under Section 16(6), the State Government shall provide the State Chief Information Commissioner and State Information Commissioners with the officers and employees necessary for the efficient performance of their functions.

4. Removal of the State Chief Information Commissioner and State Information Commissioners

Section 17 provides the procedure and grounds for removal. The formal authority to remove a State Chief Information Commissioner or State Information Commissioner is the Governor.

4.1 Removal on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity

Under Section 17(1), the Governor may remove a commissioner on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity only after the Supreme Court, on a reference made by the Governor, has conducted an inquiry and reported that the commissioner ought to be removed on that ground.

During the inquiry, the Governor may suspend the concerned commissioner and, where considered necessary, prohibit the commissioner from attending office until the Governor passes orders after receiving the Supreme Court's report.

4.2 Other grounds for removal

Under Section 17(3), the Governor may remove a commissioner by order on specified grounds, including:

  • Being adjudged insolvent.
  • Having been convicted of an offence which, in the opinion of the Governor, involves moral turpitude.
  • Engaging during the term of office in paid employment outside the duties of the office.
  • Being, in the opinion of the Governor, unfit to continue in office by reason of infirmity of mind or body.
  • Having acquired a financial or other interest likely to prejudicially affect the functions of the office.

4.3 Deemed misbehaviour

Under Section 17(4), a commissioner is deemed guilty of misbehaviour for the purposes of Section 17(1) if directly or indirectly concerned or interested in a contract or agreement made by or on behalf of the State Government, or participates in its profit, benefit or emoluments in the manner specified in the provision.

5. Powers and Functions under Section 18

Section 18 deals with the powers and functions of Information Commissions. It applies to both the Central Information Commission and State Information Commissions, according to their respective jurisdictions.

5.1 Receiving and inquiring into complaints

Under Section 18(1), the State Information Commission has a duty to receive and inquire into complaints from persons who are unable to exercise their right to information in the circumstances specified by the Act.

These include situations where:

  • No State Public Information Officer has been appointed.
  • A State Assistant Public Information Officer refuses to accept an application or appeal for forwarding it to the appropriate authority.
  • A person has been refused access to information requested under the Act.
  • No response has been given within the prescribed time limit.
  • An unreasonable fee has been demanded.
  • Incomplete, misleading or false information has been supplied.
  • Any other matter relating to requesting or obtaining access to records under the Act has arisen.

Under Section 18(2), the Commission may initiate an inquiry where it is satisfied that there are reasonable grounds to inquire into the matter.

5.2 Powers of a civil court

While inquiring into a complaint under Section 18, the Commission has the same powers as are vested in a civil court while trying a suit under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, in respect of specified matters.

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of persons and compelling them to give oral or written evidence on oath and produce documents or things.
  • Requiring the discovery and inspection of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavit.
  • Requisitioning public records or copies from any court or office.
  • Issuing summons for examination of witnesses or documents.
  • Other matters that may be prescribed.

5.3 Inspection of records

Under Section 18(4), the Commission may examine any record to which the Act applies and which is under the control of a public authority during an inquiry into a complaint. Such a record cannot be withheld from the Commission on any ground.

6. Appellate Jurisdiction under Section 19

The State Information Commission acts as the second appellate authority under Section 19. The appeal mechanism is separate from the complaint-inquiry mechanism under Section 18.

6.1 First appeal

Under Section 19(1), a person who does not receive a decision within the time prescribed under Section 7, or who is aggrieved by a decision of the State Public Information Officer, may file a first appeal before an officer senior in rank to that State Public Information Officer in the same public authority.

The first appeal is ordinarily to be filed within 30 days from the expiry of the response period or receipt of the decision. The appellate officer may admit a delayed appeal if satisfied that sufficient cause prevented timely filing.

6.2 Second appeal

Under Section 19(3), a second appeal lies before the State Information Commission against the decision under Section 19(1). It must ordinarily be filed within 90 days from the date on which the decision should have been made or was actually received.

The Commission may admit a second appeal after the expiry of 90 days if it is satisfied that sufficient cause prevented the appellant from filing it within the prescribed period.

6.3 Disposal of appeals

Under Section 19(6), an appeal under Section 19(1) or Section 19(2) must ordinarily be disposed of within 30 days of receipt. The period may be extended to a maximum of 45 days from the date of filing, for reasons recorded in writing.

Under Section 19(7), the decision of the Information Commission is binding under the Act.

6.4 Powers while deciding an appeal

Section 19(8) empowers the Commission, in its decision in an appeal, to require a public authority to take steps necessary to secure compliance with the Act. These may include:

  • Providing access to information in a particular form.
  • Appointing a State Public Information Officer where required.
  • Publishing specified information or categories of information.
  • Making necessary changes to record maintenance, management and destruction practices.
  • Enhancing training on the right to information for officials.
  • Providing an annual report in compliance with Section 4(1)(b).

The Commission may also require the public authority to compensate the complainant for loss or other detriment suffered, impose penalties under the Act, or reject the application.

7. Penalties under Section 20

Section 20 provides for penalties against a State Public Information Officer where the statutory conditions are satisfied.

The Commission may impose a penalty where, without reasonable cause, the officer:

  • Refuses to receive an application for information.
  • Fails to furnish information within the time specified under Section 7(1).
  • Malafidely denies a request for information.
  • Knowingly gives incorrect, incomplete or misleading information.
  • Destroys information that was the subject of a request.
  • Obstructs the furnishing of information in any manner.

7.1 Amount of penalty

Daily penalty ₹250 for each day
Maximum penalty ₹25,000
Hearing The concerned officer must be given a reasonable opportunity of being heard before a penalty is imposed.
Burden of proof The burden of proving that the officer acted reasonably and diligently is on the officer.

7.2 Recommendation for disciplinary action

Under Section 20(2), where the Commission finds that a Public Information Officer has, without reasonable cause and persistently, committed the specified failures, it shall recommend disciplinary action under the service rules applicable to that officer.

8. Monitoring and Annual Reporting

Section 25 provides for monitoring and reporting on the implementation of the RTI Act.

The State Information Commission is required to collect and consider information about the implementation of the Act by public authorities within its jurisdiction and prepare an annual report for the relevant year.

The report includes information such as:

  • The number of requests received by each public authority.
  • The number of decisions rejecting requests and the provisions under which they were rejected.
  • The number of appeals and complaints received and their outcomes.
  • Details of disciplinary action taken against officers under the Act.
  • The amount of fees and charges collected under the Act.
  • Measures taken to implement the spirit and requirements of the Act.
  • Recommendations for reform and improvement in the implementation of the Act.

Under Section 25(4), the appropriate Government shall cause the annual report of the Information Commission to be laid before each House of Parliament or the State Legislature, as the case may be.

10. Complaint and Appeal: Important Distinction

Sections 18 and 19 provide different statutory mechanisms. They should not be treated as interchangeable.

Section 18 Section 19
Deals with complaints. Deals with appeals.
Provides for inquiry into specified difficulties in exercising the right to information. Provides a first appeal and a second appeal.
Includes powers of a civil court for specified inquiry-related purposes. Provides appellate powers and directions for securing compliance with the Act.
Section 18(4) permits examination of relevant records during a complaint inquiry. Section 19(8) specifies powers available in an appellate decision.

UPSC point: The Commission's complaint-inquiry jurisdiction under Section 18 must be distinguished from its appellate jurisdiction and remedial powers under Section 19. The statutory basis for directions such as providing access to information is specifically set out in Section 19(8).

11. Significance of State Information Commissions

  • Administrative transparency: They help citizens obtain information about the functioning and decisions of State public authorities.
  • Accountability: Their complaint, appeal and penalty-related functions encourage compliance with the RTI Act.
  • Citizen empowerment: They provide a statutory mechanism through which citizens can challenge failures to provide information.
  • Participatory democracy: Access to public information enables citizens to make informed interventions in public affairs.
  • Improved governance: Their monitoring and reporting functions can identify weaknesses in record management and disclosure practices.
  • Decentralised enforcement: They provide a State-level institutional mechanism for enforcing the right to information.

12. Challenges in Functioning

  • Vacancies: Delays in appointments can reduce the capacity of commissions to hear cases.
  • Pending appeals: A high volume of pending matters can delay access to information.
  • Administrative capacity: Inadequate staffing and infrastructure may affect disposal of complaints and appeals.
  • Compliance with decisions: Effective implementation of Commission decisions is necessary for meaningful access to information.
  • Awareness: Citizens may not know how to file applications, complaints or appeals.
  • Record management: Poor maintenance and digitisation of records can make timely disclosure difficult.
  • Institutional autonomy: Timely appointments and adequate administrative support are important for effective functioning.

13. Important Amendments and Current Legal Position

Right to Information (Amendment) Act, 2019

The 2019 Amendment changed Sections 13 and 16 of the RTI Act, which deal respectively with the Central and State Information Commissions.

Before the amendment, the Act itself specified a five-year term and linked certain service conditions to other constitutional offices. The amended framework authorises the Central Government to prescribe the term, salaries, allowances and other service conditions through rules.

The RTI Rules, 2019 prescribe a three-year term for State Chief Information Commissioners and State Information Commissioners under Rule 12.

The 2019 Amendment did not remove the requirement of oath or affirmation under Section 16(3). Nor did it remove the age ceiling of 65 years or the statutory removal procedure under Section 17.

15. Important Distinctions for UPSC

  • State Information Commissions are statutory bodies, not constitutional bodies.
  • They are constituted by State Governments under Section 15 of the RTI Act.
  • The Governor appoints the commissioners, but the selection committee is chaired by the Chief Minister.
  • The Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Assembly is part of the selection committee; the Act does not specify the Leader of Opposition in the Legislative Council for this purpose.
  • The Commission may have up to ten State Information Commissioners in addition to the State Chief Information Commissioner.
  • The term is three years under Rule 12 of the 2019 Rules, subject to the statutory age ceiling.
  • The oath or affirmation requirement continues under Section 16(3).
  • Section 18 deals with complaints; Section 19 deals with appeals.
  • Section 20 provides for penalties against Public Information Officers.
  • Section 25 deals with monitoring and annual reporting.
