Statutory Bodies

Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA)

PFRDA is the statutory regulator responsible for promoting, developing and regulating India's pension sector, including the National Pension System.

1. Introduction and Evolution

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) was initially established as an interim authority by the Central Government in 2003. It was given statutory status through the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 2013.

  • Interim establishment: 2003.
  • Statutory status: PFRDA Act, 2013.
  • Headquarters: New Delhi.
  • Nature: Statutory regulatory body.
  • Sector: Pension funds and pension systems within its statutory jurisdiction.

The creation of PFRDA was part of the reform process aimed at developing a sustainable, regulated and accessible pension system in India.

2. Objectives of PFRDA

The principal objectives of PFRDA include:

  • Promoting old-age income security through a regulated pension system.
  • Protecting the interests of subscribers to pension schemes covered by the Act.
  • Regulating pension funds and intermediaries.
  • Promoting the orderly growth and development of pension systems.
  • Encouraging wider pension coverage, including among workers in the unorganised sector.
  • Ensuring transparency, accountability and sound governance in pension management.

3. Composition and Organisation

Section 4 of the PFRDA Act, 2013, provides for the establishment and composition of the Authority.

PFRDA consists of:

  • A Chairperson.
  • Not more than six members.
  • At least three of the members must be whole-time members.

The Chairperson and members are appointed by the Central Government in accordance with the Act. They are required to possess prescribed expertise and experience in areas relevant to pension administration, finance, law, economics and related fields.

4. Functions of PFRDA

PFRDA performs regulatory, supervisory and developmental functions under the PFRDA Act, 2013.

A. Regulatory Functions

  • Regulates pension funds and other intermediaries covered by the Act.
  • Registers and regulates intermediaries within its statutory jurisdiction.
  • Prescribes standards for pension fund management.
  • Regulates investment of pension fund assets under the applicable framework.
  • Prescribes disclosure and reporting requirements.

B. Subscriber Protection

  • Protects the interests of subscribers to regulated pension schemes.
  • Promotes transparency in pension account management.
  • Prescribes grievance redressal arrangements.
  • Promotes fair treatment and appropriate disclosure to subscribers.

C. Developmental Functions

  • Promotes the development of pension markets.
  • Encourages wider pension coverage.
  • Promotes awareness about retirement planning and pension savings.
  • Encourages efficient administration and technological improvements.

5. Powers of PFRDA

PFRDA exercises powers conferred by the PFRDA Act, 2013, and regulations made under it.

  • Registration: Registers pension funds and other intermediaries covered by the Act.
  • Regulation: Frames regulations governing pension schemes and intermediaries.
  • Supervision: Calls for information, conducts inspections and monitors compliance.
  • Directions: Issues directions to regulated entities within its statutory authority.
  • Enforcement: Takes action against violations and imposes penalties where authorised by law.
  • Subscriber protection: Prescribes standards and mechanisms to protect subscriber interests.

6. National Pension System (NPS)

The National Pension System is a defined-contribution pension system designed to provide retirement income through individual pension accounts and regulated investment arrangements.

It was introduced for new Central Government employees, excluding the armed forces, joining service on or after 1 January 2004. It was subsequently extended to other categories of employees and citizens under the applicable framework.

Important features include:

  • Individual pension accounts for subscribers.
  • Contributions by subscribers and, where applicable, employers.
  • Investment of contributions through regulated pension funds.
  • Choice among permitted investment options, subject to scheme rules.
  • Accumulation of retirement savings over the contribution period.
  • Withdrawal and annuity arrangements governed by applicable rules.

NPS is market-linked and does not provide a guaranteed fixed return in the same manner as a defined-benefit pension.

7. Structure of the National Pension System

The NPS operates through a system of specialised intermediaries and record-keeping arrangements.

Institution or Intermediary Role
PFRDA Regulates and supervises the pension system and its intermediaries under the statutory framework.
National Pension System Trust Holds and administers the assets of the NPS trust for the benefit of subscribers, subject to the applicable framework.
Central Recordkeeping Agency Maintains subscriber records and individual pension account information.
Pension Funds Manage pension contributions and invest assets according to the permitted investment framework.
Points of Presence Provide subscriber-facing registration and service facilities.
Annuity Service Providers Provide annuity products and services in accordance with applicable rules.
Trustee Bank Facilitates the banking and settlement functions prescribed for the pension system.

8. Categories of NPS Subscribers

The NPS has been made available to different categories of subscribers under separate arrangements.

Government Sector

The NPS covers eligible Central Government and State Government employees under the respective arrangements, subject to applicable rules and notified exceptions.

All Citizen Model

The All Citizen Model allows eligible individuals to voluntarily join the NPS and build retirement savings.

Corporate Sector

Employers may adopt NPS for eligible employees under the corporate framework, with contributions and operational arrangements governed by applicable rules.

Atal Pension Yojana

Atal Pension Yojana (APY) is a government-backed pension scheme administered within the PFRDA framework. It primarily targets workers in the unorganised sector and eligible individuals seeking a defined pension benefit subject to scheme conditions.

9. Atal Pension Yojana (APY)

Atal Pension Yojana was launched in 2015 to encourage retirement savings, particularly among workers in the unorganised sector.

  • It provides a guaranteed minimum monthly pension at the age of 60, subject to eligibility and contribution conditions.
  • The subscriber chooses a permitted pension amount at enrolment.
  • Contributions depend on the subscriber's age at entry and the selected pension amount.
  • The spouse is entitled to the applicable survivor benefit after the subscriber's death.
  • After the death of both the subscriber and spouse, the accumulated pension wealth is payable to the nominee as prescribed.

Eligibility and enrolment conditions are determined by the scheme guidelines as amended from time to time. APY should be distinguished from the market-linked NPS.

10. Pension Funds and Investment Regulation

Pension funds manage contributions accumulated in subscribers' pension accounts. They invest these assets in accordance with investment guidelines and regulatory limits.

PFRDA regulates pension fund operations to promote:

  • Prudent investment management.
  • Diversification and risk management.
  • Transparency and accountability.
  • Protection of subscriber assets.
  • Disclosure of investment performance and relevant information.

Since NPS is market-linked, investment performance depends on market conditions and the permitted asset allocation framework.

11. Withdrawals and Exit from NPS

Withdrawal and exit rules determine how subscribers may access their accumulated pension wealth. The applicable conditions depend on the subscriber category, age, duration of subscription and relevant regulations.

The framework provides for different types of exits, including:

  • Normal exit on reaching the prescribed age or retirement point.
  • Premature exit subject to specified conditions.
  • Partial withdrawals for permitted purposes under the applicable rules.
  • Exit and benefit arrangements in the event of the subscriber's death.

At normal exit, the subscriber may withdraw the permitted lump-sum portion and use the prescribed portion of the accumulated pension wealth to purchase an annuity, subject to the applicable rules and thresholds.

Exact withdrawal percentages and thresholds are governed by current PFRDA regulations and scheme rules and should be checked in their latest form.

12. Subscriber Protection and Grievance Redressal

PFRDA seeks to protect subscribers by regulating intermediaries, requiring transparent account management and providing grievance redressal arrangements.

  • Prescribing standards for subscriber services.
  • Monitoring the conduct of pension intermediaries.
  • Requiring maintenance of subscriber records.
  • Providing mechanisms for raising and tracking grievances.
  • Promoting awareness about pension products and retirement planning.

Subscribers can raise grievances through the designated NPS grievance redressal system and the channels prescribed by the Authority.

14. Challenges Before PFRDA

  • Limited pension coverage: Expanding formal retirement savings among workers outside organised employment.
  • Awareness: Improving understanding of long-term pension planning and market-linked risks.
  • Investment risk: Balancing long-term returns with prudent risk management.
  • Financial inclusion: Making pension services accessible to low-income and remote communities.
  • Subscriber protection: Ensuring transparency, service quality and effective grievance resolution.
  • Longevity risk: Supporting adequate retirement income as life expectancy increases.
  • Digital security: Protecting subscriber data and pension account infrastructure.

15. Significance for Indian Governance

PFRDA contributes to India's social security and financial inclusion architecture. A regulated pension system can reduce old-age income insecurity, mobilise long-term savings and support the development of financial markets.

Its role is particularly significant in a country with a large workforce outside formal employer-based pension arrangements.

16. Prelims Facts

  • PFRDA was initially established as an interim authority in 2003.
  • It received statutory status under the PFRDA Act, 2013.
  • Its headquarters is in New Delhi.
  • Section 4 deals with the composition of the Authority.
  • PFRDA regulates the National Pension System.
  • NPS is a defined-contribution, market-linked pension system.
  • Atal Pension Yojana was launched in 2015.
  • PFRDA regulates pension funds and specified intermediaries.
  • PFRDA is a statutory body, not a constitutional body.
