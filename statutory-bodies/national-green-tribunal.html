Statutory Bodies

National Green Tribunal (NGT)

The National Green Tribunal is a specialised statutory judicial body established for the effective and expeditious disposal of cases relating to environmental protection, conservation of forests and other natural resources, and enforcement of environmental rights.

1. Introduction

The National Green Tribunal (NGT) is a specialised tribunal established under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010. It provides a dedicated forum for adjudicating civil cases involving substantial questions relating to the environment and for hearing appeals under specified environmental laws.

The Tribunal combines judicial expertise with scientific and technical knowledge. Its purpose is to improve the quality and speed of environmental adjudication and provide relief to persons affected by environmental damage.

The NGT is a statutory body, not a constitutional body. It functions independently within the framework of the NGT Act and other applicable laws.

2. Background and Evolution

  • Constitutional context: Environmental protection is connected with Article 21, Article 48A and Article 51A(g) of the Constitution.
  • Environmental legislation: A series of environmental laws created the need for specialised adjudication and effective enforcement.
  • Supreme Court interventions: Judicial decisions developed important environmental principles, including sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle.
  • Law Commission and policy discussions: The need for specialised environmental courts and tribunals gained recognition through legal and administrative reform discussions.
  • 2010: Parliament enacted the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.
  • 18 October 2010: The NGT was established and began functioning as a specialised environmental adjudicatory institution.

3. Legal Framework

The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 is the principal legislation governing the Tribunal. It provides for its establishment, composition, jurisdiction, powers, procedures and other institutional arrangements.

The Act aims to provide effective and expeditious disposal of cases relating to:

  • Environmental protection.
  • Conservation of forests and other natural resources.
  • Enforcement of legal rights relating to the environment.
  • Relief and compensation for damage to persons and property.
  • Restitution of damaged property and the environment.

The NGT exercises jurisdiction under the laws listed in the Schedule to the Act, subject to the scope and conditions of the relevant provisions.

4. Objectives

  • Provide specialised adjudication of environmental disputes.
  • Ensure effective and speedy disposal of environmental cases.
  • Promote environmental protection and conservation of natural resources.
  • Provide relief and compensation to victims of environmental damage.
  • Order restitution of damaged property and the environment.
  • Apply established environmental principles in decision-making.
  • Reduce the burden on ordinary courts by creating a specialised forum.

5. Composition

The NGT consists of a Chairperson, Judicial Members and Expert Members. The Act provides for a minimum and maximum strength, subject to the statutory framework.

Category Role and qualification
Chairperson Heads the Tribunal. Must be or have been a Judge of the Supreme Court or Chief Justice of a High Court, subject to the Act.
Judicial Members Bring judicial experience to the adjudication of environmental disputes and must satisfy the qualifications prescribed by the Act.
Expert Members Bring specialised knowledge and experience in areas such as environmental science, engineering, technology, administration or related fields.

The statutory framework provides for a minimum of 10 and a maximum of 20 full-time Judicial Members and an equal number of Expert Members, as prescribed by the Act and applicable rules.

The combination of judicial and technical expertise is a defining feature of the Tribunal.

6. Appointment

The Chairperson, Judicial Members and Expert Members are appointed by the Central Government in accordance with the NGT Act and applicable rules.

The selection process involves the prescribed committee and consultation arrangements. The statutory framework seeks to ensure that persons with appropriate judicial and technical qualifications are appointed.

The qualifications and appointment procedures differ according to the category of office. The Chairperson and Judicial Members require judicial experience, while Expert Members must satisfy the prescribed technical or administrative experience requirements.

7. Tenure and Conditions of Service

  • The Chairperson, Judicial Members and Expert Members hold office for the term prescribed under the NGT Act and applicable rules.
  • The statutory term is five years, subject to the prescribed age limit.
  • The Chairperson cannot continue beyond the age of 70 years.
  • Judicial Members and Expert Members cannot continue beyond the age of 67 years.
  • They are not eligible for reappointment under the statutory framework.
  • Salary, allowances and other conditions of service are governed by the Act and applicable rules.

8. Benches and Regional Structure

The principal place of sitting of the NGT is New Delhi. The Tribunal also has zonal benches to improve access to environmental justice across the country.

Bench Location
Principal Bench New Delhi
Central Zonal Bench Bhopal
Eastern Zonal Bench Kolkata
Southern Zonal Bench Chennai
Western Zonal Bench Pune

The Tribunal may hold sittings at other places in accordance with the applicable legal and administrative arrangements.

9. Jurisdiction

The NGT's jurisdiction is defined by the NGT Act. It primarily covers civil cases involving a substantial question relating to the environment, including enforcement of a legal right relating to the environment, where the question arises out of the implementation of an enactment specified in the Schedule to the Act.

Original jurisdiction

Under Section 14, the Tribunal has jurisdiction over civil cases where a substantial question relating to the environment is involved, including enforcement of a legal right relating to the environment, and the question arises out of the implementation of a scheduled enactment.

Relief and compensation

Under Section 15, the Tribunal may provide relief and compensation to victims of pollution and other environmental damage. It may also order restitution of damaged property and restitution of the environment for the areas it considers appropriate.

Appellate jurisdiction

Under Section 16, the Tribunal hears appeals against specified orders or decisions made under the environmental laws listed in that section.

10. Laws Covered by the NGT Act

The Schedule to the NGT Act identifies the environmental enactments to which the Tribunal's jurisdiction applies. The principal enactments include:

Legislation Subject
Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 Prevention and control of water pollution and maintenance or restoration of water quality.
Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Cess Act, 1977 Historical enactment relating to cess on water consumption; subsequently repealed.
Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980 Regulation of diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes; the legislation is now titled the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980.
Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 Prevention, control and abatement of air pollution.
Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 Protection and improvement of the environment.
Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 Immediate relief to persons affected by accidents involving hazardous substances.
Biological Diversity Act, 2002 Conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use and fair and equitable sharing of benefits.

The Schedule must be read with the relevant provisions of the NGT Act. The Tribunal does not acquire general jurisdiction over every dispute that has an environmental aspect.

11. Substantial Question Relating to the Environment

The expression "substantial question relating to the environment" is central to the Tribunal's original jurisdiction.

Under the NGT Act, it includes cases where there is a direct violation of a specific statutory environmental obligation by a person, or where the consequence of such violation affects the community at large or a group of individuals beyond the dispute between the parties.

It also includes cases involving environmental consequences resulting from a specific activity or point source of pollution, where the environmental damage is substantial, or where the environmental or public health impact is broadly measurable.

The statutory definition helps distinguish substantial environmental disputes from ordinary private disputes that do not fall within the Tribunal's jurisdiction.

12. Powers and Functions

  • Adjudicate civil cases involving substantial questions relating to the environment within its statutory jurisdiction.
  • Hear appeals against specified orders and decisions under scheduled environmental laws.
  • Grant relief and compensation to victims of pollution and environmental damage.
  • Order restitution of damaged property and the environment.
  • Issue orders and directions necessary for effective environmental protection within its jurisdiction.
  • Apply the principles of sustainable development, precautionary action and polluter pays.
  • Exercise powers similar to those of a civil court for specified procedural purposes.
  • Review its decisions in accordance with the Act and applicable procedural rules.

13. Environmental Principles Applied by the NGT

Sustainable development

Development should meet present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. Economic development must be balanced with environmental protection.

Precautionary principle

Where there is a threat of serious or irreversible environmental damage, lack of full scientific certainty should not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation.

Polluter pays principle

The person or entity responsible for pollution should bear the cost of preventing and remedying environmental damage. This may include compensation to affected persons and the cost of restoring the environment.

Inter-generational equity

Natural resources and environmental quality should be protected for future generations, rather than being exhausted or degraded for short-term benefits.

Public trust doctrine

Natural resources such as rivers, forests and other ecological assets are held by the state in trust for the public. Public authorities must protect these resources and cannot treat them as unrestricted private assets.

14. Procedure of the Tribunal

The NGT is not bound by the procedure laid down in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. It is guided by the principles of natural justice and may regulate its own procedure, subject to the NGT Act and applicable rules.

The Tribunal has powers similar to those of a civil court for specified matters, including:

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of persons.
  • Requiring discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning public records or documents.
  • Issuing commissions for examination of witnesses or documents.
  • Reviewing its decisions in accordance with law.

The Tribunal is also not bound by the strict rules of evidence under the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Its procedure is intended to facilitate effective and fair adjudication.

15. Limitation Periods

The NGT Act prescribes limitation periods for filing applications and appeals.

Proceeding Limitation period
Application under Section 14 Six months from the date on which the cause of action first arose, with a possible further extension not exceeding 60 days for sufficient cause.
Application for relief, compensation or restitution under Section 15 Five years from the date on which the cause for such compensation or relief first arose, with a possible further extension not exceeding 60 days for sufficient cause.
Appeal under Section 16 Thirty days from the date on which the relevant order or decision is communicated, with a possible further extension not exceeding 60 days for sufficient cause.

The applicable provision and the date from which limitation begins must be checked for each case.

16. Orders, Enforcement and Appeals

Orders of the NGT are binding and enforceable in accordance with the Act. The Tribunal may direct the payment of compensation, restoration of the environment or other measures within its statutory powers.

Failure to comply with an order may attract the penalties prescribed under the NGT Act. The Act also provides for enforcement of orders through the mechanisms specified in it.

Appeal to the Supreme Court

Under Section 22, a person aggrieved by an order or decision of the NGT may appeal to the Supreme Court within 90 days from the date of communication of the order or decision, on one or more of the grounds specified in the provision. The Supreme Court may allow a further period where the statutory conditions for condonation of delay are met.

The NGT is therefore subject to appellate judicial oversight by the Supreme Court.

17. Suo Motu Powers

The NGT Act does not expressly set out a separate provision titled "suo motu jurisdiction". However, the Supreme Court has recognised that the Tribunal can initiate proceedings on its own motion in appropriate environmental matters.

In Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai v. Ankita Sinha (2021), the Supreme Court considered the nature of the NGT's powers and recognised its authority to take suo motu cognisance of environmental issues within its jurisdiction.

This recognition strengthens the Tribunal's ability to respond to serious environmental concerns even where a conventional application has not been filed by an affected person.

18. Limitations of NGT Jurisdiction

  • Scheduled enactments: The Tribunal's jurisdiction is linked to the laws listed in the Schedule to the NGT Act and the specific provisions conferring jurisdiction.
  • Not every environmental dispute: A matter does not automatically fall within NGT jurisdiction merely because it involves an environmental issue.
  • Wildlife legislation: The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 is not included in the Schedule to the NGT Act.
  • Indian Forest Act: The Indian Forest Act, 1927 is not included in the Schedule.
  • Other remedies: Matters outside the Tribunal's statutory jurisdiction may have to be pursued before the competent courts or authorities.
  • Time limits: Applications and appeals must comply with the limitation periods prescribed by the Act.

19. Significance

  • Specialised adjudication: Combines judicial and technical expertise in environmental disputes.
  • Access to environmental justice: Provides a dedicated forum for affected persons and communities.
  • Environmental protection: Enables orders for compensation, restoration and compliance with environmental laws.
  • Speedy disposal: The Act expects the Tribunal to endeavour to dispose of applications or appeals finally within six months of filing.
  • Development of environmental jurisprudence: Applies and develops important principles of environmental law.
  • Institutional accountability: Provides judicial scrutiny of specified environmental decisions and actions.

20. Challenges

  • Vacancies: Vacancies in judicial and expert positions can affect the Tribunal's capacity and speed of disposal.
  • Case backlog: Increasing environmental litigation can place pressure on available benches and resources.
  • Technical complexity: Environmental disputes often require specialised scientific data and expert evidence.
  • Enforcement: Effective implementation of orders depends on compliance by public authorities and private entities.
  • Regional access: Geographical distance and limited sittings may affect access for people in remote areas.
  • Jurisdictional gaps: The exclusion of certain environmental laws from the Schedule limits the range of matters the Tribunal can hear.
  • Institutional resources: Adequate staffing, infrastructure and technical support are essential for effective functioning.

21. Measures to Strengthen the NGT

  • Fill judicial and expert vacancies promptly.
  • Strengthen regional access and provide adequate infrastructure for zonal benches.
  • Improve scientific and technical support for environmental adjudication.
  • Ensure timely implementation and monitoring of Tribunal orders.
  • Promote transparent and accessible environmental information systems.
  • Improve coordination with pollution control boards, environmental authorities and local administrations.
  • Review the statutory framework periodically to address emerging environmental challenges.
  • Ensure that the Tribunal retains institutional independence and adequate resources.

22. Constitutional Provisions Relating to Environment

Provision Significance
Article 21 The right to life has been judicially interpreted to include the right to a clean and healthy environment.
Article 48A Directs the State to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.
Article 51A(g) Imposes a fundamental duty on every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment.
Article 32 Provides the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights, including environmental dimensions of Article 21.
Article 226 Empowers High Courts to issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for other purposes.

23. Important Environmental Judgments

  • Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996): Recognised the precautionary principle and polluter pays principle as essential features of environmental law in India.
  • Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India (1996): Emphasised the polluter pays principle and the responsibility of polluting industries to bear the cost of environmental restoration.
  • M.C. Mehta v. Union of India: A series of cases that significantly developed environmental jurisprudence, including principles concerning industrial pollution and public safety.
  • T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India: Played a major role in the judicial protection and conservation of forests.
  • Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai v. Ankita Sinha (2021): Recognised the NGT's power to take suo motu cognisance of environmental issues within its jurisdiction.

24. Important Facts for Prelims

  • The NGT is a statutory body established under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.
  • It began functioning on 18 October 2010.
  • Its principal place of sitting is New Delhi.
  • It has a Principal Bench and four zonal benches: Bhopal, Kolkata, Chennai and Pune.
  • It consists of a Chairperson, Judicial Members and Expert Members.
  • Section 14 deals with original jurisdiction over civil cases involving substantial questions relating to the environment.
  • Section 15 deals with relief, compensation and restitution.
  • Section 16 deals with appellate jurisdiction.
  • Section 20 requires the Tribunal to apply sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle.
  • Section 22 provides for appeals to the Supreme Court.
  • The NGT is not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 or the strict rules of evidence, and is guided by natural justice.
  • The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 is not included in the Schedule to the NGT Act.

25. Mains Perspective

Role of the NGT in environmental governance

The NGT has strengthened environmental governance by creating a specialised forum that combines judicial and technical expertise. It can provide compensation, order environmental restoration and examine specified decisions under environmental legislation. Its jurisprudence has also reinforced principles such as sustainable development, precaution and polluter pays.

Challenges to effectiveness

Vacancies, limited resources, enforcement difficulties and jurisdictional restrictions can affect its performance. Environmental disputes also require reliable scientific evidence and coordination among multiple authorities.

Way forward

Strengthening the NGT requires timely appointments, adequate technical support, improved regional access and effective monitoring of its orders. Its statutory framework should remain responsive to emerging environmental threats while preserving procedural fairness and judicial independence.

26. Practice Questions

Prelims

  1. Under which Act was the National Green Tribunal established?

    Answer: National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.

  2. Which section of the NGT Act deals with original jurisdiction?

    Answer: Section 14.

  3. Which section deals with relief, compensation and restitution?

    Answer: Section 15.

  4. Which three principles must the NGT apply under Section 20?

    Answer: Sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle.

  5. Is the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 included in the Schedule to the NGT Act?

    Answer: No.

  6. Which court hears appeals against NGT orders under Section 22?

    Answer: The Supreme Court of India.
