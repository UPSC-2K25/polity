INDIAN POLITY · INSTITUTIONS AND ACCOUNTABILITY

Statutory and Other Bodies

Institutions that regulate, investigate, protect rights and strengthen accountability.

India's governance framework includes several institutions established by legislation or executive decisions. These institutions perform specialised functions in areas such as human rights, transparency, vigilance, investigation, environmental protection, financial regulation and public policy.

Their legal status, powers and independence differ according to the law or administrative instrument under which they are established. Understanding these differences is important for studying Indian governance and constitutional administration.

CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK

How are these bodies established?

Statutory bodies derive their authority from legislation enacted by Parliament or a State Legislature. Executive bodies are established through executive resolutions, orders or administrative decisions. Regulatory institutions may exercise specialised powers under their respective laws.

Every institution must operate within the Constitution and the legal framework governing its establishment.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Constitutional, Statutory and Executive Bodies

A constitutional body derives its existence or essential framework from the Constitution. A statutory body is established under legislation. An executive body is created through executive authority, such as a government resolution or order.

The label alone does not determine the extent of an institution's independence or powers. These must be examined from its governing constitutional provision, statute or establishing instrument.

How to Study Each Institution

For every institution, understand the following aspects instead of memorising only its establishment year:

  1. Legal basis and historical background.
  2. Composition and organisational structure.
  3. Appointment, removal and tenure of members.
  4. Powers, functions and jurisdiction.
  5. Independence and relationship with the government.
  6. Reporting, accountability and enforcement mechanisms.
  7. Limitations, challenges and important reforms.
  8. Important judicial decisions and current developments.
