Statutory Bodies

Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA)

CARA is the statutory body responsible for regulating and monitoring adoption in India, with a focus on ensuring that adoption processes protect the best interests and rights of children.

1. Introduction

The Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA) is a statutory body under the Ministry of Women and Child Development. It regulates and monitors in-country and inter-country adoptions in accordance with the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 and the Adoption Regulations.

  • Nature: Statutory body.
  • Administrative ministry: Ministry of Women and Child Development.
  • Statutory basis: Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015.
  • Principal role: Regulation and monitoring of adoption processes.
  • Central concern: Best interests and welfare of the child.

CARA functions as the Central Authority for inter-country adoptions under the Hague Convention on Protection of Children and Co-operation in Respect of Intercountry Adoption, 1993.

2. Evolution and Legal Background

CARA was initially established in 1990 as an autonomous body under the Ministry of Women and Child Development. Its role was subsequently strengthened through statutory recognition under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015.

The legal framework governing adoption in India has evolved to ensure:

  • Protection of children without parental care.
  • Transparent and legally valid adoption procedures.
  • Prevention of trafficking and illegal placement of children.
  • Recognition of the child's best interests as the primary consideration.
  • Regulation of both domestic and inter-country adoption.

3. Objectives of CARA

  • Regulate and monitor adoption processes in India.
  • Promote the adoption of orphaned, abandoned and surrendered children who are legally free for adoption.
  • Protect the rights and interests of children throughout the adoption process.
  • Ensure transparency and accountability in adoption procedures.
  • Prevent illegal adoption, trafficking and exploitation.
  • Facilitate in-country and inter-country adoption in accordance with law.
  • Coordinate with State Governments, specialised adoption agencies and other authorities.

4. Organisation and Composition

CARA functions under the administrative control of the Ministry of Women and Child Development. Its statutory structure and responsibilities are governed by the Juvenile Justice Act and the applicable rules.

The Authority operates through a central administrative framework and coordinates with the adoption agencies and authorities involved in the adoption process.

Its work involves cooperation with:

  • State Adoption Resource Agencies (SARAs).
  • Specialised Adoption Agencies (SAAs).
  • District Child Protection Units.
  • Child Welfare Committees.
  • Authorised foreign adoption agencies and Central Authorities in other countries.
  • Other institutions and authorities prescribed under the adoption framework.

5. Functions of CARA

A. Regulation of Adoption

  • Regulates in-country and inter-country adoption procedures.
  • Frames and administers adoption standards within the statutory framework.
  • Monitors compliance by adoption agencies and other stakeholders.

B. Child Protection

  • Promotes adoption in the best interests of the child.
  • Supports the identification and rehabilitation of children legally free for adoption.
  • Works to prevent illegal placement and trafficking.

C. Coordination

  • Coordinates with State Governments and adoption agencies.
  • Facilitates inter-country adoption in accordance with applicable international obligations.
  • Provides guidance and support to stakeholders.

D. Monitoring and Oversight

  • Maintains and supervises the adoption process through the prescribed digital system.
  • Monitors adoption agencies and compliance with regulations.
  • Promotes accountability and transparency.

6. Legal Framework for Adoption in India

Adoption in India is governed by more than one legal framework. The applicable law depends on the circumstances and personal law framework involved.

A. Juvenile Justice Act, 2015

The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, as amended, provides the secular framework for adoption of children covered by the Act.

B. Adoption Regulations

The Adoption Regulations, 2022, notified under the Juvenile Justice Act, prescribe procedures for in-country and inter-country adoption, including registration, referral, matching, legal processes and post-adoption follow-up.

C. Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, 1956

The Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, 1956, governs adoption among persons to whom the Act applies. It is a separate personal law framework and should not be confused with the adoption process administered by CARA under the Juvenile Justice Act.

7. Children Eligible for Adoption Under the Juvenile Justice Framework

The Juvenile Justice Act provides for adoption of children who are:

  • Orphaned: Children without legal or biological parents or lawful guardians willing to take care of them.
  • Abandoned: Children deserted by their biological or adoptive parents or guardians and declared abandoned through the prescribed procedure.
  • Surrendered: Children relinquished by parents or guardians due to physical, emotional or social factors beyond their control, following the prescribed process.

A child must be declared legally free for adoption by the competent Child Welfare Committee before being placed for adoption under this framework.

8. Adoption Process

The adoption process is designed to ensure that children are placed in safe and suitable families through a transparent legal procedure.

  1. Registration: Prospective adoptive parents register through the prescribed CARA online system.
  2. Home Study Report: An authorised agency assesses the prospective parents' suitability and prepares the required report.
  3. Child referral: A child legally free for adoption is referred to prospective adoptive parents according to the applicable priority and matching procedures.
  4. Acceptance: Prospective parents consider and accept the referred child in accordance with the regulations.
  5. Legal process: The adoption application is processed by the competent District Magistrate under the statutory framework.
  6. Post-adoption follow-up: Follow-up reports and supervision are carried out as prescribed.

The process and timelines may vary according to the category of adoption, availability of children and applicable regulations.

10. Inter-Country Adoption

Inter-country adoption refers to adoption involving prospective adoptive parents and a child who are habitually resident in different countries.

CARA acts as India's Central Authority under the Hague Convention on Intercountry Adoption, 1993.

The international framework seeks to:

  • Ensure that inter-country adoption takes place in the best interests of the child.
  • Establish safeguards against abduction, sale and trafficking of children.
  • Promote cooperation between the authorities of the countries concerned.
  • Ensure that the necessary consents are informed and legally valid.
  • Provide recognition and protection for adoptions completed in accordance with the Convention.

11. Hague Convention on Intercountry Adoption

The Hague Convention of 29 May 1993 on Protection of Children and Co-operation in Respect of Intercountry Adoption establishes safeguards for inter-country adoption.

Its key principles include:

  • The best interests of the child must be the paramount consideration.
  • Domestic solutions should receive appropriate consideration before inter-country adoption.
  • Adoption must be carried out through competent authorities.
  • Consent must be free, informed and legally valid.
  • Commercial trafficking and improper financial gain must be prevented.
  • Countries must cooperate to protect children and ensure lawful procedures.

India became a party to the Convention in 2003. CARA performs the Central Authority functions for India under the Convention.

12. Best Interests of the Child

The best interests of the child are central to the adoption framework.

This principle requires authorities to consider:

  • The child's safety and physical well-being.
  • Emotional and psychological development.
  • Stability and suitability of the proposed family environment.
  • The child's identity, background and individual needs.
  • Protection from abuse, exploitation and trafficking.
  • The child's views, where appropriate, in accordance with age and maturity.

Adoption is intended to provide a permanent family environment for a child, rather than to satisfy the preferences of prospective parents alone.

13. Digital Adoption System

CARA uses a centralised digital system to facilitate adoption-related registration, referral, matching and monitoring.

The system is intended to:

  • Improve transparency in adoption procedures.
  • Maintain records of prospective adoptive parents and children legally free for adoption.
  • Facilitate the matching and referral process.
  • Reduce opportunities for irregularities and unauthorised placements.
  • Support monitoring and coordination among adoption authorities.

14. Challenges in Adoption

  • Low awareness: Limited understanding of legal adoption procedures among prospective parents.
  • Waiting periods: Delays may arise from procedural requirements and differences between the number of children available and parental preferences.
  • Institutional capacity: Adoption agencies may face shortages of trained personnel and resources.
  • Special needs adoption: Children with disabilities or medical needs may face greater difficulty in finding suitable families.
  • Illegal practices: Trafficking and unauthorised placements require constant vigilance.
  • Post-adoption support: Families and children may require counselling and continued assistance.
  • Inter-agency coordination: Effective cooperation is required among central, State and district authorities.

15. Way Forward

  • Strengthen specialised adoption agencies and child protection institutions.
  • Improve training and capacity-building for officials and social workers.
  • Increase awareness of lawful adoption procedures.
  • Strengthen safeguards against trafficking and illegal placements.
  • Improve transparency and accessibility of digital adoption services.
  • Provide adequate counselling and post-adoption support.
  • Encourage adoption of older children and children with special needs while ensuring suitable family assessments.
  • Strengthen coordination between CARA, State authorities and district institutions.

17. Prelims Facts

  • CARA is a statutory body under the Ministry of Women and Child Development.
  • It was initially established in 1990 as an autonomous body.
  • Its statutory recognition is under the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015.
  • It regulates and monitors in-country and inter-country adoption under the Juvenile Justice framework.
  • It is India's Central Authority under the Hague Convention on Intercountry Adoption.
  • The Child Welfare Committee declares eligible children legally free for adoption.
  • The District Magistrate issues adoption orders under the amended statutory framework.
  • The Adoption Regulations, 2022, prescribe detailed adoption procedures.
  • The Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, 1956, is a separate personal law framework.
