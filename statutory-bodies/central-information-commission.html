Central Information Commission (CIC)

The Central Information Commission is a statutory institution established under the Right to Information Act, 2005. It plays an important role in enforcing the right of citizens to access information held by public authorities under the jurisdiction of the Union Government.

The Commission hears second appeals, examines complaints relating to access to information, and exercises powers to ensure compliance with the provisions of the RTI Act. It is an important institutional mechanism for transparency, accountability and citizen participation in governance.

1. Establishment and Legal Basis

The Central Information Commission was constituted with effect from 12 October 2005 under Section 12 of the Right to Information Act, 2005.

The RTI Act gives citizens a legal framework to obtain information from public authorities, subject to exemptions and other restrictions specified in the Act. The Central Information Commission functions as the central appellate and oversight institution under this framework.

2. Right to Information: Constitutional and Legal Context

The Right to Information is closely connected with the freedom of speech and expression guaranteed under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution.

The Supreme Court has recognised that the freedom of speech and expression includes the right to receive information necessary for meaningful participation in democratic processes.

The RTI Act, 2005 gives this principle a statutory framework by creating enforceable procedures through which citizens can seek information from public authorities.

The right is not absolute. Sections 8 and 9 provide exemptions and grounds for rejection in specified circumstances. Section 24 also provides for certain intelligence and security organisations to be excluded, subject to the statutory exceptions.

3. Objectives of the Right to Information Act

The RTI Act seeks to promote transparency and accountability in the functioning of public authorities. Its major objectives include:

  • Providing citizens access to information held by public authorities.
  • Promoting transparency in government administration.
  • Increasing accountability of public authorities.
  • Reducing secrecy and improving administrative openness.
  • Enabling citizens to participate more effectively in democratic governance.
  • Creating an institutional mechanism for appeals and complaints.

4. Composition of the Central Information Commission

Section 12(2) provides that the Central Information Commission consists of:

  • One Chief Information Commissioner.
  • Such number of Central Information Commissioners as may be deemed necessary, subject to a maximum of ten.

Thus, the Commission may have a maximum of eleven members, including the Chief Information Commissioner.

4.1 Qualifications of the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners

Under Section 12(5), the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners must be persons of eminence in public life with wide knowledge and experience in one or more of the following fields:

  • Law.
  • Science and technology.
  • Social service.
  • Management.
  • Journalism.
  • Mass media.
  • Administration and governance.

4.2 Disqualifications and Restrictions

Section 12(6) provides that the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners shall not:

  • Be a Member of Parliament or a Member of the Legislature of any State or Union Territory.
  • Hold any other office of profit.
  • Be connected with any political party.
  • Carry on any business.
  • Pursue any profession.

These restrictions seek to protect the impartiality and independence expected of the Commission.

4.3 Headquarters

Under Section 12(7), the headquarters of the Central Information Commission is at Delhi. It may establish offices at other places in India with the previous approval of the Central Government.

5. Appointment of the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners

Under Section 12(3), the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners are appointed by the President of India on the recommendation of a statutory selection committee.

5.1 Selection Committee

Member Position
Prime Minister Chairperson
Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha Member
Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister Member

The Act clarifies that where the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha has not been recognised as such, the Leader of the single largest group in opposition to the Government in the Lok Sabha is treated as the Leader of Opposition for this purpose.

The selection committee recommends candidates. The formal appointment is made by the President.

6. Tenure and Conditions of Service

The Right to Information (Amendment) Act, 2019 changed the statutory framework relating to the term of office and service conditions of Information Commissioners.

Under the amended Section 13, the term of office is prescribed by the Central Government through rules. The Right to Information Rules, 2019 prescribe a term of three years for the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners.

Office Term Age limit Reappointment
Chief Information Commissioner Three years Cannot hold office after 65 years Not eligible
Information Commissioner Three years Cannot hold office after 65 years Not eligible for reappointment as Information Commissioner

An Information Commissioner may be appointed as the Chief Information Commissioner. However, the aggregate term served in the two offices cannot exceed five years.

6.1 Resignation

Under Section 13, the Chief Information Commissioner or an Information Commissioner may resign by writing under their hand addressed to the President of India.

6.2 Oath or Affirmation

Before entering office, the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners must make and subscribe an oath or affirmation in the prescribed form. The oath is made before the President or a person appointed by the President for this purpose.

6.3 Conditions of Service

The salaries, allowances and other conditions of service are prescribed by the Central Government under the amended statutory framework. Such conditions cannot be varied to the disadvantage of an incumbent after appointment.

7. Removal of the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners

Section 14 of the RTI Act provides for the removal of the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners. The President is the authority empowered to remove them under the Act.

7.1 Removal on Grounds of Proved Misbehaviour or Incapacity

The President may remove the Chief Information Commissioner or an Information Commissioner on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity, after the Supreme Court, on a reference made by the President, has conducted an inquiry and reported that the person ought to be removed.

During the inquiry, the President may suspend the concerned office-holder and, where appropriate, prohibit attendance at office.

7.2 Other Grounds of Removal

Section 14 also specifies circumstances in which the President may remove an office-holder without the Supreme Court inquiry procedure applicable to proved misbehaviour or incapacity.

  • Being adjudged an insolvent.
  • Being convicted of an offence which, in the opinion of the President, involves moral turpitude.
  • Engaging in paid employment outside the duties of office during the term.
  • Being unfit to continue in office by reason of infirmity of mind or body.
  • Acquiring a financial or other interest likely to prejudicially affect the discharge of official functions.

The Act also provides for removal where the office-holder is concerned in a contract or agreement made by or on behalf of the Government of India, or participates in its profits or benefits, subject to the statutory conditions.

8. Jurisdiction of the Central Information Commission

The jurisdiction of the Central Information Commission extends to Central public authorities under the RTI Act. It deals with complaints and second appeals arising from the exercise of the right to information in relation to these authorities.

A Central public authority may include a ministry, department, public sector undertaking, statutory authority or other body falling within the definition of a public authority under Section 2(h), subject to the provisions of the Act.

8.1 Meaning of Public Authority

Section 2(h) defines a public authority to include an authority, body or institution of self-government established or constituted:

  • By or under the Constitution.
  • By any other law made by Parliament.
  • By any other law made by a State Legislature.
  • By notification or order issued by the appropriate Government.

The definition also covers bodies owned, controlled or substantially financed by the appropriate Government and non-governmental organisations substantially financed directly or indirectly by government funds.

8.2 Central and State Public Authorities

The Central Information Commission deals with matters concerning Central public authorities. State Information Commissions deal with matters concerning State public authorities.

The appropriate commission is determined by the public authority involved, not merely by the location of the applicant or the place where the information is requested.

9. Functions and Powers of the Commission

The principal functions and powers of the Central Information Commission are contained in Sections 18, 19, 20 and 25 of the RTI Act.

9.1 Complaint Jurisdiction: Section 18

Section 18 empowers the Commission to receive and inquire into complaints relating to the right to information.

A person may complain where:

  • A public information officer has not been appointed by a public authority.
  • An application for information has been refused.
  • No response has been received within the prescribed time.
  • An applicant has been required to pay an unreasonable fee.
  • The information provided is incomplete, misleading or false.
  • There are other difficulties relating to access to information under the Act.

The Commission may conduct an inquiry into a complaint and exercise the powers provided by the Act.

9.2 Powers During an Inquiry

While conducting an inquiry under Section 18, the Commission has powers similar to those of a civil court in specified matters, including:

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of persons.
  • Requiring discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavit.
  • Requisitioning public records or copies from courts or offices.
  • Issuing summons for examination of witnesses or documents.

The Commission may examine any record to which the RTI Act applies and which is under the control of the public authority. Such a record cannot be withheld from the Commission during an inquiry under the statutory provision.

9.3 Second Appeal: Section 19

Section 19 provides the appellate mechanism for applicants who are dissatisfied with the decision of a public information officer or the first appellate authority.

The RTI Act provides for two levels of appeal:

Stage Authority Time limit
First appeal Officer senior in rank to the Central Public Information Officer or State Public Information Officer in the same public authority Within 30 days from the relevant decision or expiry of the response period
Second appeal Central Information Commission or State Information Commission, as applicable Within 90 days from the date on which the first appellate decision should have been made or was actually received

The first appellate authority may admit an appeal after the prescribed period if sufficient cause for the delay is established.

The Commission may also admit a second appeal after the prescribed period if it is satisfied that the appellant was prevented by sufficient cause from filing it in time.

9.4 Burden of Proof in Appeal

Under Section 19(5), in an appeal proceeding, the burden of proving that denial of a request was justified lies on the Central Public Information Officer or State Public Information Officer who denied the request.

This provision is important because the public information officer must justify the refusal under the provisions of the Act.

9.5 Compensation and Directions

Under Section 19(8), the Commission may require a public authority to take steps necessary to secure compliance with the Act. These may include:

  • Providing access to information in a particular form.
  • Appointing public information officers where required.
  • Publishing specified categories of information.
  • Making necessary changes to record-management practices.
  • Enhancing training for officials dealing with RTI applications.
  • Providing compensation to the complainant for loss or other detriment suffered.

10. Penalties: Section 20

Section 20 empowers the Commission to impose a monetary penalty on a Central Public Information Officer or State Public Information Officer in specified circumstances.

A penalty may be imposed where the officer, without reasonable cause:

  • Refuses to receive an application for information.
  • Fails to furnish information within the prescribed time.
  • Malafidely denies a request for information.
  • Knowingly provides incorrect, incomplete or misleading information.
  • Destroys information that was the subject of a request.
  • Obstructs the furnishing of information in any manner.
Penalty rate ₹250 per day of delay or default
Maximum penalty ₹25,000
Authority imposing penalty Central Information Commission or State Information Commission, as applicable
Opportunity of hearing The officer must be given a reasonable opportunity of being heard

The Commission must be satisfied that the officer acted without reasonable cause before imposing the penalty.

10.1 Disciplinary Action

Under Section 20(2), where the Commission is of the opinion that the officer has persistently, without reasonable cause and with improper conduct, failed to perform duties under the Act, it may recommend disciplinary action under the applicable service rules.

The monetary penalty under Section 20(1) and the recommendation for disciplinary action under Section 20(2) are distinct statutory measures.

11. Monitoring and Annual Reports

Section 25 requires the Central Information Commission to prepare an annual report on the implementation of the provisions of the RTI Act.

The report includes information such as:

  • The number of requests received by public authorities.
  • The number of decisions where applicants were not entitled to access information.
  • The number of appeals and complaints received and disposed of by the Commission.
  • Details of disciplinary action taken against officers.
  • Amounts of charges and penalties collected under the Act.
  • Recommendations for reform and improvement in implementation.

The Central Government is required to place the annual report before each House of Parliament.

12. Exemptions from Disclosure of Information

The right to information is subject to exemptions. The RTI Act seeks to balance transparency with legitimate requirements such as national security, privacy, confidentiality and protection of certain public interests.

12.1 Section 8: Exemptions

Section 8 provides categories of information that are exempt from disclosure, subject to the statutory provisions and applicable public-interest considerations.

Category Nature of exemption
National security Information affecting sovereignty, integrity, security, strategic interests or relations with foreign States.
Contempt of court Information whose disclosure has been expressly forbidden by a court or tribunal or may constitute contempt of court.
Legislative privilege Information whose disclosure would cause a breach of privilege of Parliament or a State Legislature.
Commercial confidence Trade secrets and commercial confidence where disclosure would harm competitive position, subject to public-interest considerations.
Fiduciary relationship Information available to a person in a fiduciary relationship, subject to the public-interest provision.
Foreign government information Information received in confidence from a foreign government.
Life and safety Information whose disclosure would endanger the life or physical safety of a person or identify a confidential source in specified circumstances.
Investigation Information that would impede the process of investigation, apprehension or prosecution of offenders.
Cabinet papers Cabinet papers and related deliberative material, subject to the statutory provisions.
Personal information Personal information, subject to the applicable statutory test and public-interest provisions.

12.2 Severability: Section 10

Where a record contains both exempt and non-exempt information, Section 10 permits access to the non-exempt portion if it can reasonably be separated from the exempt material.

12.3 Third-Party Information: Section 11

Section 11 lays down a procedure for dealing with information relating to or supplied by a third party which has been treated as confidential.

The procedure includes giving the third party an opportunity to make representations in specified circumstances. The third party does not possess an automatic veto over disclosure.

12.4 Intelligence and Security Organisations: Section 24

Section 24 provides that the RTI Act does not apply to certain intelligence and security organisations specified in the Second Schedule, subject to the statutory exceptions.

The exemption does not extend to allegations of corruption and human rights violations. In cases involving allegations of human rights violations, information may be provided only after approval of the Central Information Commission and within the statutory time limit.

13. Important Amendments to the RTI Act

Amendment Important changes
Right to Information (Amendment) Act, 2019 Changed the framework for the term of office, salaries, allowances and other conditions of service of the Chief Information Commissioner and Information Commissioners, empowering the Central Government to prescribe these matters through rules.

The 2019 amendment is important for understanding the present statutory framework governing the tenure and service conditions of Information Commissioners.

14. Independence and Accountability of the Commission

The RTI Act contains provisions intended to enable the Commission to discharge its functions without being subjected to directions from other authorities in the exercise of its statutory powers.

Section 12(4) vests the general superintendence, direction and management of the affairs of the Central Information Commission in the Chief Information Commissioner.

At the same time, the Commission is accountable through statutory procedures such as annual reporting, prescribed appointment and removal processes, and judicial review of its decisions by constitutional courts.

16. Important Distinctions for UPSC

16.1 CIC and Constitutional Bodies

The Central Information Commission is a statutory body established under the RTI Act, 2005. It is not a constitutional body established directly by the Constitution.

16.2 CIC and CVC

The Central Information Commission deals with access to information and enforcement of the RTI framework. The Central Vigilance Commission deals with vigilance administration and specified anti-corruption oversight functions.

16.3 Complaint and Appeal

A complaint under Section 18 and a second appeal under Section 19 are distinct statutory remedies. A complaint concerns specified difficulties in accessing information, while the appeal mechanism provides a process for challenging decisions relating to an RTI request.

16.4 CIC and SIC

The Central Information Commission deals with Central public authorities, while State Information Commissions deal with State public authorities. Both operate under the Right to Information Act, 2005.
