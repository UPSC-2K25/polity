Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)

The Central Vigilance Commission is the apex statutory vigilance institution of the Union Government. It supervises vigilance administration in Central Government organisations and exercises specified superintendence over the Delhi Special Police Establishment in relation to investigations under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988.

The Commission seeks to promote integrity, transparency and accountability in public administration. It advises Central Government organisations on vigilance matters and helps strengthen institutional mechanisms against corruption.

1. Establishment and Legal Basis

The Central Vigilance Commission was originally established in February 1964 through a resolution of the Government of India, following the recommendations of the Committee on Prevention of Corruption, popularly known as the Santhanam Committee.

Initially, the Commission was an executive body. It received statutory status through the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003, which provides for its constitution, powers, functions and service conditions.

2. Background: Santhanam Committee

The Government of India appointed the Committee on Prevention of Corruption in 1962 under the chairmanship of K. Santhanam. The Committee examined the causes of corruption in public administration and suggested institutional and administrative measures to prevent it.

One of its important recommendations was the establishment of a Central Vigilance Commission to provide independent oversight and guidance on vigilance matters.

The Commission was consequently established in 1964 as an executive institution. The Supreme Court, in the Vineet Narain case (1997), emphasised the need to strengthen the institutional framework for vigilance and investigation of corruption. The Central Vigilance Commission Ordinance, 1998, was an important step in this direction before the enactment of the 2003 legislation.

3. Objectives of the Commission

The CVC is intended to strengthen vigilance administration and promote integrity in public institutions. Its major objectives include:

  • Preventing corruption in Central Government organisations.
  • Promoting integrity and ethical conduct among public servants.
  • Improving vigilance administration and internal controls.
  • Supervising specified investigations conducted by the CBI.
  • Advising government organisations on vigilance matters.
  • Reviewing the progress of investigations and prosecution in specified cases.
  • Encouraging transparency and accountability in public administration.

4. Composition of the Central Vigilance Commission

Section 3 of the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003, provides for the constitution of the Commission.

The Commission consists of:

  • One Central Vigilance Commissioner, who acts as the Chairperson.
  • Not more than two Vigilance Commissioners, who act as Members.

Thus, the Commission may have a maximum of three members, including the Central Vigilance Commissioner.

Office Position
Central Vigilance Commissioner Chairperson
Vigilance Commissioners Members; maximum two

4.1 Qualifications

Under Section 3(3), the Central Vigilance Commissioner and Vigilance Commissioners are appointed from among persons who have held or are holding specified senior positions in the Central Government, All India Services or other relevant services, or persons with expertise in specified fields.

The statutory framework requires consideration of experience and expertise in areas such as vigilance, policy-making, administration, finance, including insurance and banking, and law.

4.2 Headquarters

The headquarters of the Central Vigilance Commission is located in New Delhi.

5. Appointment of the Commissioners

Under Section 4 of the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003, the Central Vigilance Commissioner and Vigilance Commissioners are appointed by the President of India by warrant under his hand and seal.

The President makes the appointments on the recommendation of a committee consisting of the following members:

Member Position
Prime Minister Chairperson
Minister of Home Affairs Member
Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha Member

Where the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha has not been recognised as such, the leader of the single largest group in opposition to the Government in the Lok Sabha is treated as the Leader of Opposition for this purpose.

6. Tenure and Conditions of Service

Section 5 of the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003, deals with the term of office and conditions of service of the Central Vigilance Commissioner and Vigilance Commissioners.

Aspect Provision
Term of office Four years from the date of entering office
Age limit Cannot continue after attaining 65 years of age
Reappointment Not eligible for reappointment to the same office
Resignation By writing addressed to the President

A Vigilance Commissioner who is appointed as the Central Vigilance Commissioner may hold office for a maximum aggregate period of four years, including the period already served as Vigilance Commissioner.

6.1 Conditions of Service

The salary, allowances and other conditions of service are governed by the Central Vigilance Commission Act. The service conditions of the Central Vigilance Commissioner are equivalent to those of the Chairperson of the Union Public Service Commission. Those of a Vigilance Commissioner are equivalent to those of a Member of the Union Public Service Commission.

The salary, allowances and other conditions of service cannot be varied to the disadvantage of an incumbent after appointment.

7. Removal of the Commissioners

Section 6 of the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003, provides for the removal of the Central Vigilance Commissioner and Vigilance Commissioners.

7.1 Removal on Grounds of Proved Misbehaviour or Incapacity

The President may remove the Central Vigilance Commissioner or a Vigilance Commissioner on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity after the Supreme Court, on a reference made by the President, has conducted an inquiry and reported that the person ought to be removed.

During the inquiry, the President may suspend the concerned office-holder and, where appropriate, prohibit attendance at office.

7.2 Other Grounds of Removal

The President may also remove an office-holder on specified statutory grounds, including:

  • Being adjudged an insolvent.
  • Being convicted of an offence which, in the opinion of the President, involves moral turpitude.
  • Engaging in paid employment outside the duties of office during the term.
  • Being unfit to continue in office by reason of infirmity of mind or body.
  • Acquiring a financial or other interest likely to prejudicially affect the discharge of official functions.

The Act also provides for removal where the office-holder is concerned in a contract or agreement made by or on behalf of the Government of India, or participates in its profits or benefits, subject to the statutory conditions.

8. Functions and Powers of the Commission

Section 8 of the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003, specifies the functions and powers of the Commission. Its role includes vigilance supervision, advice, review and oversight in matters falling within its statutory jurisdiction.

8.1 Superintendence over the Delhi Special Police Establishment

The CVC exercises superintendence over the functioning of the Delhi Special Police Establishment in relation to investigations of offences alleged to have been committed under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988.

The Commission may review the progress of investigations conducted by the DSPE in relation to such offences. This role does not make the CVC the investigating agency; investigation is carried out by the DSPE, commonly known as the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI).

8.2 Inquiry into Complaints

The Commission may inquire into or cause an inquiry or investigation to be made on a reference from the Central Government, or on a complaint, in respect of specified categories of public servants and matters within its jurisdiction.

It may also refer matters to the CBI or other appropriate agencies for investigation, as permitted by the law.

8.3 Advice to the Central Government

The Commission advises the Central Government and specified Central Government organisations on vigilance matters. Its advice is intended to improve the handling of disciplinary and vigilance cases.

8.4 Review of Investigation and Prosecution

The Commission reviews the progress of investigations conducted by the DSPE in cases involving alleged offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act. It also reviews the progress of applications for prosecution sanction in such cases.

8.5 Vigilance Administration

The Commission exercises general superintendence over vigilance administration in Central Government ministries, departments and specified organisations. It may issue guidance and seek information to strengthen vigilance systems.

8.6 Annual Report

The Commission submits an annual report to the President of India on its activities. The report is placed before each House of Parliament.

9. Jurisdiction of the CVC

The jurisdiction of the Central Vigilance Commission is defined by the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003, and related statutory provisions. It is not a general anti-corruption authority over every public servant in India.

Its jurisdiction includes specified categories of public servants and employees of Central Government organisations and bodies covered by the Act.

9.1 Public Servants under its Jurisdiction

The Act covers specified categories of public servants, including:

  • Members of the All India Services serving in connection with the affairs of the Union.
  • Group A officers of the Central Government.
  • Officers of specified Central Government corporations, government companies, societies and local authorities.
  • Other categories of public servants covered by the statutory provisions.

The precise coverage depends on the applicable statutory provisions and the category of organisation or public servant concerned.

9.2 Central and State Jurisdiction

The CVC primarily deals with vigilance matters concerning the Union Government and specified Central public organisations. State Government vigilance matters ordinarily fall within the respective State's vigilance and anti-corruption arrangements.

10. Relationship between CVC and CBI

The Central Vigilance Commission and the Central Bureau of Investigation perform different but complementary functions in the institutional framework for preventing and investigating corruption.

Basis CVC CBI
Nature Statutory vigilance institution Investigative agency functioning under the DSPE Act, 1946
Primary role Vigilance oversight, advice and superintendence in specified matters Investigation of specified offences and cases
Legal basis Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003 Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946
Relationship Exercises statutory superintendence over specified CBI functions Conducts investigations within its legal authority

The CVC does not investigate criminal cases itself merely by virtue of its supervisory role. The CBI remains responsible for conducting investigations assigned to it under the applicable law.

10.1 Limits of CVC Superintendence

The statutory superintendence of the CVC over the DSPE is specifically connected with investigations relating to offences under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. It should not be confused with unrestricted control over every investigation or every function of the CBI.

11. Central Vigilance Officers

Central Vigilance Officers (CVOs) are appointed in Central Government ministries, departments and specified organisations to assist in vigilance administration.

They act as important links between the organisations in which they serve and the Central Vigilance Commission.

Their functions generally include:

  • Identifying areas vulnerable to corruption.
  • Examining complaints involving vigilance issues.
  • Coordinating vigilance investigations and inquiries.
  • Monitoring disciplinary proceedings in vigilance cases.
  • Advising the organisation on preventive vigilance.
  • Maintaining coordination with the CVC and investigative agencies.

CVOs are part of the internal vigilance framework of their respective organisations and do not replace the statutory role of the CVC.

12. Chief Technical Examiners' Organisation

The Chief Technical Examiners' Organisation functions as the technical wing associated with the Central Vigilance Commission. It provides technical assistance in vigilance matters, particularly those involving public works, procurement and contracts.

Its activities include:

  • Technical examination of works and contracts.
  • Scrutiny of tendering and procurement procedures.
  • Examination of suspected irregularities in public works.
  • Providing technical advice in vigilance investigations.

13. Preventive and Punitive Vigilance

Vigilance administration includes both preventive and punitive measures. The CVC promotes a system in which corruption is discouraged through institutional safeguards as well as action against misconduct.

Preventive vigilance Punitive vigilance
Improving procedures and internal controls Taking action against established misconduct
Reducing opportunities for corruption Disciplinary proceedings and other lawful action
Promoting transparency in procurement Investigation of suspected corruption
Training and awareness programmes Prosecution where legally justified

14. Important Judicial and Institutional Developments

14.1 Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1997)

The Vineet Narain case, commonly associated with the Jain Hawala investigations, is an important judgment concerning the independence and accountability of investigative agencies.

The Supreme Court issued directions to strengthen the institutional framework for investigating corruption involving public officials. These directions included measures relating to the functioning of the CBI and the Central Vigilance Commission.

The judgment contributed to the subsequent statutory strengthening of the CVC through legislative action.

14.2 Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003

The 2003 Act gave statutory status to the Commission and established a legal framework for its composition, appointment, tenure, removal, functions and powers.

15. Limitations and Challenges

Although the CVC is an important institution in vigilance administration, its effectiveness depends on institutional capacity, cooperation from government organisations and timely implementation of its recommendations.

  • Advisory character: A significant part of its vigilance advice is advisory in nature and does not automatically operate as a binding judicial order.
  • Limited jurisdiction: Its statutory jurisdiction is confined to specified public servants and organisations.
  • Dependence on other agencies: Investigation and prosecution generally require action by the appropriate investigative and prosecuting authorities.
  • Delays: Delayed departmental inquiries and disciplinary proceedings can weaken the deterrent effect of vigilance action.
  • Institutional coordination: Effective action requires coordination among the CVC, CBI, CVOs, disciplinary authorities and other bodies.
  • Preventive vigilance: Reducing systemic opportunities for corruption requires continuous administrative reforms, not only investigation after misconduct occurs.

16. Measures to Strengthen Vigilance Administration

  • Strengthening the independence and professional capacity of vigilance institutions.
  • Ensuring timely completion of investigations and disciplinary proceedings.
  • Improving transparency in public procurement and government contracts.
  • Strengthening internal audit and risk-management systems.
  • Promoting ethical conduct and integrity in public services.
  • Protecting persons who report corruption through appropriate legal mechanisms.
  • Improving coordination among vigilance, investigative and prosecuting agencies.
  • Using digital systems to improve monitoring, record management and transparency.

18. Important Distinctions for UPSC

18.1 CVC and CBI

The CVC is a vigilance oversight institution, whereas the CBI is an investigative agency. The CVC exercises statutory superintendence over specified CBI functions relating to corruption investigations.

18.2 CVC and Lokpal

The CVC performs vigilance oversight and advisory functions within its statutory jurisdiction. The Lokpal is an anti-corruption ombudsman established under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013, with jurisdiction and powers specified by that Act.

18.3 CVC and Constitutional Bodies

The CVC is a statutory body created under the Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003. It is not a constitutional body established directly by the Constitution of India.

18.4 CVC and State Vigilance Institutions

The CVC primarily deals with specified Union Government organisations and public servants. State vigilance institutions operate within the respective State's administrative and legal framework.
