Administrative Relations between the Union and States

Legislative federalism distributes law-making powers, while administrative federalism determines how executive authority is exercised and coordinated. The Constitution gives States substantial executive responsibility but also establishes mechanisms through which the Union can ensure constitutional compliance, national coordination and effective implementation of laws.

1. Meaning and Constitutional Framework

Administrative relations concern the distribution and exercise of executive powers between the Union and State governments, including the implementation of laws, administrative directions, coordination and the functioning of public services.

The principal constitutional provisions are found in Part XI, Chapter II, particularly Articles 256–263. Other relevant provisions include Articles 73, 162, 355, 365 and 312.

Basic constitutional principles

  • The Union and States exercise executive power in accordance with the constitutional distribution of legislative competence.
  • State executive authority must be exercised consistently with laws made by Parliament and applicable Union executive authority.
  • The Union can issue certain directions to States to secure compliance with constitutional and statutory obligations.
  • Administrative coordination is necessary where responsibilities overlap or national interests are involved.
  • Federal administration must balance national unity with State autonomy.

2. Distribution of Executive Powers

Article 73: Executive power of the Union

Article 73 provides that the executive power of the Union extends to matters with respect to which Parliament has power to make laws and to the exercise of rights, authority and jurisdiction conferred on the Union by treaties or agreements.

However, where the Constitution or a law made by Parliament expressly provides otherwise, the general scope of Union executive power is subject to that provision.

In matters in the Concurrent List, Union executive authority is not automatically unlimited merely because Parliament can legislate on those subjects. The constitutional scheme and relevant laws determine the applicable executive authority.

Article 162: Executive power of the States

Article 162 provides that State executive power extends to matters with respect to which the State Legislature has power to make laws.

In matters on which both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate, State executive power is subject to the executive power expressly conferred on the Union by the Constitution or by a law made by Parliament.

Relationship between Articles 73 and 162

  • Article 73 defines the general extent of Union executive power.
  • Article 162 defines the general extent of State executive power.
  • Both provisions operate within the constitutional distribution of legislative competence.
  • Neither provision should be read in isolation from specific constitutional provisions and parliamentary laws.

3. Union Directions to States

The Constitution authorises the Union to issue directions to State governments in specified circumstances. These provisions seek to ensure that State executive action remains consistent with constitutional and national obligations.

Article 256: Obligation of States and the Union's power to give directions

Article 256 requires every State to exercise its executive power in a manner that ensures compliance with laws made by Parliament and existing laws applicable in that State.

The Union may give directions to a State when necessary for this purpose.

The provision is significant because parliamentary legislation may require implementation through State administrative machinery.

Article 257: Control of the Union over States in certain cases

Article 257 provides that State executive power must be exercised so as not to impede or prejudice the exercise of Union executive power.

The Union may issue directions to States where necessary to secure compliance with this principle.

Specific matters include:

  • Construction and maintenance of means of communication declared by the Union to be of national or military importance.
  • Measures to be taken for the protection of railways within a State.

The Union must compensate a State for additional costs incurred in carrying out directions under the relevant provisions of Article 257, subject to the constitutional arrangements for determining such compensation.

Article 257A: Omitted provision

Article 257A, which had dealt with the deployment of Union armed forces in certain circumstances, was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment and later omitted by the 44th Constitutional Amendment.

It should not be treated as an operative constitutional provision.

4. Delegation of Functions between Governments

Article 258: Union functions entrusted to States

Article 258 empowers the President, with the consent of the State Government, to entrust Union executive functions to that State or its officers.

Such entrustment may be conditional or unconditional.

Parliament may also confer powers and impose duties on a State or its officers in relation to matters within Union legislative competence.

Article 258A: State functions entrusted to the Union

Article 258A empowers the Governor of a State, with the consent of the Government of India, to entrust State executive functions to the Union or its officers.

Importance of Articles 258 and 258A

  • Enable administrative cooperation without changing the constitutional distribution of legislative powers.
  • Allow governments to use existing administrative capacity for efficient implementation.
  • Support coordinated delivery of public services.
  • Provide flexibility in the allocation of administrative responsibilities.

5. Jurisdiction of the Union in Relation to Territories outside States

Article 260

Article 260 allows the Government of India, by agreement with the government of a territory not forming part of India, to undertake executive, legislative or judicial functions vested in that government.

Such an agreement is subject to applicable foreign jurisdiction laws and constitutional requirements.

This provision is distinct from the ordinary Union-State administrative relationship.

6. Cooperation and Coordination between States

Article 261: Public acts, records and judicial proceedings

Article 261 provides that full faith and credit must be given throughout India to public acts, records and judicial proceedings of the Union and every State.

Parliament may determine the manner in which and the conditions under which such acts, records and proceedings are to be proved and their effect determined.

Final judgments or orders delivered by civil courts in any part of India are capable of execution anywhere in India according to law.

Article 262: Inter-State river water disputes

Article 262 empowers Parliament to provide for adjudication of disputes relating to the use, distribution or control of waters of inter-State rivers and river valleys.

Parliament may also provide that neither the Supreme Court nor any other court shall exercise jurisdiction over such disputes as specified by law.

The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 provides a statutory framework for adjudication of qualifying disputes.

The broader framework for inter-State water disputes is discussed in the chapter on Inter-State Relations.

Article 263: Inter-State Council

Article 263 provides for the establishment of an Inter-State Council when public interest would be served by its creation.

It enables inquiry into disputes, discussion of common interests and recommendations for improved coordination of policy and action.

The institutional structure and functioning of the Council are covered separately in Inter-State Council and Zonal Councils.

7. All India Services

All India Services are an important institutional feature of Indian federalism. Their officers serve both the Union and State governments, creating an administrative link between the two levels.

Constitutional basis: Article 312

Article 312 empowers Parliament to create one or more All India Services common to the Union and States, subject to the constitutional procedure.

The Rajya Sabha must pass a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting, declaring that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest to create such a service.

Parliament may then provide by law for the creation and regulation of the service.

Existing All India Services

  • Indian Administrative Service (IAS).
  • Indian Police Service (IPS).
  • Indian Forest Service (IFoS).

The Indian Forest Service was constituted in 1966 under the relevant statutory framework.

Distinctive features

  • Recruitment is conducted through a national-level process.
  • Officers are allocated to State cadres or joint cadres.
  • Officers serve in State administrations as well as on deputation to the Union.
  • Service conditions are governed by parliamentary laws and applicable rules.
  • Cadre management involves both Union and State governments.

Importance for Indian federalism

  • Promotes common administrative standards across States.
  • Provides an institutional connection between Union and State administrations.
  • Facilitates national coordination in areas such as public order, development and environmental management.
  • Supports continuity and professional expertise in administration.
  • Can facilitate the transfer of administrative experience across regions.

Concerns and criticisms

  • Cadre management tensions: Differences may arise between Union and State governments over postings, deputation and service control.
  • Political neutrality: Officers may face pressure to act in ways inconsistent with professional independence.
  • State autonomy: States may seek greater control over officers serving in their administrations.
  • Accountability: Dual institutional responsibilities can create complexity in supervision and performance evaluation.
  • Capacity concerns: Vacancies and uneven distribution of experienced personnel may affect administrative effectiveness.

8. Union's Duty to Protect States

Article 355

Article 355 imposes a duty on the Union to protect every State against external aggression and internal disturbance and to ensure that the government of every State is carried on in accordance with the Constitution.

It reflects the Union's responsibility to safeguard the constitutional order and territorial integrity of India.

Article 355 is not, by itself, a general licence for unrestricted intervention in State administration. Any action must have a valid constitutional basis and comply with the relevant safeguards.

Relationship with Article 356

Article 356 provides for President's Rule where the government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution, subject to the constitutional conditions and parliamentary approval requirements.

Article 355 and Article 356 are related but distinct. The duty under Article 355 does not automatically justify the use of Article 356.

The constitutional limits on President's Rule and judicial review are examined in the chapters on Emergency Provisions and Judicial Review.

9. Failure to Comply with Union Directions

Article 365

Article 365 provides that where a State has failed to comply with, or give effect to, directions given by the Union under the Constitution, the President may hold that a situation has arisen in which the government of the State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution.

Such a conclusion may be relevant to the exercise of powers under Article 356, but it is not an automatic consequence of every instance of non-compliance.

Constitutional requirements, the actual circumstances and judicially established limits must be respected.

10. Administrative Relations during Emergencies

National Emergency

During a Proclamation of National Emergency, the Union's executive power can extend to giving directions to a State regarding the manner in which its executive power is to be exercised.

President's Rule

Under Article 356, where a valid proclamation is in operation, the President may assume specified State executive functions and powers of the Governor or other State authorities, subject to constitutional limitations.

Financial Emergency

Article 360 permits the Union to issue directions to States to observe specified canons of financial propriety. It also provides for other financial directions within the constitutional framework.

Emergency provisions alter the ordinary balance of Union-State relations and must be understood alongside the safeguards against arbitrary use of central authority.

11. Administrative Relations and the Governor

The Governor is the constitutional head of the State executive. The office has an important place in the Union-State relationship, but the Governor is not intended to function as an unrestricted agent of the Union.

The Governor ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the State Council of Ministers, except where the Constitution expressly provides for discretion.

Areas of federal concern

  • Appointment and removal of Governors.
  • Reservation of Bills for the consideration of the President.
  • Reporting constitutional breakdown under Article 356.
  • Communication between the State government and the Union.
  • Exercise of constitutionally recognised discretionary functions.

The Governor's constitutional position, powers and controversies are discussed in detail in Governor.

12. Important Judicial Decisions

State of West Bengal v. Union of India (1963)

The Supreme Court examined the nature of Indian federalism and the relationship between Union and State powers. The judgment emphasised the constitutional supremacy of the Union in specified matters and rejected the argument that States possess sovereignty in the same sense as independent entities.

S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)

The Supreme Court placed important limits on the use of Article 356 and held that a proclamation of President's Rule is subject to judicial review.

The judgment strengthened federalism as a constitutional principle and established that legislative majority should ordinarily be tested on the floor of the House.

Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006)

The Supreme Court examined the dissolution of the Bihar Legislative Assembly and held that the relevant constitutional action was subject to judicial review. The case illustrates the need for constitutional restraint in the exercise of Union powers affecting State governments.

Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (2018)

The Constitution Bench discussed federalism, representative government and the relationship between the elected government of Delhi and the Lieutenant Governor.

The judgment emphasised constitutional morality, collaborative functioning and the importance of respecting the representative character of government.

13. Major Issues in Administrative Federalism

Centralisation versus State autonomy

Extensive Union directions and central administrative control may reduce the practical autonomy of State governments. At the same time, national coordination is necessary in areas involving common standards and national interests.

Political use of constitutional offices

Disputes concerning Governors, State governments and the use of constitutional powers can create distrust between the Union and States.

Implementation capacity

States often carry the primary administrative burden of implementing national laws and programmes. Inadequate personnel, funding or institutional capacity can weaken implementation.

Coordination failures

Overlapping responsibilities, different priorities and fragmented administrative systems can delay public service delivery.

All India Services tensions

Disagreements over deputation, cadre management and administrative accountability can affect the functioning of All India Services.

14. Reforms for Cooperative Administrative Federalism

  1. Strengthen consultation: Use regular institutional forums to discuss proposed directions and policies affecting States.
  2. Respect constitutional boundaries: Exercise Union powers in accordance with constitutional provisions and established conventions.
  3. Improve coordination: Establish clear administrative responsibilities and coordination mechanisms for subjects involving multiple governments.
  4. Strengthen State capacity: Improve staffing, technical expertise, financial resources and administrative infrastructure at the State level.
  5. Protect civil-service neutrality: Promote professional integrity, merit-based administration and accountability to constitutional values.
  6. Improve All India Services management: Make deputation and cadre management processes transparent, predictable and consistent with the applicable legal framework.
  7. Promote cooperative implementation: Involve States in policy design and implementation where they carry significant administrative responsibilities.
  8. Strengthen dispute prevention: Use consultation, mediation and intergovernmental institutions to resolve disagreements before they become prolonged conflicts.

15. Key Takeaways

  • Articles 73 and 162 define the general extent of Union and State executive powers.
  • Articles 256 and 257 provide for Union directions to States in specified circumstances.
  • Articles 258 and 258A enable the entrustment of executive functions between the Union and States.
  • Articles 261–263 support intergovernmental recognition, dispute mechanisms and coordination.
  • Article 312 provides the constitutional basis for creating All India Services.
  • Article 355 places a duty on the Union to protect States against external aggression and internal disturbance and to ensure constitutional government.
  • Article 365 concerns failure to comply with Union directions and may be relevant to Article 356, but does not automatically trigger President's Rule.
  • Effective administrative federalism requires both national coordination and respect for State autonomy.
