Nature of Indian Federalism

Indian federalism is a constitutional arrangement that distributes public authority between the Union and the States while preserving the unity and integrity of the country. It combines constitutionally protected State governments with a comparatively strong Union, and relies on cooperation, coordination and constitutional adjudication to manage the country's diversity.

1. Meaning and Constitutional Idea of Federalism

Federalism is a system of government in which constitutional authority is divided between at least two levels of government. Each level has its own sphere of responsibility and derives its authority from the Constitution rather than merely from the administrative delegation of another level.

Federalism combines two principles:

  • Self-rule: Constituent units exercise authority over matters assigned to them.
  • Shared rule: Constituent units participate, directly or indirectly, in national decision-making and common institutions.

Federal arrangements seek to reconcile national unity with regional autonomy. They are particularly relevant in large and diverse societies where linguistic, cultural, geographical and economic differences require constitutionally recognised spaces for regional decision-making.

Federalism as a constitutional arrangement

In a federation, the powers of different levels of government are constitutionally distributed. Neither level is ordinarily free to abolish the other or unilaterally rewrite the constitutional distribution of powers. An independent judiciary interprets the Constitution and adjudicates disputes concerning the respective spheres of authority.

2. Constitutional Foundation of Indian Federalism

The Constitution does not describe India as a federation of States. Article 1 declares:

India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.

The expression reflects the constitutional understanding that the Indian Union is not the result of an agreement among sovereign States that retain a unilateral right to withdraw. The Constitution establishes the Union and provides for the existence, powers and reorganisation of States within it.

Important constitutional features

  • Article 1: Describes India as a Union of States and identifies its territory.
  • Article 2: Enables Parliament to admit into the Union, or establish, new States on such terms and conditions as it thinks fit.
  • Article 3: Empowers Parliament to form new States and alter the areas, boundaries or names of existing States, following the constitutionally prescribed procedure.
  • Article 4: Provides that laws made under Articles 2 and 3 may include supplemental, incidental and consequential provisions and are not treated as constitutional amendments under Article 368.
  • Articles 245–255: Establish the principal framework for legislative relations between the Union and the States.
  • Articles 268–293: Deal with important aspects of financial relations between the Union and the States.
  • Article 131: Gives the Supreme Court original jurisdiction in specified federal disputes.
  • Article 263: Provides for the establishment of an Inter-State Council.

Federalism is also part of the Constitution's Basic Structure. Parliament's power to amend the Constitution cannot be used to destroy the federal character of the constitutional system.

3. Federal Features of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution contains several features commonly associated with federal systems.

Written and supreme Constitution

The constitutional distribution of powers is contained in a written Constitution. Ordinary legislation and executive action must remain consistent with constitutional provisions.

Division of legislative powers

The Seventh Schedule distributes legislative subjects between the Union and the States through the Union List, State List and Concurrent List. This division provides constitutionally recognised spheres of legislative authority.

Constitutional status of State governments

State governments are not simply administrative agencies of the Union. They have their own elected legislatures, executives and constitutionally assigned responsibilities.

Independent judiciary

The Supreme Court and High Courts interpret the Constitution, examine the legality of governmental action and adjudicate disputes arising under the constitutional framework.

Bicameral Union Legislature

The Rajya Sabha provides a forum for representation of the States at the Union level. It also has special constitutional powers in specified federal circumstances, including under Articles 249 and 312.

Special amendment procedure for federal provisions

Under the proviso to Article 368(2), an amendment affecting specified federal provisions requires ratification by the legislatures of not less than one-half of the States before it is presented to the President for assent.

4. Unitary and Centralising Features

The Indian Constitution also gives the Union substantial powers to preserve national unity, maintain constitutional governance and respond to exceptional circumstances. These features distinguish India's federal arrangement from some classical federations.

  • Strong Union: The Union has important legislative, administrative and financial powers.
  • Single Constitution: The constitutional framework generally applies to both the Union and the States.
  • Single citizenship: The Constitution provides for Indian citizenship rather than separate Union and State citizenship.
  • Integrated judiciary: The country has a unified judicial hierarchy rather than separate federal and State court systems.
  • Parliamentary power over State boundaries: Parliament may alter the areas, boundaries and names of States under Article 3, subject to the prescribed procedure.
  • Emergency provisions: National, State and financial emergency provisions can substantially affect the ordinary distribution of governmental authority.
  • Union role in State administration: Articles 256 and 257 provide mechanisms governing the exercise of executive power by States in specified circumstances.
  • All India Services: These services create an administrative link between the Union and the States.
  • Integrated electoral framework: The Election Commission of India conducts elections to Parliament, State Legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President.

These provisions do not make State governments constitutionally insignificant. They reflect the particular balance between regional autonomy and national coordination chosen by the Constitution.

5. Federal and Unitary Features: A Comparison

Basis Federal orientation Unitary or centralising orientation
Constitution Written and supreme Constitution. Single constitutional framework for the Union and States.
Government Constitutionally recognised Union and State governments. Strong Union institutions and coordinating powers.
Legislation Constitutional division of legislative subjects. Parliament can legislate on State List matters in specified circumstances.
Judiciary Independent judiciary and constitutional adjudication. Integrated judicial system.

6. Why India Adopted a Strong Union

The constitutional design emerged in the context of Partition, the integration of princely States, social and economic inequalities, linguistic and cultural diversity, and the need to establish stable institutions after Independence.

The framers sought to preserve regional self-government without creating conditions that could weaken national unity or obstruct coordinated action.

Major considerations

  • National integration: The need to consolidate a newly independent country and maintain territorial integrity.
  • Administrative continuity: The need for coordination across regions and a common administrative framework.
  • Economic planning and development: The need to address regional disparities and mobilise resources for national development.
  • Security: The need for coordinated responses to external threats and serious internal disturbances.
  • Social transformation: The need for common constitutional commitments to equality, liberty and social justice.
  • Diversity: The need to accommodate regional, linguistic and cultural differences within a common political framework.

A strong Union was therefore intended to support national cohesion. It was not intended to eliminate the constitutional role of the States.

7. Is India Federal, Unitary or Quasi-Federal?

The nature of Indian federalism has been discussed by constitutional scholars and examined by the Supreme Court. Descriptions such as federal, quasi-federal and cooperative federalism attempt to explain different aspects of the constitutional arrangement.

Federal interpretation

The federal interpretation emphasises the written Constitution, constitutional division of powers, elected State governments, independent judiciary and special amendment procedure for specified federal provisions.

Quasi-federal interpretation

The term quasi-federal is often used to describe the combination of federal institutions with a comparatively strong Union and centralising constitutional provisions. It is a descriptive academic expression, not a separate constitutional classification expressly adopted by the Constitution.

Cooperative federal interpretation

Cooperative federalism highlights the practical need for the Union and States to work together in areas involving shared responsibilities, common resources and national development priorities.

Constitutional position

Indian federalism is best understood by examining the actual distribution of constitutional powers and the operation of institutions, rather than relying exclusively on a single label.

8. Important Judicial Interpretations

Case Year Constitutional significance
State of West Bengal v. Union of India 1963 The Supreme Court examined the nature of the Indian Union and rejected the proposition that States possess sovereignty independent of the Constitution.
Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala 1973 Established the Basic Structure doctrine, limiting Parliament's power to amend the Constitution.
S.R. Bommai v. Union of India 1994 Recognised federalism as part of the Basic Structure and placed constitutional limits on the use of Article 356.
Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India 2006 Examined the constitutional limits on the exercise of gubernatorial powers and the dissolution of a Legislative Assembly.

Significance of S.R. Bommai

The judgment is particularly important because it connected federalism with the Basic Structure doctrine and strengthened judicial review of proclamations under Article 356. It affirmed that State governments cannot be displaced through arbitrary or constitutionally improper action.

9. Major Tensions in Indian Federalism

The constitutional distribution of powers does not eliminate disagreements between the Union and the States. Federal tensions arise from political differences, administrative arrangements, fiscal dependencies and competing development priorities.

  • Political differences: Conflicts may arise when different political parties govern the Union and individual States.
  • Legislative overlap: Concurrent List subjects can create disagreements over policy priorities and legislative competence.
  • Fiscal imbalance: States have substantial expenditure responsibilities but do not possess equivalent independent revenue powers.
  • Administrative friction: Disputes may arise over the role of Governors, centrally sponsored schemes and Union directions.
  • Regional disparities: States differ in their fiscal capacity, infrastructure, natural resources and development needs.
  • Inter-State disputes: Water sharing, boundaries and resource allocation can generate prolonged disagreements.
  • Centralising tendencies: Excessive concentration of decision-making may weaken State autonomy and responsiveness to local conditions.

These tensions require constitutional restraint, consultation, transparent fiscal arrangements and effective dispute-resolution institutions.

10. Principles for a Balanced Federal System

Constitutional supremacy

Both the Union and the States must exercise their powers within constitutional limits. Neither level can claim authority beyond the Constitution.

Respect for institutional autonomy

Each level of government should have meaningful space to discharge its constitutionally assigned responsibilities.

Consultation and cooperation

Governments should use institutional forums to discuss shared policy concerns, fiscal questions and inter-State issues.

Fiscal fairness

Financial arrangements should consider both the expenditure responsibilities of governments and differences in their revenue-raising capacity.

Subsidiarity

Public functions should, where appropriate, be performed by the lowest level of government capable of carrying them out effectively. This principle supports decentralisation while recognising the need for higher-level coordination.

Constitutional adjudication

Disputes concerning constitutional powers should be addressed through established legal and institutional mechanisms, including judicial remedies where constitutionally available.

11. Significance of Federalism for Indian Democracy

  • Accommodation of diversity: It allows regional aspirations and differences to find expression within a common constitutional framework.
  • Democratic participation: It creates multiple elected levels of government and opportunities for political participation.
  • Policy responsiveness: States can adapt policies to local social, economic and geographical conditions.
  • Checks on concentration of power: Distribution of authority creates institutional limits on the excessive concentration of governmental power.
  • Innovation: States can experiment with policy approaches and share successful practices.
  • Balanced development: Federal institutions can help address regional disparities through transfers, coordination and joint programmes.

12. Key Takeaways

  • Article 1 describes India as a Union of States.
  • Indian federalism combines constitutionally recognised State governments with a comparatively strong Union.
  • Legislative, administrative and financial relations are governed by distinct constitutional provisions.
  • The Constitution contains both federal and centralising features.
  • Federalism is part of the Basic Structure of the Constitution.
  • S.R. Bommai is a landmark judgment on federalism and Article 356.
  • Effective federalism requires constitutional respect, consultation, fiscal fairness and cooperation.
