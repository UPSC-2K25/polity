Election Commission of India

The Election Commission of India is an independent constitutional authority responsible for the superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President. It plays a central role in ensuring free and fair elections in the world's largest democracy.

1. Constitutional Background

The Constitution establishes the Election Commission under Article 324. The provision vests the Commission with the superintendence, direction and control of electoral processes within its constitutional jurisdiction.

The Election Commission is a constitutional body. Its authority does not originate merely from an Act of Parliament. Its constitutional foundation is Article 324, while several aspects of election administration are governed by parliamentary laws.

Constitutional provisions

Article Subject
Article 324Superintendence, direction and control of elections vested in the Election Commission.
Article 325One general electoral roll for every territorial constituency and prohibition of exclusion from electoral rolls on specified grounds.
Article 326Elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies on the basis of adult suffrage.
Article 327Power of Parliament to make provisions regarding elections to legislatures.
Article 328Power of State Legislatures to make provisions regarding elections to their legislatures, subject to the Constitution and parliamentary law.
Article 329Bar to interference by courts in electoral matters, subject to the constitutional scheme.
Article 243KState Election Commission for Panchayat elections.
Article 243ZAState Election Commission for Municipal elections.
Important distinction: The Election Commission of India conducts elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President. Panchayat and municipal elections are conducted by separate State Election Commissions.

2. Composition of the Election Commission

Article 324 provides for an Election Commission consisting of the Chief Election Commissioner and such number of other Election Commissioners, if any, as the President may from time to time fix.

When other Election Commissioners are appointed, the Chief Election Commissioner acts as the Chairperson of the Commission.

The Commission may also be assisted by Regional Commissioners appointed by the President after consultation with the Election Commission, as provided under Article 324(4).

Present institutional structure

  • One Chief Election Commissioner.
  • Two Election Commissioners.

The Commission is a multi-member body. Its decisions are taken according to the applicable decision-making framework, with the Chief Election Commissioner presiding over its proceedings.

3. Appointment of the Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners

Article 324(2) provides that the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners shall be appointed by the President, subject to the provisions of any law made by Parliament.

The Constitution originally did not prescribe a detailed selection committee procedure. Parliament subsequently enacted the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023.

Selection Committee under the 2023 Act

The Act provides for a Selection Committee consisting of:

  • The Prime Minister as Chairperson.
  • The Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha or the leader of the largest opposition party in that House, where applicable.
  • A Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.

The Act also provides for a Search Committee headed by the Minister of Law and Justice to prepare a panel of suitable candidates for consideration.

The Selection Committee may consider candidates beyond the panel prepared by the Search Committee, subject to the Act.

UPSC focus: Distinguish the constitutional provision in Article 324(2) from the statutory appointment procedure introduced by the 2023 Act.

4. Qualifications and Eligibility

Article 324 does not itself specify detailed educational or professional qualifications for appointment as Chief Election Commissioner or Election Commissioner.

The 2023 Act provides eligibility requirements. A person appointed must be holding or have held a post equivalent to the rank of Secretary to the Government of India and must have integrity and knowledge and experience in management and conduct of elections.

5. Tenure and Conditions of Service

The Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners hold office for six years from the date they assume office or until attaining the age of sixty-five years, whichever is earlier, under the 2023 statutory framework.

They are not eligible for reappointment.

The Act also provides for salary and service conditions corresponding to those of a Supreme Court judge.

6. Removal and Service Protection

Removal of the Chief Election Commissioner

Article 324(5) provides that the Chief Election Commissioner shall not be removed from office except in the same manner and on the same grounds as a judge of the Supreme Court.

This means that removal requires the constitutionally prescribed parliamentary process for proved misbehaviour or incapacity.

Removal of other Election Commissioners

An Election Commissioner or Regional Commissioner cannot be removed from office except on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner.

Office Removal protection
Chief Election Commissioner Same manner and grounds as a Supreme Court judge.
Other Election Commissioners Cannot be removed except on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner.
Regional Commissioners Cannot be removed except on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner.

7. Powers and Functions

7.1 Conduct of elections

The Commission supervises and administers elections to:

  • Lok Sabha.
  • Rajya Sabha.
  • State Legislative Assemblies.
  • State Legislative Councils, where they exist.
  • Office of the President of India.
  • Office of the Vice-President of India.

7.2 Preparation of electoral rolls

The Commission supervises the preparation and revision of electoral rolls for elections within its jurisdiction. Electoral registration officers and other officials perform statutory functions under election laws.

7.3 Recognition of political parties

The Commission registers political parties under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951. It also recognises political parties as national or State parties and allots election symbols under the applicable law and the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968.

7.4 Allotment of election symbols

The Commission regulates the reservation and allotment of election symbols. It also decides certain disputes concerning claims to recognised party symbols under the applicable legal framework.

7.5 Model Code of Conduct

The Commission issues and enforces the Model Code of Conduct during elections. The Code guides political parties, candidates and governments regarding appropriate conduct during the election period.

7.6 Election schedule

The Commission announces the schedule for elections within its jurisdiction, including the phases of polling and counting arrangements.

7.7 Monitoring election expenditure

The Commission monitors election expenditure by candidates and political parties under the applicable statutory framework and election instructions.

7.8 Advising on disqualification matters

Under Article 103, the President decides questions relating to disqualification of Members of Parliament under Article 102 after obtaining the opinion of the Election Commission and acting according to that opinion.

Under Article 192, the Governor decides corresponding questions relating to disqualification of members of State Legislatures under Article 191 after obtaining the opinion of the Election Commission and acting according to that opinion.

7.9 Election-related advisory functions

The Commission performs functions assigned by the Constitution and election laws, including specified advisory and administrative responsibilities.

8. Electoral Laws

The Election Commission functions within a framework of constitutional provisions and parliamentary legislation. Important election laws include:

8.1 Representation of the People Act, 1950

This Act primarily deals with allocation of seats in legislatures, delimitation-related matters and the preparation and revision of electoral rolls.

8.2 Representation of the People Act, 1951

This Act deals with the conduct of elections, qualifications and disqualifications of members, election disputes, corrupt practices and other matters relating to elections.

8.3 Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952

This Act provides the statutory framework for elections to the offices of President and Vice-President.

8.4 Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968

This Order regulates the reservation and allotment of election symbols to political parties and candidates.

9. Election Commission and State Election Commissions

The Election Commission of India and State Election Commissions are separate constitutional authorities. They have different electoral responsibilities.

Basis Election Commission of India State Election Commission
Constitutional provision Article 324. Articles 243K and 243ZA.
Head Chief Election Commissioner. State Election Commissioner.
Election responsibility Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President. Panchayats and Municipalities.
Appointment By the President under Article 324 and applicable law. By the Governor under the relevant constitutional provisions.
Removal protection Chief Election Commissioner has protection comparable to a Supreme Court judge; other Election Commissioners have the protection specified in Article 324(5). State Election Commissioner cannot be removed except in the manner and on the grounds prescribed for a High Court judge.

10. Model Code of Conduct

The Model Code of Conduct is a set of guidelines intended to ensure fair behaviour by political parties and candidates during elections.

Important principles

  • Political parties and candidates should avoid activities that aggravate existing differences or create hatred between communities.
  • Criticism should generally be confined to policies, programmes, past records and work.
  • Bribery, intimidation and other electoral malpractices are prohibited under applicable law.
  • Ministers should not misuse official machinery or public resources for electoral advantage.
  • Public announcements and use of government facilities must comply with the applicable restrictions.
  • Political parties should respect the electoral process and avoid practices that undermine free and fair polling.

The Model Code is not itself a comprehensive parliamentary statute. Its enforcement operates through the Commission's constitutional and statutory authority, election instructions and relevant provisions of existing laws.

11. Challenges Faced by the Election Commission

11.1 Money power

Excessive campaign expenditure and the use of unaccounted money can affect electoral fairness.

11.2 Criminalisation of politics

The participation of candidates facing serious criminal charges raises concerns about the integrity of democratic representation.

11.3 Misuse of official machinery

The use of public resources and government influence for electoral advantage can undermine the level playing field between contestants.

11.4 Paid news and misinformation

Misleading information, undisclosed political advertising and paid news can affect informed voter choice.

11.5 Internal party democracy

Concerns about opaque party functioning and candidate selection raise broader questions about democratic accountability within political organisations.

11.6 Institutional independence

The appointment process, financial autonomy and institutional safeguards of the Commission are important to public confidence in election administration.

11.7 Technology and electoral trust

Electronic voting, voter-verifiable paper audit trails, digital campaigning and misinformation require transparent procedures and effective public communication.

12. Important Supreme Court Judgments

12.1 Mohinder Singh Gill v. Chief Election Commissioner (1978)

The Supreme Court interpreted the broad powers under Article 324 and recognised the Election Commission's authority to act in areas where the law does not make adequate provision, subject to the Constitution and statutory law.

12.2 A.C. Jose v. Sivan Pillai (1984)

The Supreme Court clarified that Article 324 does not permit the Election Commission to override existing legislation governing elections. The Commission's powers operate in areas not covered by law and must remain consistent with statutory provisions.

12.3 T.N. Seshan v. Union of India (1995)

The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of a multi-member Election Commission and recognised the role of other Election Commissioners in the collective functioning of the institution.

12.4 Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms (2002)

The Supreme Court recognised the voter's right to information about candidates, including specified information concerning criminal antecedents, assets, liabilities and educational qualifications.

12.5 People's Union for Civil Liberties v. Union of India (2003)

The Supreme Court reinforced the importance of the voter's right to information and examined disclosure requirements concerning election candidates.

12.6 Subramanian Swamy v. Election Commission of India (2013)

The Supreme Court recognised the importance of the voter-verifiable paper audit trail system in ensuring transparency and confidence in the electoral process.

12.7 Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India (2023)

The Supreme Court addressed the appointment process for the Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners. It directed that appointments should be made on the recommendation of a committee consisting of the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha and the Chief Justice of India until Parliament made a law on the subject.

Parliament subsequently enacted the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023, which provides a different statutory Selection Committee structure.

13. Reforms to Strengthen the Election Commission

  • Strengthen transparency and public confidence in the appointment process.
  • Provide adequate administrative and financial resources.
  • Ensure impartial enforcement of election laws and the Model Code of Conduct.
  • Improve monitoring of election expenditure and political advertising.
  • Strengthen action against electoral misinformation and illegal inducements.
  • Promote transparency in political party funding.
  • Improve voter education and accessibility for persons with disabilities and other eligible voters.
  • Use technology with verifiable safeguards and transparent procedures.
  • Strengthen institutional independence and accountability.

  • The Election Commission is established under Article 324.
  • Article 324 covers elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President.
  • Article 326 provides for adult suffrage.
  • Article 325 provides for one general electoral roll for every territorial constituency.
  • The Representation of the People Acts of 1950 and 1951 are major election laws.
  • The Chief Election Commissioner has removal protection comparable to a Supreme Court judge.
  • Other Election Commissioners cannot be removed except on the recommendation of the Chief Election Commissioner.
  • Article 243K deals with Panchayat elections.
  • Article 243ZA deals with municipal elections.
  • State Election Commissions are separate from the Election Commission of India.
  • The Model Code of Conduct guides the behaviour of political parties and candidates during elections.
  • Anoop Baranwal (2023) is an important judgment concerning appointments to the Election Commission.

Which Article establishes the Election Commission of India?

Answer: Article 324.

Question 2

Who appoints the Chief Election Commissioner?

Answer: The President of India, under Article 324 and the applicable law.

Question 3

Which constitutional provision deals with adult suffrage?

Answer: Article 326.

Question 4

Who conducts elections to Panchayats?

Answer: The State Election Commission under Article 243K.

Question 5

Answer: The State Election Commission under Article 243ZA.
