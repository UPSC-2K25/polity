State Public Service Commissions

State Public Service Commissions are constitutional bodies responsible for conducting recruitment examinations and advising State Governments on specified matters relating to public services. They help ensure merit-based recruitment and professional administration at the State level.

1. Constitutional Background

State Public Service Commissions are established under Article 315 of Part XIV of the Constitution of India. Articles 315 to 323 provide the constitutional framework governing Public Service Commissions at the Union and State levels.

Every State has its own Public Service Commission. The Constitution also permits the creation of a Joint State Public Service Commission for two or more States.

2. Types of Public Service Commissions

Article 315 recognises three institutional arrangements for Public Service Commissions.

2.1 Union Public Service Commission

The Union Public Service Commission is responsible for recruitment to services under the Union and performs advisory functions specified in the Constitution.

2.2 State Public Service Commission

Each State has its own Public Service Commission. It conducts examinations and performs advisory functions relating to services under that State.

2.3 Joint State Public Service Commission

Two or more States may agree to have a Joint State Public Service Commission. Parliament may establish such a Commission by law after the concerned State Legislatures pass resolutions requesting its creation.

A Joint State Public Service Commission is a statutory body rather than a constitutional body created directly by the Constitution. Article 315 provides the constitutional basis for its establishment.

Prelims distinction: UPSC and individual State PSCs are constitutional bodies. A Joint State PSC is established by an Act of Parliament under Article 315(2).

3. Composition of State PSC

Article 316 provides that the Chairman and other members of a State Public Service Commission are appointed by the Governor of the State.

The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed number of members for each State PSC. The Governor determines the number of members and their conditions of service through regulations, subject to the constitutional framework.

As nearly as may be, one-half of the members of a Public Service Commission should be persons who have held office for at least ten years under the Government of India or a State Government.

4. Appointment of Chairman and Members

The Governor appoints the Chairman and other members of a State Public Service Commission under Article 316.

The appointment is made through the applicable governmental process. The Constitution does not prescribe a separate constitutional selection committee for these appointments.

The Chairman and members of a State PSC hold office for six years or until attaining the age of sixty-two years, whichever is earlier.

5. Tenure and Conditions of Service

Under Article 316, a member of a State Public Service Commission holds office for six years from the date of entering office or until attaining the age of sixty-two years, whichever is earlier.

A member may resign by submitting a written resignation to the Governor.

A member is not eligible for reappointment to the same office after completing the term.

6. Removal and Suspension

An important feature of State Public Service Commissions is that their members are appointed by the Governor but can be removed only by the President.

Article 317 provides the procedure for removal and suspension of members of Public Service Commissions.

6.1 Removal on grounds of misbehaviour

The President may remove the Chairman or a member of a State PSC on the ground of misbehaviour after the Supreme Court, on a reference made by the President, conducts an inquiry and reports that the person ought to be removed.

During the inquiry, the President may suspend the member from office.

6.2 Other grounds for removal

The President may also remove a member if the member:

  • Is adjudged an insolvent.
  • Engages during the term of office in any paid employment outside the duties of the office.
  • Is, in the opinion of the President, unfit to continue in office by reason of infirmity of mind or body.

6.3 Deemed misbehaviour

A member is deemed guilty of misbehaviour if the member is concerned or interested in a contract or agreement made by or on behalf of the Government of India or a State Government, or participates in its profit or benefit other than as an ordinary member of an incorporated company.

Important distinction: The Governor appoints State PSC members, but the President has the constitutional authority to remove them.

7. Restrictions on Further Employment

Article 319 imposes restrictions on further employment after a person ceases to hold office in a Public Service Commission.

A Chairman of a State PSC may be appointed as Chairman or Member of UPSC or as Chairman of another State PSC, but is not eligible for other employment under the Government of India or a State Government.

A member of a State PSC may be appointed as Chairman or Member of UPSC or as Chairman of that or another State PSC, but is not eligible for other government employment.

8. Functions of State Public Service Commissions

Article 320 provides the functions of Public Service Commissions. A State PSC performs recruitment and advisory functions relating to State services.

8.1 Conducting examinations

State PSCs conduct examinations for appointments to services under the respective State Governments. These may include State civil services and other State-level administrative and technical posts.

8.2 Direct recruitment

State PSCs conduct recruitment examinations, interviews and other selection processes for eligible candidates seeking appointment to notified State posts.

8.3 Recruitment methods

The Commission is consulted on methods of recruitment to civil services and civil posts under the State.

8.4 Promotions and transfers

The Commission is consulted on principles to be followed in making appointments, promotions and transfers from one service to another, wherever consultation is required.

8.5 Disciplinary matters

The Commission is consulted on disciplinary matters affecting persons serving under the State Government, within the scope of Article 320 and applicable rules.

8.6 Claims relating to legal expenses and injuries

The Commission may be consulted on claims relating to legal expenses incurred in defending proceedings connected with official duties and claims for compensation pensions in respect of injuries sustained in the course of official duty.

8.7 Advisory functions

The Commission advises the State Government on matters referred to it under the Constitution and applicable rules.

9. Exceptions to Consultation

Article 320 provides for consultation with Public Service Commissions on specified service matters. However, consultation is not mandatory in every situation.

Article 320(3) permits the Governor to make regulations specifying matters in which consultation with the State PSC is not necessary.

Article 320(4) provides that consultation is not required in respect of the manner in which provisions under Article 16(4) may be made or the manner in which effect may be given to Article 335.

The constitutional scheme therefore allows consultation requirements to be regulated while preserving the Commission's advisory role.

10. Independence of State PSCs

The Constitution provides several safeguards intended to protect the independence and impartiality of State Public Service Commissions.

  • Constitutional status: State PSCs are provided for directly under Article 315.
  • Security of tenure: Members have a fixed constitutional term, subject to the prescribed age limit.
  • Removal by President: The Governor cannot independently remove a State PSC member.
  • Supreme Court inquiry: Removal on grounds of misbehaviour requires an inquiry by the Supreme Court following a presidential reference.
  • Restrictions on further employment: Article 319 limits certain post-tenure government appointments.
  • Charged expenditure: Under Article 322, expenses of a State PSC are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State.
  • Annual reporting: The Commission submits an annual report to the Governor under Article 323.

11. Expenses and Financial Independence

Article 322 provides that the expenses of a Public Service Commission, including salaries, allowances and pensions payable to its members or staff, are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India or the State, as applicable.

Therefore, the expenses of a State Public Service Commission are charged on the Consolidated Fund of that State.

Charged expenditure is not submitted to the vote of the legislature, although it may be discussed in the legislature.

12. Annual Report and Accountability

Under Article 323, a State Public Service Commission submits an annual report of its work to the Governor.

The Governor causes the report to be laid before the State Legislature, together with a memorandum explaining cases, if any, where the advice of the Commission was not accepted and the reasons for such non-acceptance.

This reporting mechanism enables the State Legislature to examine the Commission's work and the Government's response to its advice.

14. Joint State Public Service Commission

Article 315(2) provides that Parliament may by law provide for the appointment of a Joint State Public Service Commission for two or more States, if the legislatures of those States pass resolutions requesting such an arrangement.

The Joint Commission serves the participating States according to the law establishing it. Its members are appointed by the President.

The Constitution also provides for the possibility of a State Public Service Commission serving the needs of another State if requested by the Governor of that State and approved by the President.

Exam distinction: A Joint State PSC is not the same as a State PSC. It is established by parliamentary law after resolutions from the concerned State Legislatures.

15. Limitations and Challenges

15.1 Recruitment delays

Delays in conducting examinations, publishing results and completing appointment procedures can affect the functioning of State departments.

15.2 Examination-related concerns

Paper leaks, allegations of irregularities and administrative shortcomings can undermine confidence in recruitment procedures.

15.3 Administrative capacity

State PSCs must maintain adequate staff, technology and examination infrastructure to conduct large-scale recruitment fairly.

15.4 Transparency

Clear recruitment rules, timely communication and accessible grievance mechanisms are important for maintaining public confidence.

15.5 Independence and impartiality

The constitutional safeguards must be supported by impartial appointments, professional administration and transparent institutional practices.

17. Quick Revision

  • State PSCs are constitutional bodies under Article 315.
  • The Governor appoints the Chairman and members of a State PSC.
  • The President has the authority to remove State PSC members.
  • The tenure is six years or until attaining the age of sixty-two years, whichever is earlier.
  • Article 317 deals with removal and suspension.
  • Article 319 deals with restrictions on further employment.
  • Article 320 deals with functions of Public Service Commissions.
  • Article 322 provides for expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State.
  • Article 323 provides for annual reports.
  • A Joint State PSC is established by Parliament through a law after resolutions from the concerned State Legislatures.

18. Self-Assessment Questions

Question 1

Which Article provides for State Public Service Commissions?

Answer: Article 315.

Question 2

Who appoints the Chairman of a State Public Service Commission?

Answer: The Governor.

Question 3

Who has the authority to remove a State PSC member?

Answer: The President.

Question 4

What is the retirement age of a State PSC member?

Answer: Sixty-two years.

Question 5

Which Article deals with the functions of State PSCs?

Answer: Article 320.

Question 6

On which fund are the expenses of a State PSC charged?

Answer: The Consolidated Fund of the State.

Question 7

How is a Joint State Public Service Commission established?

Answer: By an Act of Parliament after the concerned State Legislatures pass resolutions requesting its establishment.
