National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)

The National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) is a constitutional body concerned with the safeguards and interests of socially and educationally backward classes. It examines complaints, monitors constitutional protections and advises the Union Government on matters relating to backward classes.

The Commission's present constitutional status was established through the 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018, which inserted Article 338B into the Constitution.

1. Historical Background

The demand for institutional safeguards for backward classes has a long history in Indian constitutional development. Several commissions and legal arrangements preceded the present constitutional body.

1.1 First Backward Classes Commission

The First Backward Classes Commission was appointed in 1953 under the chairmanship of Kaka Kalelkar. It submitted its report in 1955 and examined the identification of socially and educationally backward classes.

1.2 Second Backward Classes Commission

The Second Backward Classes Commission, commonly known as the Mandal Commission, was appointed in 1979 under the chairmanship of B. P. Mandal.

It submitted its report in 1980 and recommended measures for the advancement of socially and educationally backward classes, including reservation in public employment.

1.3 Statutory Commission of 1993

The National Commission for Backward Classes was originally established as a statutory body under the National Commission for Backward Classes Act, 1993.

The statutory Commission was created in the context of the implementation of the recommendations relating to Other Backward Classes and the need for a mechanism to examine inclusion and exclusion claims.

1.4 Constitutional Status in 2018

The 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018 gave the NCBC constitutional status by inserting Article 338B and related provisions, including Article 342A.

The 105th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2021 subsequently clarified the powers of States and Union Territories to identify and maintain their own lists of socially and educationally backward classes.

3. Composition

Under Article 338B(2), the NCBC consists of:

  • A Chairperson.
  • A Vice-Chairperson.
  • Three other Members.

The Commission therefore has a total strength of five members. The Constitution provides for the composition, while the conditions of service and tenure are determined through applicable rules.

4. Appointment and Tenure

Under Article 338B(3), the President appoints the Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and other Members by warrant under his or her hand and seal.

Their conditions of service and tenure of office are determined by the President through rules, subject to the constitutional framework.

5. Functions and Duties

Article 338B(5) lays down the principal functions of the NCBC. Its responsibilities include monitoring safeguards, investigating complaints and advising governments.

5.1 Investigation and Monitoring

The Commission investigates and monitors all matters relating to safeguards provided for socially and educationally backward classes under the Constitution, laws and government orders.

5.2 Inquiry into Complaints

The NCBC inquires into specific complaints concerning the deprivation of rights and safeguards of socially and educationally backward classes.

5.3 Participation in Development Planning

The Commission participates in and advises on the planning process of socio-economic development of socially and educationally backward classes. It also evaluates the progress of their development under the Union and State Governments.

5.4 Reporting to the President

The Commission presents reports to the President annually and at other times whenever it considers necessary. These reports examine the effectiveness of safeguards and identify matters requiring government attention.

5.5 Recommendations

The NCBC recommends measures that should be taken by the Union or State Governments for effective implementation of safeguards and protection of backward classes.

5.6 Other Functions

The Commission performs other functions relating to the protection, welfare and development of socially and educationally backward classes as may be specified by the President, subject to law.

6. Powers of the Commission

Article 338B(8) gives the NCBC certain powers while investigating matters or inquiring into complaints under Article 338B(5)(a) and (b).

The Commission has the powers of a civil court trying a suit, particularly in respect of the following matters:

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person from any part of India and examining them on oath.
  • Requiring the discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning any public record or copy from any court or office.
  • Issuing commissions for the examination of witnesses and documents.
  • Any other matter prescribed by the President through rules.

These powers assist the Commission in conducting inquiries. They do not make the NCBC an ordinary court or give it general judicial authority.

7. Reports and Government Action

Under Article 338B(6), the President causes the reports of the Commission to be laid before each House of Parliament, along with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed to be taken on the recommendations.

Where a report or any part of it concerns a State Government, a copy is forwarded to the Governor. The Governor causes it to be laid before the State Legislature, along with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed.

8. Consultation by Government

Article 338B(9) requires the Union and every State Government to consult the Commission on all major policy matters affecting socially and educationally backward classes.

This provision gives the NCBC an important consultative role. However, its recommendations are not automatically binding on the government in the manner of a judicial order.

9. Article 342A and Identification of Backward Classes

Article 342A deals with the identification of socially and educationally backward classes.

Following the 102nd Constitutional Amendment, the President was empowered to specify the Central List of socially and educationally backward classes for the purposes of the Central Government.

The 105th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2021 clarified that every State and Union Territory may, by law, prepare and maintain its own list of socially and educationally backward classes for its own purposes. Such a State or Union Territory list may differ from the Central List.

Therefore, the Central List and State lists must be distinguished. Inclusion in one list does not automatically establish inclusion in the other.

10. Role of the NCBC in Reservation Policy

The NCBC monitors safeguards and examines matters affecting socially and educationally backward classes. Its role should be understood alongside the constitutional provisions governing equality and reservation.

  • Article 15(4) enables special provisions for the advancement of socially and educationally backward classes.
  • Article 15(5) permits specified special provisions relating to admission in educational institutions, subject to its constitutional terms.
  • Article 16(4) permits reservation in appointments or posts in favour of backward classes that are not adequately represented in State services.
  • Article 340 provides for the appointment of a commission to investigate the conditions of backward classes.

The NCBC is not itself the authority that independently determines every reservation policy. Its constitutional functions include investigation, monitoring, consultation and recommendations.

13. Significance and Limitations

The NCBC provides a constitutional mechanism for monitoring safeguards and examining complaints relating to backward classes. It also offers an institutional channel for consultation on major policy matters.

Its effectiveness depends on the quality of its investigations, the government's response to its recommendations, adequate institutional capacity and coordination between the Union and State Governments.

The Commission is primarily an investigative, monitoring and recommendatory body. Its recommendations do not automatically operate as binding judicial orders.

  • The 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018 gave it constitutional status.

  • The Commission consists of a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three Members.
