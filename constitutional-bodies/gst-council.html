Goods and Services Tax Council (GST Council)

The Goods and Services Tax Council is a constitutional body established to make recommendations to the Union and State Governments on important matters relating to the Goods and Services Tax (GST).

It provides a common institutional forum in which the Union and the States participate in shaping the indirect tax system. The Council is an important example of cooperative federalism in India's fiscal structure.

1. Constitutional Background

The GST Council is provided under Article 279A of the Constitution. This Article was inserted by the Constitution (One Hundred and First Amendment) Act, 2016, which introduced the constitutional framework for GST.

GST replaced several indirect taxes imposed separately by the Union and the States with a coordinated system of taxation. The Council was created to facilitate coordination between the two levels of government.

The President was required to constitute the Council by order within sixty days from the commencement of the 101st Amendment Act. The Council was constituted in September 2016.

2. Composition of the GST Council

Article 279A(2) specifies the composition of the Council. It includes representatives of both the Union and the States.

Member Position in the Council
Union Finance Minister Chairperson of the Council.
Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue or Finance Member.
Minister in charge of Finance or Taxation, or another nominated Minister, of each State Government Members representing the States.

The State representatives choose one among themselves to be the Vice-Chairperson of the Council for such period as they may decide.

3. Decision-Making Procedure

Article 279A provides a special voting arrangement for recommendations of the Council.

3.1 Quorum

One-half of the total number of members of the GST Council constitutes the quorum for a meeting.

3.2 Voting Weight

Every decision is taken in accordance with the weighted voting system specified in Article 279A(9).

Category Voting Weight
Central Government One-third of the total weighted votes cast.
All State Governments taken together Two-thirds of the total weighted votes cast.

A decision requires a majority of not less than three-fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting.

The arrangement gives the States collectively a greater voting weight than the Union Government, while ensuring that neither level of government can make a decision independently under this voting formula.

4. Functions of the GST Council

Under Article 279A(4), the Council makes recommendations to the Union and the States on matters relating to GST. Its recommendations cover a broad range of tax-policy issues.

4.1 Taxes to be Subsumed

The Council recommends the taxes, cesses and surcharges levied by the Union, States and local bodies that may be subsumed in GST.

4.2 Goods and Services Subject to GST

It recommends the goods and services that may be subjected to or exempted from GST.

4.3 Model GST Laws

The Council recommends model GST laws, principles of levy, apportionment of GST levied on inter-State supplies and the principles governing the place of supply.

4.4 Threshold Limits

It recommends the threshold limit of turnover below which goods and services may be exempted from GST.

4.5 Tax Rates

The Council recommends tax rates, including floor rates with bands, for GST.

4.6 Special Provisions

It recommends special provisions for certain States, including those with special needs or circumstances identified under the constitutional framework.

4.7 Date for GST on Certain Petroleum Products

The Council recommends the date on which GST may be levied on petroleum crude, high-speed diesel, motor spirit (commonly known as petrol), natural gas and aviation turbine fuel.

4.8 Dispute Resolution

Article 279A(11) provides for the Council to establish a mechanism to adjudicate disputes arising out of its recommendations or their implementation between:

  • The Union and one or more States.
  • The Union and one or more States on one side and one or more other States on the other side.
  • Two or more States.

5. GST Council and Cooperative Federalism

GST affects the taxation powers and revenues of both the Union and the States. The Council creates a structured platform for discussion, negotiation and coordination.

Its composition and weighted voting arrangement reflect the federal character of the Indian Constitution. The Union and the States participate jointly in recommendations concerning the national GST framework.

The Council is therefore often described as an institutional expression of cooperative federalism and fiscal federalism.

7. Is the GST Council's Recommendation Binding?

An important constitutional question concerns the legal status of GST Council recommendations.

In Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd. (2022), the Supreme Court examined the constitutional position of the Council and the nature of its recommendations.

The Court held that the recommendations of the GST Council have persuasive value and are not binding on Parliament and State Legislatures when they exercise their respective legislative powers.

The Court emphasised that Article 246A gives Parliament and State Legislatures legislative authority in relation to GST, subject to the constitutional framework. The Council's recommendations are important for coordination, but they do not replace the constitutional law-making powers of the legislatures.

9. Important Judicial Decision

Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt. Ltd. (2022)

The Supreme Court clarified the constitutional relationship between the GST Council and the legislative powers of Parliament and State Legislatures.

The Court held that GST Council recommendations are not binding on the Union and State legislatures. They carry persuasive value and support cooperative decision-making within India's federal structure.

This judgment is important for understanding the balance between institutional coordination and the independent legislative powers conferred by the Constitution.
