National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST)

The National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) is a constitutional body established to protect the rights, interests and safeguards of Scheduled Tribes in India. It monitors constitutional and legal protections, investigates complaints, evaluates development measures and advises governments on matters affecting tribal communities.

The Commission recognises the distinct social, cultural, economic and geographical circumstances of Scheduled Tribes. Its constitutional framework is provided under Article 338A.

1. Constitutional Background

The original Constitution provided for a Special Officer for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes under Article 338. The arrangement was subsequently changed to a multi-member commission through the 65th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1990.

Initially, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes were covered by a common National Commission. The 89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 separated the combined commission into two distinct constitutional bodies:

  • National Commission for Scheduled Castes under Article 338.
  • National Commission for Scheduled Tribes under Article 338A.

The separate NCST became operational in 2004.

3. Composition

Under Article 338A(2), the Commission consists of:

  • A Chairperson.
  • A Vice-Chairperson.
  • Three other Members.

The NCST therefore has a total strength of five members. The Constitution provides for its composition, while the conditions of service and tenure are determined under applicable rules.

4. Appointment and Tenure

The President appoints the Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and other Members of the Commission by warrant under his or her hand and seal.

Their conditions of service and tenure of office are determined by the President through rules, subject to the constitutional framework.

5. Functions and Duties

Article 338A(5) sets out the principal functions of the NCST. The Commission performs monitoring, investigative, advisory and reporting functions.

5.1 Investigation and Monitoring

The Commission investigates and monitors all matters relating to safeguards provided for Scheduled Tribes under the Constitution, laws and government orders.

5.2 Inquiry into Complaints

The NCST inquires into specific complaints concerning the deprivation of rights and safeguards of Scheduled Tribes. It provides an institutional mechanism for examining grievances relating to constitutional and legal protections.

5.3 Participation in Planning

The Commission participates in and advises on the planning process of socio-economic development of Scheduled Tribes. It also evaluates the progress of their development under the Union and State Governments.

5.4 Reporting to the President

The Commission presents reports to the President annually and at other times whenever it considers necessary. These reports examine the effectiveness of safeguards and identify problems requiring government attention.

5.5 Recommendations

The Commission recommends measures that should be taken by the Union or State Governments for effective implementation of safeguards and protection of Scheduled Tribes.

5.6 Other Functions

The Commission performs other functions relating to the protection, welfare and development of Scheduled Tribes as may be specified by the President, subject to law.

6. Powers of the Commission

Article 338A(8) gives the NCST certain powers while investigating matters or inquiring into complaints under Article 338A(5)(a) and (b).

The Commission has the powers of a civil court trying a suit, particularly in respect of the following matters:

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person from any part of India and examining them on oath.
  • Requiring the discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning any public record or copy from any court or office.
  • Issuing commissions for the examination of witnesses and documents.
  • Any other matter prescribed by the President through rules.

These powers help the Commission conduct inquiries and collect relevant evidence. They do not make the NCST an ordinary court or give it general judicial authority.

7. Reports and Government Action

Under Article 338A(6), the President causes the reports of the Commission to be laid before each House of Parliament, along with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed to be taken on the recommendations.

Where a report or any part of it concerns a State Government, a copy is forwarded to the Governor. The Governor causes it to be laid before the State Legislature, along with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed.

8. Consultation by Government

Article 338A(9) requires the Union and every State Government to consult the Commission on all major policy matters affecting Scheduled Tribes.

This provision gives the NCST a consultative role in policy formulation. It does not make every recommendation of the Commission automatically binding on the government.

9. Meaning and Identification of Scheduled Tribes

Article 366(25) defines Scheduled Tribes as such tribes or tribal communities, or parts of or groups within such tribes or tribal communities, as are deemed under Article 342 to be Scheduled Tribes for the purposes of the Constitution.

Under Article 342, the President specifies the Scheduled Tribes in relation to a State or Union Territory by public notification. Parliament may include or exclude groups from the list by law.

The NCST monitors safeguards and investigates complaints relating to Scheduled Tribes. It does not independently determine or amend the constitutional list of Scheduled Tribes.

10. Tribal Areas and Special Constitutional Safeguards

The Constitution provides special administrative arrangements for tribal areas through the Fifth and Sixth Schedules. These arrangements recognise the need to protect tribal interests, traditions and forms of local self-governance.

Fifth Schedule

The Fifth Schedule deals with the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in States other than the tribal areas covered by the Sixth Schedule.

Sixth Schedule

The Sixth Schedule provides for autonomous district and regional councils in specified tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.

These Schedules establish important administrative safeguards. The NCST monitors broader constitutional protections for Scheduled Tribes, including issues relating to their welfare and development.

Amendment Significance
65th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1990 Created a multi-member National Commission for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in place of the earlier Special Officer arrangement.

13. Limitations and Significance

The NCST provides an institutional mechanism for monitoring safeguards, examining grievances and advising governments on tribal welfare and development.

Its work is particularly relevant in matters involving displacement, land and forest rights, access to education, employment, development programmes and protection of tribal identity.

The Commission is primarily an investigative, monitoring and recommendatory constitutional body. Its recommendations do not automatically operate as binding judicial orders.

14. Quick Revision

  • The NCST is a constitutional body under Article 338A.
  • The 89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 separated the NCST from the combined SC-ST Commission.
  • The separate Commission became operational in 2004.
  • It consists of a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three Members.
  • The President appoints all members.
  • It investigates complaints relating to deprivation of safeguards for Scheduled Tribes.
  • It has specified powers of a civil court under Article 338A(8).
  • It submits reports to the President under Article 338A(5)(d).
  • Its reports are placed before Parliament under Article 338A(6).
  • Governments must consult the Commission on major policy matters affecting Scheduled Tribes under Article 338A(9).
  • The NCST does not independently amend the list of Scheduled Tribes.
  • Articles 244, 275(1), 342 and the Fifth and Sixth Schedules are also important for tribal administration and safeguards.
