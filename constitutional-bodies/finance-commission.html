Finance Commission

The Finance Commission is a constitutional body that recommends how financial resources should be distributed between the Union and the States. It plays an important role in fiscal federalism by recommending tax devolution, grants-in-aid and measures to strengthen the financial position of local governments.

1. Constitutional Background

The Finance Commission is provided for under Article 280 of Part XII of the Constitution of India, which deals with finance, property, contracts and suits.

Article 280 requires the President to constitute a Finance Commission at the expiration of every fifth year or at such earlier time as the President considers necessary.

The Commission examines the financial relationship between the Union and the States and recommends principles for the distribution of specified financial resources.

2. Need for the Finance Commission

India follows a federal system in which legislative and administrative responsibilities are divided between the Union and the States. However, the distribution of financial resources does not always match the distribution of responsibilities.

The Union Government has access to important sources of revenue, while State Governments are responsible for delivering many essential public services, including health, education, agriculture, policing and local infrastructure.

This creates a need for an institutional mechanism to recommend a fair distribution of financial resources and address differences in the fiscal capacity of States.

Major reasons

  • To address vertical fiscal imbalance between the Union and the States.
  • To reduce horizontal financial inequalities among States.
  • To recommend the distribution of the divisible pool of Union taxes.
  • To recommend grants-in-aid to States.
  • To strengthen the financial resources of Panchayats and Municipalities.
  • To promote fiscal stability and cooperative federalism.

3. Composition of the Finance Commission

Article 280 provides that the Finance Commission consists of a Chairman and four other members.

Parliament is empowered to determine by law the qualifications required for appointment as members of the Commission and the manner in which they shall be selected.

The Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951 provides the statutory framework relating to qualifications, appointment and other matters concerning the Commission.

Qualifications of members

Under the 1951 Act, the Chairman should be a person having experience in public affairs.

The other members are selected from persons having relevant qualifications and experience in areas such as:

  • Judicial matters.
  • Government finances and accounts.
  • Financial and administrative matters.
  • Economics.

4. Appointment and Tenure

The President constitutes the Finance Commission and appoints its Chairman and members.

The Commission is constituted at the expiration of every fifth year or at an earlier time if the President considers it necessary.

The term of office of the Chairman and members is determined by the President through the applicable statutory framework.

The Commission may regulate its own procedure and has powers specified under the Constitution and the Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951.

5. Functions of the Finance Commission

Article 280(3) specifies the principal functions of the Finance Commission. The President refers these matters to the Commission, which makes recommendations.

5.1 Distribution of taxes between the Union and States

The Commission recommends the distribution between the Union and the States of the net proceeds of taxes that are to be divided between them under the Constitution.

It also recommends the allocation among the States of their respective shares of such proceeds.

5.2 Grants-in-aid to States

The Commission recommends the principles governing grants-in-aid of the revenues of States out of the Consolidated Fund of India.

It also recommends the amounts to be paid to States that are in need of assistance under Article 275.

5.3 Resources of Panchayats

The Commission recommends measures needed to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of Panchayats in the State, based on the recommendations made by the State Finance Commission.

5.4 Resources of Municipalities

It also recommends measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of Municipalities, based on the recommendations made by the State Finance Commission.

5.5 Other matters referred by the President

The President may refer any other matter to the Finance Commission in the interests of sound finance.

6. Vertical and Horizontal Devolution

Tax devolution is one of the most important responsibilities of the Finance Commission. It involves two different dimensions of distribution.

6.1 Vertical devolution

Vertical devolution refers to the distribution of the divisible pool of Union taxes between the Union Government and all the States together.

The Commission recommends the percentage of the divisible pool that should be allocated to the States collectively.

6.2 Horizontal devolution

Horizontal devolution refers to the distribution of the States' share among individual States.

The Commission uses specified criteria to determine how the share should be allocated among States. These criteria may consider population, income distance, area, forest cover, demographic performance and other relevant factors.

Remember: Vertical devolution concerns the Union-State distribution. Horizontal devolution concerns distribution among the States.

7. Divisible Pool of Taxes

The divisible pool refers to the net proceeds of those Union taxes that are shared between the Union and the States under Article 270.

The constitutional scheme excludes certain categories from the divisible pool, including Union surcharges under Article 271 and cesses levied for specific purposes.

Consequently, an increase in the proportion of revenue collected through cesses and surcharges can affect the size of the divisible pool available for distribution.

Important distinction

  • Divisible pool: The pool of taxes distributed between the Union and States under Article 270.
  • Surcharge: A surcharge under Article 271 is for purposes of the Union and is not shared with States through the divisible pool.
  • Cess: A cess levied for a specific purpose is generally outside the divisible pool.
  • Grants-in-aid: Financial assistance provided under constitutional provisions such as Article 275.

8. Grants-in-Aid

Grants-in-aid are financial transfers made by the Union to States to help meet specified financial requirements.

Article 275 provides for grants from the Consolidated Fund of India to States that Parliament may determine to be in need of assistance.

The Finance Commission recommends the principles governing such grants and the amounts to be provided to eligible States.

Purpose of grants

  • To address revenue deficits where recommended.
  • To support specific public services.
  • To address regional and developmental disparities.
  • To support local governments through recommended transfers.
  • To strengthen the fiscal capacity of States.

9. Local Government Finances

The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts strengthened local self-government by providing constitutional status to Panchayats and Municipalities.

The Finance Commission at the Union level has a role in recommending measures to supplement the resources of local governments through State finances.

9.1 Panchayats

Under Article 280(3)(bb), the Finance Commission recommends measures to augment the Consolidated Fund of a State to supplement the resources of Panchayats in the State, based on the recommendations of the State Finance Commission.

9.2 Municipalities

Under Article 280(3)(c), it recommends similar measures for Municipalities, based on the recommendations of the State Finance Commission.

State Finance Commission

Article 243-I provides for a State Finance Commission to review the financial position of Panchayats. Article 243-Y extends the relevant framework to Municipalities.

The State Finance Commission examines the financial relationship between the State Government and local bodies. Its recommendations provide an important input for the Union Finance Commission's local government-related responsibilities.

10. Procedure and Powers

Under the Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951, the Commission has specified powers to carry out its work.

In performing its functions, it has powers similar to those of a civil court in certain matters, including:

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of witnesses.
  • Requiring the production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning public records or copies from courts or offices.

The Commission may determine its own procedure and conduct consultations and studies necessary for preparing its recommendations.

11. Submission of Recommendations

Article 281 requires the President to cause every recommendation made by the Finance Commission, together with an explanatory memorandum as to the action taken on it, to be laid before each House of Parliament.

The recommendations are therefore presented to Parliament along with the Government's response.

The Finance Commission's recommendations are generally recommendatory in nature. The Government's response and the explanatory memorandum provide an important mechanism of financial and parliamentary accountability.

12. Finance Commission and Planning

The Finance Commission is a constitutional institution concerned primarily with the distribution of financial resources and fiscal relations between the Union and States.

It should not be confused with the former Planning Commission, which was an executive institution responsible for economic planning and allocation-related functions.

The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015. NITI Aayog is an executive policy think tank and is not a constitutional body.

13. Finance Commission and GST Council

The Finance Commission and GST Council are distinct constitutional institutions that influence India's fiscal federal structure.

The Finance Commission is established under Article 280 and recommends the distribution of specified financial resources.

The GST Council is established under Article 279A and makes recommendations on important GST-related matters, including tax rates, exemptions and model laws.

14. Importance in Indian Federalism

The Finance Commission is an important instrument of fiscal federalism. It helps balance the financial requirements of the Union and States while considering the different economic conditions of individual States.

Major contributions

  • Promotes a more equitable distribution of public revenue.
  • Helps reduce regional financial disparities.
  • Supports States with limited revenue-raising capacity.
  • Strengthens the financial position of local governments.
  • Encourages responsible fiscal management.
  • Provides an institutional forum for examining Union-State financial relations.

15. Challenges and Issues

15.1 Vertical fiscal imbalance

The distribution of taxation powers and expenditure responsibilities between the Union and States can create continuing fiscal imbalances.

15.2 Horizontal inequalities

States differ in population, income, geography, infrastructure and revenue capacity. Designing fair distribution criteria requires balancing these differences.

15.3 Cesses and surcharges

The use of cesses and surcharges, which are generally outside the divisible pool, can influence the resources available for tax devolution.

15.4 Fiscal discipline

The Commission must consider the need for adequate public expenditure while encouraging responsible borrowing and sustainable finances.

15.5 Local government capacity

Local bodies often face limitations in revenue generation, financial management and administrative capacity. Strengthening their financial position remains an important concern.

17. Quick Revision

  • The Finance Commission is a constitutional body under Article 280.
  • It consists of a Chairman and four other members.
  • The President constitutes the Commission every five years or earlier.
  • Article 280(3) specifies its principal functions.
  • Vertical devolution concerns distribution between the Union and States collectively.
  • Horizontal devolution concerns distribution among individual States.
  • Article 275 deals with grants-in-aid to certain States.
  • Article 281 deals with the presentation of recommendations to Parliament.
  • Article 243-I and Article 243-Y relate to State Finance Commissions.
  • The Finance Commission is different from the GST Council and NITI Aayog.
