National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC)

The National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC) is a constitutional body established to safeguard the rights and interests of Scheduled Castes in India. It monitors constitutional and legal safeguards, investigates complaints relating to the deprivation of rights, and advises the government on measures for the social and economic development of Scheduled Castes.

The Commission reflects the constitutional commitment to equality, social justice and protection against discrimination. Its present constitutional framework is provided under Article 338.

1. Constitutional Background

The Constitution originally provided for a Special Officer for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes under Article 338. The constitutional arrangement subsequently evolved into a commission with broader investigative and monitoring responsibilities.

The 65th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1990 replaced the Special Officer arrangement with a multi-member National Commission for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.

The 89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 separated the combined commission into two distinct constitutional bodies:

  • National Commission for Scheduled Castes under Article 338.
  • National Commission for Scheduled Tribes under Article 338A.

The separate commissions became operational in 2004.

3. Composition

Under Article 338(2), the Commission consists of:

  • A Chairperson.
  • A Vice-Chairperson.
  • Three other Members.

Thus, the Commission has a total strength of five members. The Constitution provides for the composition but leaves details of service conditions and tenure to be determined through applicable rules.

4. Appointment and Tenure

The President appoints the Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and other Members of the Commission by warrant under his or her hand and seal.

Their conditions of service and tenure of office are determined by the President through rules, subject to the constitutional framework.

The Commission is therefore a constitutional body whose members are appointed by the President.

5. Functions and Duties

Article 338(5) lays down the principal functions of the NCSC. Its responsibilities extend beyond receiving individual complaints and include monitoring safeguards and advising governments.

5.1 Investigation and Monitoring

The Commission investigates and monitors all matters relating to the safeguards provided for Scheduled Castes under the Constitution, laws and government orders.

5.2 Inquiry into Complaints

The NCSC inquires into specific complaints concerning the deprivation of rights and safeguards of Scheduled Castes. This provides an institutional channel for examining alleged violations of protective provisions.

5.3 Participation in Planning

The Commission participates in and advises on the planning process of socio-economic development of Scheduled Castes. It also evaluates the progress of their development under the Union and State Governments.

5.4 Reporting to the President

The Commission presents reports to the President annually and at other times whenever it considers necessary. These reports assess the working of constitutional safeguards and highlight issues requiring government attention.

5.5 Recommendations

The Commission recommends measures that should be taken by the Union or State Governments for effective implementation of safeguards and protection of Scheduled Castes.

5.6 Other Functions

The Commission performs other functions relating to the protection, welfare and development of Scheduled Castes as may be specified by the President, subject to law.

6. Powers of the Commission

Article 338(8) gives the NCSC certain powers while investigating matters or inquiring into complaints under Article 338(5)(a) and (b).

The Commission has the powers of a civil court trying a suit, particularly in respect of the following matters:

  • Summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person from any part of India and examining them on oath.
  • Requiring the discovery and production of documents.
  • Receiving evidence on affidavits.
  • Requisitioning any public record or copy from any court or office.
  • Issuing commissions for the examination of witnesses and documents.
  • Any other matter prescribed by the President through rules.

These powers help the Commission conduct inquiries effectively. However, possessing civil-court powers for specified inquiries does not make the NCSC an ordinary court or give it general judicial authority.

7. Reports and Government Action

Under Article 338(6), the President causes the reports of the Commission to be laid before each House of Parliament, along with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed to be taken on the recommendations.

Where a report or any part of it concerns a State Government, a copy is forwarded to the Governor. The Governor causes it to be laid before the State Legislature, along with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed.

The memorandum is important because it makes the government's response to the Commission's recommendations visible to the legislature.

8. Consultation by Government

Article 338(9) requires the Union and every State Government to consult the Commission on all major policy matters affecting Scheduled Castes.

This provision gives the NCSC an important consultative role in policy formulation. It does not, however, make every recommendation of the Commission automatically binding on the government.

9. Meaning and Identification of Scheduled Castes

Article 366(24) defines Scheduled Castes as such castes, races or tribes, or parts of or groups within such castes, races or tribes, as are deemed under Article 341 to be Scheduled Castes for the purposes of the Constitution.

Under Article 341, the President specifies the Scheduled Castes in relation to a State or Union Territory by public notification. Parliament may include or exclude groups from the list by law.

The NCSC monitors safeguards and investigates complaints relating to Scheduled Castes. It does not independently determine or amend the constitutional list of Scheduled Castes.

Amendment Significance

89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 Separated the combined commission into the NCSC under Article 338 and the NCST under Article 338A.

12. Limitations and Significance

The NCSC plays an important role in monitoring safeguards, investigating complaints and highlighting gaps in implementation. Its reports and recommendations can influence public policy and improve institutional accountability.

However, the Commission is primarily an investigative, monitoring and recommendatory constitutional body. Its recommendations do not automatically operate as binding judicial orders.

13. Quick Revision

  • The NCSC is a constitutional body under Article 338.
  • The 65th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1990 created a multi-member commission for SCs and STs.
  • The 89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 separated the NCSC and NCST.
  • The NCSC consists of a Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three Members.
  • The President appoints all members of the Commission.
  • The Commission investigates complaints relating to deprivation of safeguards.
  • It has specified powers of a civil court under Article 338(8).
  • It submits reports to the President under Article 338(5)(d).
  • Reports are placed before Parliament under Article 338(6).
  • Governments must consult the Commission on major policy matters affecting Scheduled Castes under Article 338(9).
  • The NCSC does not independently amend the list of Scheduled Castes.
