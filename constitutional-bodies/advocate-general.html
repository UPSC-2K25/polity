Advocate General of the State

The Advocate General is the highest law officer of a State in India. The office is provided under Article 165 of the Constitution. The Advocate General advises the State Government on legal matters and performs legal duties assigned by the Governor.

The office at the State level is broadly comparable to that of the Attorney General of India at the Union level. However, the Advocate General serves the State Government and exercises functions within the constitutional framework applicable to the State.

1. Constitutional Basis

Article 165, contained in Part VI of the Constitution, provides for the office of the Advocate General for each State.

Article Subject
Article 165 Appointment, qualifications, duties and tenure of the Advocate General.
Article 177 Rights of the Advocate General to participate in State Legislature proceedings.
Article 217(2) Qualifications for appointment as a High Court judge, which determine the qualification standard for the Advocate General.

2. Appointment

Under Article 165(1), the Governor appoints the Advocate General for the State.

The Constitution does not prescribe a separate selection committee or legislative approval process for the appointment. The appointment is made by the Governor in accordance with the constitutional framework governing the State executive.

3. Qualifications

Under Article 165(1), a person appointed as Advocate General must be qualified to be appointed as a judge of a High Court.

Article 217(2) provides the qualifications for appointment as a High Court judge. A person must:

  • Be a citizen of India; and
  • Have held a judicial office in the territory of India for at least ten years; or
  • Have been an advocate of a High Court, or of two or more such courts in succession, for at least ten years.

These are alternative professional qualifications. The Advocate General must satisfy the constitutional qualification standard for a High Court judge.

4. Tenure and Conditions of Office

Article 165(3) provides that the Advocate General holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.

  • No fixed constitutional term is prescribed.
  • The Advocate General holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.
  • The Constitution does not prescribe a separate removal procedure.
  • Remuneration is determined by the Governor.
  • The Advocate General may resign from office.

The Constitution does not require the Advocate General to resign automatically whenever the State Government changes. The practical continuation of the office depends on the constitutional arrangement and the confidence of the government being served.

5. Duties and Functions

Article 165(2) sets out the principal functions of the Advocate General. The office provides legal assistance to the State Government.

5.1 Legal Advice to the State Government

The Advocate General advises the State Government on legal matters referred by the Governor. Such advice may concern constitutional questions, legislation, administrative decisions and legal disputes.

5.2 Performance of Assigned Legal Duties

The Advocate General performs legal duties assigned by the Governor. The office also discharges functions conferred by the Constitution or any other law in force.

5.3 Representation of the State

The Advocate General represents the State Government in legal proceedings when required or assigned. This may include proceedings before the High Court and other courts within the applicable legal framework.

5.4 Right of Audience

As the State's highest law officer and an advocate, the Advocate General has a right of audience in courts in accordance with applicable law and professional rules. This should be distinguished from Article 76(3), which expressly grants the Attorney General a right of audience in all courts in India.

6. Rights in the State Legislature

Article 177 grants the Advocate General certain rights in the proceedings of the State Legislature. These rights allow the Advocate General to provide legal assistance during legislative discussions.

  • The Advocate General may speak and otherwise take part in the proceedings of the Legislative Assembly.
  • In a State having a Legislative Council, the Advocate General may participate in its proceedings as well.
  • The Advocate General may participate in a committee of the State Legislature of which the Advocate General is named as a member.
  • The Advocate General does not have the right to vote by virtue of Article 177.

The Advocate General does not automatically become a member of the State Legislature. The right to participate in proceedings is distinct from legislative membership and voting rights.

7. Restrictions and Professional Conduct

The Advocate General is the principal legal adviser to the State Government. The office must maintain professional standards and avoid conflicts between official responsibilities and private legal work.

The Constitution does not provide an exhaustive list of restrictions on private practice. Applicable professional rules, government conditions and conventions govern the conduct of the office.

Important principles include:

  • The Advocate General should not act against the legal interests of the State Government in matters connected with official responsibilities.
  • Conflicts of interest must be avoided.
  • Professional conduct must comply with applicable rules governing advocates.
  • Official legal advice and representation must be carried out in accordance with the duties of the office.

8. Status and Role in the State Executive

The Advocate General is the highest law officer of the State but is not a member of the State Council of Ministers merely by virtue of holding this office.

The office is not an independent constitutional authority in the same sense as the State Public Service Commission or the State Election Commission. The Advocate General serves the State Government and holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.

Legal advice given by the Advocate General is advisory. It does not constitute a judicial decision and does not bind courts.

9. Advocate General and Attorney General

The Attorney General is the highest law officer of the Union, whereas the Advocate General is the highest law officer of a State. Their offices are created by separate constitutional provisions.
