Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG)

The Comptroller and Auditor General of India is an independent constitutional authority responsible for auditing the accounts of the Union and State Governments and certain public authorities. The CAG safeguards public financial accountability by examining whether public money has been collected and spent in accordance with the Constitution, laws and applicable rules.

1. Constitutional Background

The office of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India is established under Article 148 of Part V of the Constitution.

Articles 148 to 151 provide the constitutional framework relating to the appointment, independence, duties, powers and audit reports of the CAG.

The CAG is often described as the guardian of the public purse because the office independently examines the financial transactions of government institutions and reports significant findings to the appropriate constitutional authorities.

2. Historical Background

The institution of public audit in India developed during British rule as part of the administrative system for supervising government expenditure.

  • 1858: The office of the Auditor General was developed in the colonial financial administration.
  • Government of India Act, 1919: Provided an important institutional framework for the Auditor General.
  • Government of India Act, 1935: Strengthened the constitutional and administrative position of the Auditor General.
  • 26 January 1950: The office became the Comptroller and Auditor General of India under the Constitution.

The Constitution gave the office independent status so that government expenditure could be audited without direct interference from the executive.

3. Appointment of the CAG

Under Article 148(1), the Comptroller and Auditor General of India is appointed by the President of India by warrant under his hand and seal.

Before entering office, the CAG makes and subscribes an oath or affirmation before the President or a person appointed by the President for this purpose.

The Constitution does not prescribe a separate constitutional selection committee for the appointment of the CAG.

4. Tenure and Conditions of Service

The CAG holds office for a term of six years or until attaining the age of sixty-five years, whichever is earlier.

The CAG may resign by submitting a written resignation to the President.

The conditions of service of the CAG are determined by Parliament through law.

After appointment, the conditions of service of the CAG cannot be varied to the disadvantage of the office-holder.

5. Removal of the CAG

Article 148(1) provides that the CAG can be removed from office only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court.

This means that the CAG cannot be removed merely at the discretion of the executive.

Grounds for removal

  • Proved misbehaviour.
  • Incapacity.

Removal procedure

The removal process follows the constitutional procedure applicable to a Supreme Court Judge. Each House of Parliament must pass an address supported by a majority of the total membership of that House and by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting.

The address must be presented to the President in the same session for removal on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.

Prelims fact: The CAG enjoys a removal procedure equivalent to that of a Supreme Court Judge.

6. Independence of the CAG

The Constitution provides several safeguards to ensure that the CAG can audit government finances independently.

6.1 Security of tenure

The CAG has a fixed term and can be removed only through the special constitutional procedure applicable to a Supreme Court Judge.

6.2 Protection of service conditions

The service conditions of the CAG cannot be varied to the office-holder's disadvantage after appointment.

6.3 Financial independence

The administrative expenses of the CAG's office, including salaries, allowances and pensions payable to persons serving in that office, are charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India under Article 148(6).

6.4 Restrictions after retirement

Under Article 148(4), the CAG is not eligible for any further office, either under the Government of India or under the Government of any State, after ceasing to hold office.

6.5 Administrative autonomy

The conditions of service of persons serving in the Indian Audit and Accounts Department and the administrative powers of the CAG are governed by the constitutional and statutory framework.

7. Duties and Powers of the CAG

Article 149 provides that the CAG shall perform such duties and exercise such powers in relation to the accounts of the Union, States and other authorities as may be prescribed by Parliament by law.

The principal statutory framework governing the duties and powers of the CAG is the Comptroller and Auditor General's (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971.

7.1 Audit of Union Government accounts

The CAG audits the accounts of the Union Government, including expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India, subject to the applicable legal framework.

7.2 Audit of State Government accounts

The CAG audits the accounts of State Governments and reports on their financial transactions.

7.3 Audit of expenditure

The CAG examines government expenditure to determine whether money was spent for the purposes authorised by law and in accordance with applicable rules.

7.4 Audit of receipts

The CAG examines the assessment, collection and allocation of government revenue to ensure that financial procedures and applicable laws are followed.

7.5 Audit of public sector enterprises

The CAG performs audit functions relating to government companies and certain statutory corporations in accordance with the applicable legal provisions.

7.6 Audit of autonomous bodies

The CAG audits certain authorities and bodies that receive substantial government funding or are specifically brought within its audit jurisdiction by law.

7.7 Certification of net proceeds

Under Article 279(1), the net proceeds of a tax or duty are ascertained and certified by the CAG. The certificate is final.

8. Types of Audit

The CAG conducts different forms of audit to examine public financial management from different perspectives.

8.1 Financial audit

Financial audit examines whether financial statements and accounts present a reliable picture of the financial position and transactions of the audited entity.

8.2 Compliance audit

Compliance audit examines whether the expenditure and activities of government entities conform to the Constitution, laws, rules, regulations and applicable instructions.

8.3 Performance audit

Performance audit examines whether government programmes and activities have been carried out with due regard to economy, efficiency and effectiveness.

Type of audit Main focus
Financial audit Reliability of financial statements and accounts.
Compliance audit Compliance with laws, rules and regulations.
Performance audit Economy, efficiency and effectiveness of programmes.

9. Article 150: Form of Accounts

Article 150 provides that the accounts of the Union and the States shall be kept in such form as the President may, on the advice of the CAG, prescribe.

This provision recognises the CAG's role in the accounting framework of government.

The distinction between maintaining government accounts and auditing them is important. The constitutional and statutory framework has evolved over time, including the separation of the accounting functions of the Union Government from the CAG's audit functions.

10. Article 151: Audit Reports

Article 151 provides for the submission of audit reports by the CAG.

10.1 Union Government reports

Reports of the CAG relating to the accounts of the Union are submitted to the President, who causes them to be laid before each House of Parliament.

10.2 State Government reports

Reports of the CAG relating to the accounts of a State are submitted to the Governor of that State, who causes them to be laid before the State Legislature.

These reports provide the legislature with independent information on government expenditure, revenue and financial administration.

11. CAG and Parliamentary Financial Control

The CAG plays an important role in helping Parliament and State Legislatures exercise control over public expenditure.

Audit reports are examined by legislative committees, particularly the Public Accounts Committee and, where relevant, the Committee on Public Undertakings.

11.1 Public Accounts Committee

The Public Accounts Committee examines government expenditure and considers the CAG's audit reports to determine whether public money has been used for authorised purposes.

11.2 Committee on Public Undertakings

The Committee on Public Undertakings examines the functioning and financial performance of selected public sector undertakings, using audit findings where relevant.

The CAG provides independent audit information, while legislative committees examine the findings and hold the executive accountable.

13. Role of the CAG in Public Sector Enterprises

The CAG has an important role in auditing government companies and certain public sector undertakings.

Under the Companies Act, 2013, the CAG appoints the statutory auditors of government companies and has specified powers relating to supplementary audit and comments on their audit reports.

The CAG may also conduct supplementary or test audits in accordance with the applicable legal framework.

These arrangements help ensure that public investments and resources in government companies are subject to independent scrutiny.

14. Limitations and Challenges

14.1 Audit is generally retrospective

Most audit findings relate to transactions that have already taken place. Audit therefore cannot always prevent irregularities before they occur.

14.2 Follow-up on audit observations

Effective accountability depends on government departments responding to audit findings and implementing corrective measures.

14.3 Expanding public sector activities

Increasingly complex public programmes, public-private arrangements and technology-driven services create new audit requirements.

14.4 Timely audit and reporting

Delays in producing accounts or completing audits can reduce the usefulness of audit findings for legislative scrutiny.

14.5 Technological complexity

Digital transactions, data systems and complex financial arrangements require specialised audit skills and modern tools.

15. Importance of the CAG in Indian Democracy

The CAG is an essential institution for ensuring financial accountability in a democratic system.

  • Promotes transparency in public expenditure.
  • Strengthens legislative control over government finances.
  • Helps detect irregularities and financial mismanagement.
  • Examines whether public funds are used for authorised purposes.
  • Encourages economy, efficiency and effectiveness in public programmes.
  • Supports responsible financial administration.
  • Strengthens public confidence in government institutions.

17. Quick Revision

  • The CAG is a constitutional authority under Article 148.
  • The CAG is appointed by the President.
  • The term is six years or until attaining the age of sixty-five years, whichever is earlier.
  • The CAG can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court Judge.
  • Article 149 deals with the duties and powers of the CAG.
  • Article 150 deals with the form of government accounts.
  • Article 151 deals with CAG audit reports.
  • Article 279 provides for certification of net proceeds by the CAG.
  • The CAG's reports are examined by legislative committees such as the Public Accounts Committee.
  • The CAG is not a court and does not itself impose criminal punishment for financial irregularities.
