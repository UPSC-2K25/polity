Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)

The Union Public Service Commission is a constitutional body responsible for conducting examinations for recruitment to various Union services and advising the Government of India on specified matters relating to recruitment, appointments, promotions, transfers and disciplinary matters. It supports merit-based recruitment and professional administration in India.

1. Constitutional Background

The Union Public Service Commission is provided for under Part XIV of the Constitution, which deals with Services under the Union and the States.

Articles 315 to 323 provide the constitutional framework for Public Service Commissions. These provisions cover their establishment, composition, appointment, tenure, removal, functions and reporting responsibilities.

2. Historical Background

The development of public service recruitment institutions in India has its roots in the colonial administrative system.

  • Government of India Act, 1919: Provided for the establishment of a Public Service Commission in India.
  • Lee Commission, 1924: Recommended the establishment of a Public Service Commission to regulate recruitment to public services.
  • 1 October 1926: The first Public Service Commission in India was established.
  • Government of India Act, 1935: Provided for a Federal Public Service Commission and Provincial Public Service Commissions.
  • 26 January 1950: The Federal Public Service Commission became the Union Public Service Commission under the Constitution of India.
Prelims fact: The first Public Service Commission in India was established on 1 October 1926.

3. Composition of UPSC

Article 316 provides that the Chairman and other members of the Union Public Service Commission shall be appointed by the President of India.

The Constitution does not specify the exact number of members of the Commission. The President determines the number of members and their service conditions through regulations.

Important features

  • The Commission consists of a Chairman and other members.
  • The President appoints the Chairman and members.
  • As nearly as may be, one-half of the members should be persons who have held office for at least ten years under the Government of India or a State Government.
  • The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed numerical strength for the Commission.

4. Appointment of Chairman and Members

Under Article 316, the President appoints the Chairman and other members of UPSC.

The Constitution does not prescribe a separate constitutional selection committee for these appointments. The appointments are made through the applicable governmental process.

5. Tenure and Conditions of Service

Under Article 316, a member of UPSC holds office for six years from the date of entering office or until attaining the age of sixty-five years, whichever is earlier.

A member may resign by submitting a written resignation to the President.

A member is not eligible for reappointment to the same office after completing the term.

Position Retirement age Maximum tenure
UPSC Chairman 65 years 6 years
UPSC Member 65 years 6 years
State PSC Chairman or Member 62 years 6 years

6. Removal and Suspension of Members

Article 317 provides the constitutional procedure for removal and suspension of the Chairman or any other member of a Public Service Commission.

6.1 Removal on grounds of misbehaviour

The President can remove the Chairman or another member of UPSC on the ground of misbehaviour after the Supreme Court, on a reference made by the President, has held an inquiry and reported that the person ought to be removed.

During the inquiry, the President may suspend the member from office.

6.2 Other grounds for removal

The President may also remove a member if the member:

  • Is adjudged an insolvent.
  • Engages during the term of office in any paid employment outside the duties of the office.
  • Is, in the opinion of the President, unfit to continue in office by reason of infirmity of mind or body.

6.3 Deemed misbehaviour

Article 317 provides that if a member is concerned or interested in any contract or agreement made by or on behalf of the Government of India or a State Government, or participates in its profit or benefit other than as an ordinary member of an incorporated company, the member shall be deemed guilty of misbehaviour for the purposes of Article 317.

7. Restrictions on Further Employment

Article 319 imposes restrictions on further employment after a person ceases to hold office in a Public Service Commission. These restrictions help protect the Commission's independence.

Person Permissible future appointment
Chairman of UPSC Not eligible for further employment under the Government of India or a State Government.
Member of UPSC Eligible to be appointed as Chairman of UPSC or Chairman of a State Public Service Commission, but not to other government employment.
Chairman of a State PSC Eligible to be appointed as Chairman or Member of UPSC or Chairman of another State PSC, but not to other government employment.
Member of a State PSC Eligible to be appointed as Chairman or Member of UPSC or Chairman of that or another State PSC, but not to other government employment.

8. Functions of UPSC

Article 320 defines the functions of Public Service Commissions. The UPSC performs several important recruitment and advisory responsibilities.

8.1 Conducting examinations

UPSC conducts examinations for appointments to services under the Union. Important examinations include:

  • Civil Services Examination.
  • Engineering Services Examination.
  • Combined Defence Services Examination.
  • National Defence Academy and Naval Academy Examination.
  • Central Armed Police Forces (Assistant Commandants) Examination.
  • Combined Medical Services Examination.
  • Indian Forest Service Examination.
  • Combined Geo-Scientist Examination.

8.2 Recruitment through selection

The Commission may conduct recruitment through interviews and other selection processes for posts under the Union Government.

8.3 Recruitment rules

UPSC is consulted on matters relating to methods of recruitment to civil services and civil posts.

8.4 Appointment, promotion and transfer

The Commission is consulted on principles to be followed in making appointments, promotions and transfers from one service to another, as prescribed by the Constitution and applicable rules.

8.5 Disciplinary matters

UPSC is consulted on disciplinary matters affecting persons serving under the Government of India, within the scope of Article 320 and applicable rules.

8.6 Claims for legal expenses and injury pensions

The Commission is consulted on claims by or in respect of persons serving or having served under the Government of India for costs incurred in defending legal proceedings relating to official duties, and on claims for awards of compensation pensions in respect of injuries sustained in the course of official duty.

8.7 Advisory role

The Commission advises the Government on matters referred to it under the constitutional framework.

9. Exceptions to Consultation

Article 320(3) provides for consultation with the Public Service Commission on specified matters. However, consultation is not required in every recruitment or service matter.

Article 320(4) provides that consultation with the Commission is not required in respect of the manner in which provisions under Article 16(4) may be made or the manner in which effect may be given to Article 335.

Article 320(3) also permits the President or Governor, as applicable, to make regulations specifying matters in which consultation is not necessary.

Important: The recommendations of UPSC are generally advisory. They are not automatically binding on the Government unless a specific legal provision provides otherwise.

10. Independence of UPSC

The Constitution provides several safeguards to protect the impartial functioning of the Commission.

  • Constitutional status: The Commission is established directly under the Constitution.
  • Appointment by President: The Chairman and members are appointed by the President.
  • Security of tenure: Members have a constitutionally prescribed term.
  • Special removal procedure: Removal on grounds of misbehaviour requires an inquiry by the Supreme Court following a presidential reference.
  • Restrictions on further employment: Article 319 limits post-tenure government employment.
  • Expenses charged on Consolidated Fund: Under Article 322, expenses of the Commission, including salaries and allowances, are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
  • Reporting: The Commission submits an annual report to the President under Article 323.

11. Reporting Mechanism

Under Article 323, UPSC submits an annual report of its work to the President.

The President causes the report to be laid before each House of Parliament, together with a memorandum explaining the cases, if any, where the advice of the Commission was not accepted and the reasons for such non-acceptance.

This reporting arrangement promotes transparency and legislative accountability.

12. Limitations and Challenges

12.1 Advisory nature

The Commission's advice is generally not binding on the Government. This can limit the direct effect of its recommendations.

12.2 Recruitment delays

Delays in recruitment processes and prolonged selection procedures can affect public administration.

12.3 Changing skill requirements

Modern administration requires technical, managerial and specialised skills. Recruitment systems must respond to changing public service requirements.

12.4 Transparency and fairness

Selection procedures must remain transparent, impartial and accessible to eligible candidates.

12.5 Institutional capacity

Increasing recruitment demands require adequate administrative capacity, technology and examination infrastructure.

13. UPSC and State Public Service Commissions

UPSC operates at the Union level, while State Public Service Commissions perform recruitment and advisory functions at the State level.

  • Article 317 deals with removal and suspension.
  • Article 319 deals with restrictions on further employment.

Which Article provides for the establishment of UPSC?

Answer: Article 315.

Question 2

Who appoints the Chairman and members of UPSC?

Answer: The President of India.

Question 3

What is the maximum tenure of a UPSC member?

Answer: Six years or until attaining the age of sixty-five years, whichever is earlier.

Question 4

Which Article deals with the removal of UPSC members?

Answer: Article 317.

Question 5

Which Article deals with the functions of UPSC?

Answer: Article 320.

Question 6

Are UPSC recommendations generally binding on the Government?

Answer: No. They are generally advisory in nature.

Answer: Article 323.
