Attorney General of India

The Attorney General of India is the highest law officer of the Union Government. The office is established under Article 76 of the Constitution. The Attorney General advises the Government of India on legal matters and represents it in important legal proceedings.

The office connects the executive branch with the legal system. Unlike constitutional bodies such as the Election Commission or the Comptroller and Auditor General, the Attorney General is not an independent constitutional authority. The office primarily serves the legal needs of the Union Government.

1. Constitutional Basis

Article 76, contained in Part V, Chapter I of the Constitution, provides for the office of the Attorney General for India.

Article Subject
Article 76 Appointment, qualifications and duties of the Attorney General for India.
Article 88 Rights of the Attorney General to participate in parliamentary proceedings.
Article 105 Parliamentary powers, privileges and immunities relevant to participation in Parliament.

2. Appointment

The President of India appoints the Attorney General under Article 76(1). The Constitution does not prescribe a separate selection committee or parliamentary approval process for this appointment.

The Attorney General holds office during the pleasure of the President. This means that the Constitution does not provide a fixed tenure. The President may end the appointment, and the Attorney General may also resign.

3. Qualifications

Under Article 76(1), the Attorney General must be qualified to be appointed as a judge of the Supreme Court.

The constitutional qualifications for a Supreme Court judge under Article 124(3) are:

  • The person must be a citizen of India.
  • The person must have been a judge of a High Court for at least five years; or
  • The person must have been an advocate of a High Court for at least ten years; or
  • The person must, in the opinion of the President, be a distinguished jurist.

These are alternative professional qualifications. The Constitution does not prescribe a separate minimum or maximum age for the Attorney General.

4. Tenure and Conditions of Office

  • The Attorney General holds office during the pleasure of the President.
  • No fixed constitutional term is prescribed.
  • The Constitution does not lay down a special removal procedure.
  • Remuneration is determined by the President.
  • The Attorney General may resign from office.

It is a convention for the Attorney General to resign when a new government assumes office. This is a political and administrative convention, not an express constitutional requirement.

5. Duties and Functions

Article 76(2) describes the principal duties of the Attorney General. The office performs legal functions on behalf of the Union Government.

5.1 Legal Advice to the Government

The Attorney General advises the Government of India on legal matters referred by the President. The advice may concern constitutional interpretation, legislation, government policy and legal disputes.

5.2 Representation of the Union

The Attorney General represents the Union Government in legal proceedings before the Supreme Court and other courts when required or assigned.

5.3 Performance of Assigned Legal Duties

The Attorney General performs legal duties assigned by the President. The office also discharges functions conferred by the Constitution or any other law in force.

5.4 Right of Audience

Under Article 76(3), the Attorney General has the right of audience in all courts in the territory of India. This enables the Attorney General to appear before courts throughout the country.

6. Rights in Parliament

Article 88 grants the Attorney General certain rights in parliamentary proceedings. These rights facilitate the provision of legal advice and clarification on matters being considered by Parliament.

  • The Attorney General may speak and otherwise take part in proceedings of either House of Parliament.
  • The Attorney General may participate in a joint sitting of both Houses.
  • The Attorney General may participate in a parliamentary committee of which the Attorney General is named as a member.
  • The Attorney General does not have the right to vote by virtue of Article 88.

The Attorney General does not automatically become a Member of Parliament. The right to participate in proceedings is different from membership of either House.

7. Relationship with Parliamentary Privileges

Article 105 deals with the powers, privileges and immunities of Parliament and its members. Article 88 specifically gives the Attorney General the right to participate in parliamentary proceedings without voting rights.

For examination purposes, distinguish the Attorney General's participation rights under Article 88 from the voting rights associated with membership of Parliament.

8. Restrictions and Professional Conduct

The Attorney General is not a full-time government servant in the ordinary civil-service sense. The office may undertake certain private legal work, subject to applicable government conditions and professional restrictions.

Important restrictions and conventions include:

  • The Attorney General should not advise or hold a brief against the Government of India.
  • The Attorney General should not represent an accused person in criminal proceedings without the required government permission.
  • The Attorney General should not accept matters in which the interests of the Union Government conflict with the proposed representation.
  • Professional conduct must comply with applicable rules and the conditions governing the office.

These restrictions should not be confused with a detailed list of prohibitions written directly into Article 76. Some arise from government conditions, professional rules and conventions.

9. Status and Independence

The Attorney General is the principal legal adviser to the Union Government, but is not an independent constitutional authority. The office is held during the pleasure of the President and is closely connected with the legal work of the government.

The Attorney General is not a member of the Union Cabinet merely by virtue of holding this office. The Attorney General is also not a civil servant in the ordinary sense.

The Attorney General's legal opinions are advisory in nature. They are not judicial decisions and do not bind courts in the manner of judgments.

10. Attorney General, Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General

The Attorney General is a constitutional office under Article 76. The Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General assist the Union Government in its legal work, but their offices are not created by the Constitution.

Position Constitutional Status Principal Role
Attorney General Constitutional office under Article 76 Highest law officer of the Union
Solicitor General Not a constitutional office Assists the Attorney General and represents the Union Government
Additional Solicitor General Not a constitutional office Assists in government legal representation and related work

11. Attorney General and Advocate General

The Attorney General is the highest law officer of the Union, whereas the Advocate General is the highest law officer of a State. Their constitutional provisions and jurisdictions are different.

Basis Attorney General Advocate General
Constitutional Article Article 76 Article 165
Appointing Authority President Governor
Government Served Union Government State Government
Qualification Qualified to be appointed as a Supreme Court judge Qualified to be appointed as a High Court judge
Legislative Participation Article 88 Article 177
Voting Right by Virtue of Office No No

13. Quick Revision

  • The Attorney General is the highest law officer of India.
  • Article 76 provides for the office.
  • The President appoints the Attorney General.
  • The person must be qualified to be appointed as a Supreme Court judge.
  • The Attorney General holds office during the pleasure of the President.
  • There is no fixed constitutional tenure.
  • Remuneration is determined by the President.
  • Article 76(3) grants the right of audience in all courts in India.
  • Article 88 permits participation in parliamentary proceedings but not voting.
  • The Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General are not constitutional offices.
  • The Attorney General is not automatically a member of the Cabinet or Parliament.
