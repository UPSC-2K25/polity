INDIAN CONSTITUTION · PART V AND PART VI

The Judiciary

The guardian of the Constitution and protector of the rule of law.

The judiciary interprets the Constitution and laws, resolves disputes, protects fundamental rights and ensures that public authorities act within their constitutional limits. It plays a central role in maintaining the rule of law and the balance of power among the organs of government.

India has an integrated judicial system, with the Supreme Court at the apex, High Courts at the State level and a subordinate judiciary below them. The Constitution provides for their structure, jurisdiction, powers and safeguards for judicial independence.

CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Where does the Constitution deal with the judiciary?

The Union Judiciary is dealt with in Part V, Chapter IV, principally Articles 124 to 147. The High Courts are dealt with in Part VI, Chapter V, Articles 214 to 231. Subordinate courts are covered by Part VI, Chapter VI, Articles 233 to 237.

Other provisions concerning the judiciary include Article 32 on constitutional remedies, Article 50 on separation of the judiciary from the executive, and provisions relating to the jurisdiction of courts and the administration of justice.

IMPORTANT CONCEPT

Integrated judiciary and judicial independence

India follows an integrated judicial system in which the Supreme Court stands at the apex, followed by High Courts and subordinate courts. This differs from systems where federal and State judicial systems operate separately.

Judicial independence means that courts must be able to decide disputes impartially, without improper influence from the legislature, executive or other interests. The Constitution provides safeguards to protect this independence while also recognising the need for judicial accountability.
